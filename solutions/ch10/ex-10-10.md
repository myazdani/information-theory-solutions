# Exercise 10.10 — Only the parity of the source matters

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 339.

## Solution

Let $`B=X\bmod2`$ and $`\widehat B=\widehat X\bmod2`$. Distortion is exactly $`\mathbf1_{B\ne\widehat B}`$, and $`B`$ is a fair bit. Data processing gives
```math
I(X;\widehat X)\ge I(B;\widehat B)\ge1-h_2(D)
```
for $`0\le D\le1/2`$. Conversely, pass $`B`$ through a BSC of crossover $`D`$ and reproduce one fixed alphabet representative of the resulting parity. This depends on $`X`$ only through $`B`$ and attains $`I(X;\widehat X)=1-h_2(D)`$. Therefore
```math
R(D)=\begin{cases}1-h_2(D),&0\le D\le1/2,\\0,&D\ge1/2.\end{cases}
```
All distinctions among the $`m`$ values of the same parity are free to discard. In particular $`R(0)=1`$ rather than $`\log_2(2m)`$. The construction verifies achievability as well as the lower bound.
