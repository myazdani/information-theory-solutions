# Exercise 10.6 — The discrete Shannon lower bound and equality

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 337–338.

## Solution

Let $`\phi(D)`$ be the maximum entropy among distributions on the common list of column distortions with mean cost at most $`D`$.

### (a)
If $`q_1,q_2`$ are feasible at $`D_1,D_2`$, their mixture $`q=\theta q_1+(1-\theta)q_2`$ is feasible at $`\theta D_1+(1-\theta)D_2`$. Entropy concavity gives $`H(q)\ge\theta H(q_1)+(1-\theta)H(q_2)`$. Taking maximizing distributions proves concavity of $`\phi`$. It is also nondecreasing because its feasible sets expand with the budget.

### (b)
The first two equalities are the definition of mutual information and conditional entropy. For each reconstruction letter, permute the conditional source masses to match its column's distortion order. Its entropy is unchanged and its mean distortion is $`D_{\widehat x}`$, so $`H(X\mid\widehat X=\widehat x)\le\phi(D_{\widehat x})`$. Averaging gives (10.154). Jensen's inequality for the concave $`\phi`$ gives (10.155). Finally $`\sum_{\widehat x}p(\widehat x)D_{\widehat x}\le D`$ and monotonicity give (10.156).

### (c)
The bound applies to every feasible test channel, so minimizing gives $`R(D)\ge H(X)-\phi(D)`$ (and always $`R(D)\ge0`$).

### (d)
Suppose the source is uniform on $`m`$ symbols and there are $`\ell`$ reproduction symbols. An entropy-maximizing cost-constrained distribution on a column is the Gibbs law $`q_i=e^{-s d_i}/Z`$, $`s\ge0`$, including its limiting zero-temperature law and $`s=0`$ when the constraint is inactive. This follows by nonnegativity of divergence to the Gibbs law. Define
```math
p(x\mid\widehat x)=e^{-s d(x,\widehat x)}/Z,\qquad
p(\widehat x)=1/\ell.
```
Column permutations make $`Z`$ common to every column. Row permutations make the row sums of $`e^{-s d(x,\widehat x)}`$ equal; their total is $`\ell Z`$, so every row sum is $`\ell Z/m`$. The induced source marginal is therefore $`1/m`$. All conditional entropies equal $`\phi(D)`$ at the chosen optimal mean cost, and all inequalities above are equalities. Consequently $`R(D)=\log_2m-\phi(D)`$. Endpoint budgets follow by limits; below the smallest possible cost the optimization is infeasible.
