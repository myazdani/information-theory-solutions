# Exercise 10.1 — Optimal scalar one-bit Gaussian quantization

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 336.

## Solution

For fixed reproduction values, minimum squared error assigns each source point to its nearer reproduction value, so an optimal two-level scalar quantizer uses a threshold. For a threshold $`t=\sigma z`$, optimal reproduction values are the two conditional means,
```math
m_-=-\sigma\frac{\phi(z)}{\Phi(z)},\qquad
m_+=\sigma\frac{\phi(z)}{1-\Phi(z)},
```
where $`\phi,\Phi`$ are the standard normal density and distribution. The conditional-variance identity gives its minimized distortion
```math
D(z)=\sigma^2\left[1-\frac{\phi(z)^2}{\Phi(z)(1-\Phi(z))}\right].
```
To prove the optimum is globally at zero, rather than infer it only from symmetry, write
```math
\Phi(z)\Phi(-z)=\frac{e^{-z^2}}{2\pi}
\int_0^\infty\!\int_0^\infty e^{-(s^2+t^2)/2}\cosh(z(s-t))\,ds\,dt
\ge\frac14e^{-z^2}.
```
The equality follows by multiplying the two Gaussian integrals and averaging after interchanging $`s,t`$; the inequality is $`\cosh\ge1`$. Thus the fraction in the distortion expression is at most $`2/\pi`$, with equality only at $`z=0`$. The optimal points and distortion are
```math
m_\pm=\pm\sigma\sqrt{2/\pi},\qquad D_{\rm scalar}=(1-2/\pi)\sigma^2.
```
At one bit, the block rate-distortion limit is $`D_{\rm block}=\sigma^2/4`$. Scalar quantization has distortion approximately $`0.3633802276\sigma^2`$, about $`1.4535209105`$ times this limit. Long-block descriptions can improve on the one-symbol restriction.
