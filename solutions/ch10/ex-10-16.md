# Exercise 10.16 — Conditional type counting and typicality probabilities

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 341–344.

## Solution

Use finite alphabets of sizes $`m=|\mathcal X|`$ and $`\ell=|\mathcal Y|`$. Polynomial factors are retained below. Entropy tolerances tend to zero by uniform continuity on a finite simplex. Two qualifications matter: entropies of conditional type classes use the empirical input type, and the same numerical tolerance for marginal and joint strong typicality does not always guarantee a nonempty joint section. Part (f) makes the latter issue explicit.

### (a)
For a type $`Q`$ on $`m`$ letters,
```math
(n+1)^{-m}2^{nH(Q)}\le|T_Q|\le2^{nH(Q)},
```
and there are at most $`(n+1)^m`$ types. Every strongly typical type has entropy within $`\omega(\epsilon)`$ of $`H(X)`$. Summing the upper bounds yields $`|A_\epsilon^{*(n)}|\le(n+1)^m2^{n(H(X)+\omega(\epsilon))}`$. For sufficiently large $`n`$, round $`np_X`$ to counts, respecting zero-probability letters, to obtain one strongly typical type; its class supplies the corresponding lower bound. Therefore the exponential rate of the typical-set size approaches $`H(X)`$ as the tolerance shrinks and $`n`$ grows, which is the meaning of the dotted equality.

### (b)
A conditional type is specified by the $`m\ell`$ integers $`N(a,b)`$, each between 0 and $`n`$. The row-sum constraints only reduce their number, so there are at most $`(n+1)^{m\ell}`$ conditional types. Rows for which $`N(a)=0`$ are irrelevant; fix one canonical row there rather than count arbitrary stochastic rows.

### (c)
Let $`Q(a)=N(a)/n`$ and let $`V`$ be a realizable conditional type. Then
```math
|T_V(x^n)|=\prod_a\frac{N(a)!}{\prod_bN(a,b)!}.
```
Apply the ordinary type-class bound in each row. Since $`\sum_aN(a)H(V(\cdot\mid a))=nH_{QV}(Y\mid X)`$ and every $`N(a)+1\le n+1`$, this gives exactly
```math
(n+1)^{-m\ell}2^{nH_{QV}(Y\mid X)}
\le|T_V(x^n)|\le2^{nH_{QV}(Y\mid X)}.
```
The conditional entropy here belongs to the empirical joint law $`QV`$, not to an unrelated fixed source law.

### (d)
For the given sequence type $`Q`$ and reference channel $`V`$, every conditionally typical sequence has joint type $`QV'`$ satisfying
```math
|Q(a)V'(b\mid a)-Q(a)V(b\mid a)|\le\epsilon/(\ell+1),
```
with the required zeros. Thus $`H_{QV'}(Y\mid X)`$ differs from $`H_{QV}(Y\mid X)`$ by at most some uniform $`\epsilon_1\to0`$. The upper bound follows by summing part (c) over at most $`(n+1)^{m\ell}`$ types. For the lower bound, round the expected row counts $`N(a)V(b\mid a)`$ to nonnegative integers summing to $`N(a)`$, with each error less than 1 and respecting zeros. For sufficiently large $`n`$ this is conditionally typical; its one type class supplies the lower bound. Hence
```math
(n+1)^{-m\ell}2^{n(H_{QV}(Y\mid X)-\epsilon_1)}
\le|A_\epsilon^{*(n)}(Y\mid x^n)|
\le(n+1)^{m\ell}2^{n(H_{QV}(Y\mid X)+\epsilon_1)}.
```
If $`x^n`$ is also source-typical, continuity permits replacing the empirical conditional entropy by the fixed source's $`H(Y\mid X)`$. Without this qualification the printed formula cannot use a fixed source entropy for arbitrary $`x^n`$.

### (e)
Assume, as stated, that the joint section is nonempty. Every joint type $`Q_{XY}`$ in it is close to $`p_{XY}`$, and so is its marginal. Therefore
```math
|H_Q(Y\mid X)-H_p(Y\mid X)|\le\delta(\epsilon),\qquad\delta(\epsilon)\to0.
```
The section is a union of conditional type classes. One existing class gives the lower bound in (10.175), and their at most $`(n+1)^{m\ell}`$ upper bounds give the upper bound. Absorb $`m\ell\log_2(n+1)/n`$ into the entropy tolerance to obtain (10.176).

### (f)
For a nonempty joint section, every member $`y^n`$ has marginal type close to $`p_Y`$ and the correct zero pattern. Consequently its iid probability lies between $`2^{-n(H(Y)+\eta(\epsilon))}`$ and $`2^{-n(H(Y)-\eta(\epsilon))}`$. Multiply these bounds by the section-size bounds in (e), and absorb the polynomial factors to obtain
```math
2^{-n(I(X;Y)+\epsilon_3)}\le
\Pr((x^n,Y^n)\in A_\epsilon^{*(n)})
\le2^{-n(I(X;Y)-\epsilon_3)},
```
with $`\epsilon_3\to0`$ in the stated limits.

The literal lower bound for every marginally $`\epsilon`$-typical input using the very same $`\epsilon`$ for joint typicality is false under the printed normalizations. For example take fair $`X`$ and $`Y=X`$, both binary, $`\epsilon=0.1`$, and an input type with 54% ones. Its marginal deviations $`0.04`$ are less than $`\epsilon/2`$, but every feasible joint type has diagonal deviations $`0.04>\epsilon/4`$. The joint section is empty, so its probability is zero. This persists along arbitrarily large block lengths.

A standard valid formulation either retains (e)'s nonempty-section hypothesis, or assumes a tighter marginal tolerance, for example $`\epsilon/(2\ell)`$. Under the latter choice, rounding $`N(a)p(b\mid a)`$ has joint per-entry deviation at most $`\epsilon/(2m\ell)+1/n<\epsilon/(m\ell)`$ for all sufficiently large $`n`$, ensuring nonemptiness. The above proof then yields the desired uniform conditional-typicality exponent. This states explicitly the tolerance adjustment needed for the intended theorem.
