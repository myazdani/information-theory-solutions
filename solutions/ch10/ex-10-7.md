# Exercise 10.7 — Binary erasure reconstruction

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 338.

## Solution

Call the three reconstruction symbols 0, $`e`$, and 1. Finite distortion rules out an incorrect nonerasure. Thus distortion equals the erasure probability $`q`$. Conditional on a nonerased output the source is known, while conditional on erasure its entropy is at most 1. Hence
```math
H(X\mid\widehat X)\le q,\qquad I(X;\widehat X)\ge1-q\ge1-D.
```
Independently erase the source with probability $`D`$ and otherwise reproduce it exactly. For $`0\le D\le1`$, this yields conditional source entropy $`D`$ and attains the bound. Therefore $`R(D)=(1-D)_+`$ for nonnegative budgets.

A simple block implementation agrees in advance on a fraction $`D`$ of coordinates to erase, transmits the other fair source bits without compression, and reproduces erasures in the omitted coordinates. It uses asymptotic rate $`1-D`$ with distortion $`D`$ and never incurs an infinite-distortion wrong bit. Rational fractions are exact for suitable block lengths; arbitrary fractions follow by approximation.
