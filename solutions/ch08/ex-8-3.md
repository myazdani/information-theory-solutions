# Exercise 8.3 — Uniform noise with a peak constraint

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 257.

## Solution

Take $`a>0`$. Answers below are in bits.

### (a)
The convolution of uniforms of widths 1 and $`a`$ is trapezoidal. Let $`M=\max(1,a)`$, $`m=\min(1,a)`$. Its plateau has height $`1/M`$ and width $`M-m`$; each sloping edge has width $`m`$ and rises linearly from zero to $`1/M`$. Integrating $`-f\ln f`$ gives
```math
h(Y)=\ln M+\frac{m}{2M},\qquad h(Z)=\ln a.
```
Therefore
```math
I(X;Y)=
\begin{cases}
\log_2(1/a)+a/(2\ln2),&0<a\le1,\\
1/(2a\ln2),&a\ge1.
\end{cases}
```
### (b)
At $`a=1`$, $`Y`$ lies in an interval of length 2, so $`I=h(Y)-h(Z)\le1`$. Assign probability $`1/2`$ to each endpoint $`X=\pm1/2`$. The two output intervals have disjoint interiors and together give a uniform density on the length-2 interval. Thus $`C=1`$ bit.

### (c), including the optional general case
Translate and rescale to noise uniform on $`[0,1]`$ and input supported on $`[0,L]`$, where $`L=1/a=n+r`$, $`n=\lfloor L\rfloor`$, $`0\le r<1`$. Let $`T=Y-\lfloor Y\rfloor`$. Conditional on every input, $`T`$ is uniform on $`[0,1)`$. The integer part $`K=\lfloor Y\rfloor`$ has at most $`n+2`$ possible values for $`T<r`$, and at most $`n+1`$ otherwise. Decomposing differential entropy by these unit intervals gives
```math
h(Y)=h(T)+H(K\mid T)
\le r\log_2(n+2)+(1-r)\log_2(n+1).
```
This bound is achievable. For $`0<r<1`$, use input atoms, for $`j=0,\ldots,n`$,
```math
\Pr(X=j)=\frac{n+1-j}{(n+1)(n+2)},\qquad
\Pr(X=j+r)=\frac{j+1}{(n+1)(n+2)}.
```
Their total mass is one. Summing the masses of atoms in $`[y-1,y]`$ shows the output density is $`1/(n+2)`$ when its fractional part is less than $`r`$ and $`1/(n+1)`$ otherwise, throughout the output support. Thus each conditional $`K\mid T`$ is uniform and equality holds. If $`r=0`$, take equal mass on $`0,1,\ldots,n`$ instead. Undo the affine transformation for the original input interval. The capacity is
```math
C(a)=(1-r)\log_2(n+1)+r\log_2(n+2),\quad 1/a=n+r.
```
For zero noise the unrestricted real input has infinite capacity, consistent with the limit as $`a\downarrow0`$.
