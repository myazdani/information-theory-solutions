# Exercise 8.6 — A variational formula for a logarithmic moment

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 257.

## Solution

Write $`M=\mathbb E_PX`$ and initially assume $`0<M<\infty`$. Define the tilted distribution $`P^*(x)=xP(x)/M`$. For any $`Q\ll P`$ for which the expression is defined,
```math
D(Q\|P^*)=D(Q\|P)-\mathbb E_Q\log X+\log M.
```
Thus
```math
\mathbb E_Q\log X-D(Q\|P)=\log M-D(Q\|P^*)\le\log M.
```
Equality holds at $`Q=P^*`$ when the component expectations are finite. In more general cases the same supremum is approached by tilting on the sets $`\{1/k\le X\le k\}`$ (and finite support subsets if necessary); the normalizing constants increase to $`M`$. If $`M=\infty`$, those constants diverge and the supremum is infinite. Measures not absolutely continuous with respect to $`P`$ have infinite divergence and cannot improve the supremum. This proof also avoids an unjustified interchange of an infinite sum and a Lagrange derivative.
