# Exercise 8.7 — Discrete entropy bounded through jittering

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 258.

## Solution

The variance factor must be inside the logarithm. If the printed parentheses are read as multiplication outside, that reading is false: for $`p_1=p_2=1/2`$, the entropy is 1 bit but that reading gives $`(1/6)\log_2(2\pi e)\approx0.6824<1`$. The corrected, unambiguous bound proved below puts the whole variance factor inside the logarithm.

Let $`J`$ be integer-valued with $`\Pr(J=i)=p_i`$, and independently let $`U`$ be uniform on $`(0,1]`$. The density of $`Y=J+U`$ equals $`p_i`$ on $`(i,i+1]`$, so
```math
h_2(Y)=-\sum_i\int_i^{i+1}p_i\log_2p_i\,dy=H(p).
```
Independence gives $`\mathrm{Var}(Y)=\mathrm{Var}(J)+1/12`$. Applying the Gaussian maximum-entropy bound,
```math
H(p)\le\frac12\log_2\left[2\pi e\left(\sum_i p_i i^2-\Bigl(\sum_i ip_i\Bigr)^2+\frac1{12}\right)\right].
```
If the variance is infinite the bound is vacuous, interpreted as positive infinity (rather than subtracting two undefined infinities). For any permutation $`\sigma`$, repeat the construction with $`\Pr(J=i)=p_{\sigma(i)}`$. Entropy is unchanged by reordering the masses, while the variance becomes the permuted expression, proving the second bound. The actual numerical locations $`a_i`$ play no role; only the probability masses and the chosen indexing matter.
