# Exercise 8.9 — Gaussian correlations through a Markov chain

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 258.

## Solution

Assume initially nonsingular positive variances. Gaussian regression gives
```math
\mathbb E[X\mid Y]=\mu_X+\rho_1(\sigma_X/\sigma_Y)(Y-\mu_Y),
\quad
\mathbb E[Z\mid Y]=\mu_Z+\rho_2(\sigma_Z/\sigma_Y)(Y-\mu_Y).
```
The Markov property says $`X`$ and $`Z`$ are conditionally independent given $`Y`$. Applying the law of total covariance therefore yields
```math
\operatorname{Cov}(X,Z)=\rho_1\rho_2\sigma_X\sigma_Z,
```
so their correlation is $`\rho_1\rho_2`$. The bivariate Gaussian entropy formula now gives
```math
I(X;Z)=-\frac12\log_2(1-\rho_1^2\rho_2^2).
```
This is zero if either correlation vanishes. It diverges when the product has absolute value one and the endpoints have nonzero variances. A deterministic endpoint instead gives mutual information zero, and its correlation coefficient is undefined.
