# Exercise 8.11 — A nonergodic Gaussian process with an AEP

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 258–259.

## Solution

Assume $`S,N>0`$; $`S=0`$ reduces to iid Gaussian noise.

### (a)
The process is stationary: it has constant mean zero and covariance $`\operatorname{Cov}(X_i,X_j)=S+N\mathbf1_{i=j}`$, invariant under all time shifts. Joint Gaussianity makes this sufficient for strict stationarity.

### (b)
By the strong law, $`n^{-1}\sum_iX_i=V+n^{-1}\sum_iZ_i\to V`$ almost surely. The limit is random, with variance $`S`$, so the process is not ergodic.

### (c)
The covariance matrix is $`K_n=NI_n+S\mathbf1\mathbf1^T`$, with determinant $`N^{n-1}(N+nS)`$. Therefore
```math
\frac1n h_2(X^n)=\frac12\log_2(2\pi eN)
+\frac1{2n}\log_2(1+nS/N)
\longrightarrow h=\frac12\log_2(2\pi eN).
```
### (d)
Completing the square in the Gaussian posterior yields
```math
\mathbb E[V\mid X^n]=\frac{S}{N+nS}\sum_{i=1}^nX_i,
\qquad \operatorname{Var}(V\mid X^n)=\frac{SN}{N+nS}.
```
Thus the least-squares predictor of $`X_{n+1}`$ is this posterior mean. Its error variance is $`N+SN/(N+nS)`$, converging to $`\sigma_\infty^2=N`$.

### (e)
Yes, an AEP holds, even almost surely. Let $`\bar X_n`$ be the sample average. The Gaussian quadratic form decomposes as
```math
(X^n)^TK_n^{-1}X^n
=\frac1N\sum_i(X_i-\bar X_n)^2+
\frac{n\bar X_n^2}{N+nS}.
```
Dividing by $`n`$, the first term tends to 1 by the strong law for $`Z_i,Z_i^2`$; the second tends to zero because $`\bar X_n\to V`$. Combine this with $`n^{-1}\ln\det K_n\to\ln N`$ in the Gaussian log density to obtain
```math
-\frac1n\log_2 f(X^n)\longrightarrow\frac12\log_2(2\pi eN)=h
\quad\text{almost surely}.
```
Nonergodicity alone therefore does not rule out a constant entropy-rate AEP.
