# Exercise 16.8: Normalizing away a common heavy-tailed market scale

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 654.

## Solution

Let $`u=(1/m,\ldots,1/m)`$ and suppose $`u^TX>0`$ almost surely. For every long-only portfolio, $`b^TX/(u^TX)\le m`$, so the positive part of the normalized log is bounded. Its negative part can be infinite for some boundary portfolios, but the uniform portfolio has normalized growth exactly zero.

### (a)
Whenever $`E|\ln(u^TX)|<\infty`$ and raw expected logs are well-defined,

```math
E\ln\frac{b^TX}{u^TX}=E\ln(b^TX)-E\ln(u^TX).
```

The second term is independent of $`b`$, so the two objectives have exactly the same maximizers. The finite-integrability qualification is essential. As the example below shows, if all raw growth rates are $`+\infty`$, raw maximization does not imply normalized optimality. Thus the statement in (a), read without a finiteness qualification, is false; normalization is what supplies a meaningful ordering in that case.

### (b)
The printed price vectors are $`(2^{2^k+1},2^{2^k})`$ and its reversal, each with probability $`2^{-(k+1)}`$, for $`k\ge1`$. Put $`A_k=2^{2^k}`$ and $`b=(t,1-t)`$. The common factor $`A_k`$ cancels in relative wealth, leaving

```math
G(t)=\frac12\ln\frac{1+t}{3/2}
     +\frac12\ln\frac{2-t}{3/2}
=\frac12\ln\frac{(1+t)(2-t)}{9/4}.
```

This is strictly concave and uniquely maximized at $`t=1/2`$. Hence $`b_*=(1/2,1/2)`$ and optimal normalized growth is 0.

### (c)
The total probability of scale $`k`$ is $`2^{-k}`$. Each coordinate has conditional mean $`3A_k/2`$, so

```math
EX_1=EX_2=\frac32\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k}2^{2^k}=+\infty.
```

For the uniform portfolio,

```math
W_* =E\ln(u^TX)
=\ln(3/2)+\ln2\sum_{k=1}^\infty2^{-k}2^k=+\infty.
```

Every other fixed long-only portfolio also has raw growth $`+\infty`$, since its scale-free multiplier lies between 1 and 2. This explicitly demonstrates the issue in (a).

### (d)
For a general normalized optimum with positive wealth, let $`R=b^TX/(b_*^TX)`$. The feasible mixture $`(1-s)b_*+sb`$ cannot improve normalized growth, so
$`E\ln(1+s(R-1))\le0`$. Divide by $`s`$ and use Fatou's lemma as $`s\downarrow0`$; the integrands are bounded below by -2 for $`s\le1/2`$. This yields $`ER\le1`$. Markov's inequality now gives, for $`c>0`$,

```math
P\{b^TX>c\,b_*^TX\}=P\{R>c\}\le\frac{ER}{c}\le\frac1c.
```

In the concrete symmetric example, $`ER=1`$ for every portfolio, so this conclusion can also be checked directly from its two possible scale-free ratios.
