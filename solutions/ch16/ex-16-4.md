# Exercise 16.4: Adding a fixed fund does not enlarge the opportunity set

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 653.

## Solution

Let $`b`$ be the proposed expert portfolio in the original $`m`$-asset simplex. An augmented allocation $`c=(c_1,\ldots,c_m,c_{m+1})`$ has return

```math
\sum_{i=1}^mc_iX_i+c_{m+1}\sum_{i=1}^mb_iX_i
=\sum_{i=1}^m(c_i+c_{m+1}b_i)X_i.
```

The effective original-asset weights $`d_i=c_i+c_{m+1}b_i`$ are nonnegative and sum to 1. Thus every augmented return is exactly the return of an original admissible portfolio. Conversely, every original portfolio is represented in the augmented market by assigning zero weight to the fund. The sets of attainable wealth multipliers are identical pointwise, so their expected-log suprema are identical:

```math
\widetilde W_*=W_*.
```

This conclusion needs no approximation or distributional symmetry. It concerns a fixed portfolio fund; a strategy using additional predictive information would be a different market opportunity.
