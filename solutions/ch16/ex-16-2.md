# Exercise 16.2: The value of knowing the next market outcome

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 652.

## Solution

The side information reveals which of the two market outcomes will occur. On an up day invest entirely in the stock, obtaining multiplier $`a`$; on a down day invest entirely in cash, obtaining multiplier 1. This is pointwise optimal among nonnegative portfolios. The resulting growth rate is

```math
W_{**}=\frac12\log_2a.
```

Without that information the optimal growth from Exercise 16.1 is $`W_* =\log_2((a+1)/(2\sqrt a))`$. Thus

```math
\Delta W=W_{**}-W_*=
\log_2\frac{2a}{a+1}.
```

The side-information bit is a deterministic bijective label of the two equally likely market states. Therefore $`I(X;Y)=H(Y)=1`$ bit. For $`a>1`$, $`1<2a/(a+1)<2`$, so
$`0<\Delta W<1=I(X;Y)`$. The upper bound is approached as $`a\to\infty`$.
