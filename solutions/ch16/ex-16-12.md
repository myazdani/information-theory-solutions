# Exercise 16.12: The numeraire criterion for a convex family

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 655.

## Solution

Interpret the family as nonnegative wealth random variables, and consider a candidate $`S_*\in\mathcal S`$ with $`S_*>0`$ almost surely, so all ratios are defined. We prove that the two stated properties are equivalent for each such candidate. This also proves the equivalence of their existence statements.

First suppose $`E(S/S_*)\le1`$ for every $`S\in\mathcal S`$. Jensen's inequality gives

```math
E\ln\frac S{S_*}\le\ln E\frac S{S_*}\le0.
```

The logarithmic expectation can be $`-\infty`$, which causes no difficulty; its positive part is integrable because $`\ln^+r\le r`$.

Conversely, suppose $`E\ln(S/S_*)\le0`$ for every $`S\in\mathcal S`$. Fix $`S`$ and put $`R=S/S_*`$. Convexity makes $`S_t=(1-t)S_*+tS`$ feasible. Hence, for $`0<t\le1/2`$,

```math
E\left[\frac{\ln(1+t(R-1))}{t}\right]\le0.
```

The integrand converges pointwise to $`R-1`$ as $`t\downarrow0`$ and is bounded below by $`\ln(1-t)/t\ge-2`$. Applying Fatou's lemma after adding 2 yields

```math
E(R-1)\le\liminf_{t\downarrow0}
E\left[\frac{\ln(1+t(R-1))}{t}\right]\le0.
```

Thus $`ER\le1`$, which is the required ratio condition. This argument justifies the derivative-at-zero step even when the ratio initially might have infinite expectation.

Closedness is not needed for this equivalence. Nor do closedness and convexity alone guarantee an optimizer: the family of deterministic constants $`\mathcal S=\{S:S\equiv c,\ c\ge1\}`$ is closed and convex, but every member is beaten by a larger constant and neither property holds for any candidate. Additional compactness or boundedness assumptions are needed if unconditional existence, rather than the stated equivalence, is intended.
