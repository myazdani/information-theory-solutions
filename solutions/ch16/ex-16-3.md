# Exercise 16.3: When one stock dominates the growth optimum

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 652.

## Solution

### (a)
In the usual well-defined finite-log-growth setting, the all-second-stock portfolio is optimal exactly when

```math
X_2>0\ \text{almost surely},\qquad E\left[\frac{2}{X_2}\right]\le1,
```

or equivalently $`E(1/X_2)\le1/2`$. Let $`t`$ be the fraction moved from stock 2 into the deterministic stock. Its relative wealth multiplier is
$`1+t(R-1)`$, where $`R=2/X_2`$.
If $`ER\le1`$, Jensen gives

```math
E\ln[1+t(R-1)]\le\ln[1+t(ER-1)]\le0
```

for every $`0\le t\le1`$, proving sufficiency.

Conversely, if the all-second-stock portfolio is optimal, the left side is nonpositive for every sufficiently small $`t>0`$. Divide by $`t`$ and let $`t\downarrow0`$. The integrand tends to $`R-1`$ and is bounded below by $`\ln(1-t)/t\ge-2`$ for $`t\le1/2`$. Fatou's lemma therefore implies $`E(R-1)\le0`$, proving necessity, including exclusion of an infinite reciprocal moment. An atom at $`X_2=0`$ makes this candidate suffer ruin and cannot be optimal against the safe asset.

The optimal portfolio is unique unless $`X_2=2`$ almost surely; in that case every portfolio has the same return. If unnormalized growth is allowed to be $`+\infty`$, raw maximizers can be nonunique for pathological reasons and the stated characterization must instead be read using normalized relative growth, as in Exercise 16.8.

### (b)
Investing everything in stock 1 gives wealth factor 2 every day, hence growth $`\log_2 2=1`$. Since this is an admissible portfolio, $`W_*\ge1`$ bit per trading period for every law of stock 2.
