# Exercise 16.7: The log-optimal leveraged portfolio

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 653.

## Solution

Use base-two logarithms. Write $`t=b_2`$, so $`b_1=1-t`$.

### (a)
For $`0<p<1`$, both market states have positive probability, and a finite log growth requires positive wealth in both: $`-1<t<2`$. The growth is

```math
W(t)=p\log_2(1+t)+(1-p)\log_2(1-t/2).
```

It is strictly concave. Setting its derivative to zero gives
$`p/(1+t)=(1-p)/(2-t)`$, or

```math
t_*=3p-1,\qquad b_*=(2-3p,\,3p-1).
```

This lies in $`(-1,2)`$ for every interior $`p`$. The stock is shorted for $`p<1/3`$; cash is shorted (the stock is leveraged above 100%) for $`p>2/3`$.

### (b)
At the optimum the two multipliers are $`3p`$ and $`3(1-p)/2`$. Consequently

```math
W_*(p)=p\log_2(3p)+(1-p)\log_2\frac{3(1-p)}2
=\log_2 3-(1-p)-h_2(p)
=D\bigl(\mathrm{Bern}(p)\Vert\mathrm{Bern}(1/3)\bigr).
```

It vanishes at $`p=1/3`$, where cash alone is optimal, and is otherwise positive.

At $`p=0`$ or 1 the literal unrestricted affine portfolio set permits unbounded leverage against the outcome of zero probability, giving supremum $`+\infty`$ and no finite maximizer. If one additionally requires nonnegative wealth in both listed states even when one has probability zero, the feasible closure is $`[-1,2]`$, and the continuous endpoint formulas give $`t_*=-1,W_* =\log_2(3/2)`$ at $`p=0`$, and $`t_*=2,W_* =\log_2 3`$ at $`p=1`$. These are distinct admissibility conventions; the interior result is unaffected.
