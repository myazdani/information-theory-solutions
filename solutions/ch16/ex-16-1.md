# Exercise 16.1: Optimal rebalancing between cash and a volatile stock

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 652.

## Solution

Write the cash allocation as $`b\in[0,1]`$, so the portfolio is $`(b,1-b)`$. All logarithms in this solution are base two.

### (a)
The two possible wealth multipliers have product

```math
[b+(1-b)a][b+(1-b)/a]
=1+\frac{(a-1)^2}{a}b(1-b).
```

The expected log growth is one half the logarithm of this product. Since $`a>1`$, it is uniquely maximized at $`b=1/2`$. Thus the optimal portfolio is $`b_*=(1/2,1/2)`$.

### (b)
Substitution gives

```math
W_* =\frac12\log_2\frac{(a+1)^2}{4a}
=\log_2\frac{a+1}{2\sqrt a}>0.
```

Although either asset by itself has zero expected log growth, repeatedly rebalancing between them has positive growth.

### (c)
Under the iid daily-market assumption, the strong law applied to the bounded log multipliers gives, for every fixed portfolio,

```math
\frac1n\log_2S_n(b)\longrightarrow
W(b)=\frac12\log_2\left[1+\frac{(a-1)^2}{a}b(1-b)\right]
\quad\text{almost surely}.
```

Equivalently, $`S_n(b)=2^{n(W(b)+o(1))}`$. All strictly mixed portfolios grow exponentially almost surely, with maximum exponent at $`b=1/2`$. The all-cash portfolio $`b=1`$ has $`S_n=1`$ exactly. For the all-stock portfolio $`b=0`$, $`S_n=a^{2N_n-n}`$, where $`N_n`$ is the number of up days; its exponential rate is zero, but the symmetric random walk is unbounded in both directions, so $`\liminf S_n=0`$ and $`\limsup S_n=\infty`$ almost surely. The zero exponent does not imply convergence of wealth.
