# Exercise 15.11: Every step of the degraded broadcast converse

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), pp. 599–600, Exercise 15.11, including the final unlabeled time-sharing request. The channel is physically degraded and memoryless, the messages are independent, and the encoder has no feedback.

## Solution

Fano's inequality introduces remainders $`n\epsilon_{j,n}`$, with $`\epsilon_{j,n}\to0`$, in the displayed bounds for $`nR_j`$. They should be retained until the limit. Set $`U_i=(W_2,Y_1^{i-1})`$.

### The labeled steps

- **(a)** Apply the mutual-information chain rule to $`I(W_2;Y_2^n)`$.
- **(b)** Use $`I(A;B\mid C)=H(B\mid C)-H(B\mid A,C)`$ in each summand.
- **(c)** Removing $`Y_2^{i-1}`$ from the first entropy increases it. Adding $`Y_1^{i-1}`$ to the conditioning in the subtracted entropy decreases that entropy, hence increases the difference.
- **(d)** Conditional on $`Y_1^{i-1}`$, the past outputs $`Y_2^{i-1}`$ are produced by independent degraded-channel randomization. They give no additional information about $`Y_{2i}`$, even with $`W_2`$ known. More explicitly, the no-feedback product law factors as $`p(w_1,w_2)\prod_t p(y_{1t}\mid x_t(w_1,w_2))p(y_{2t}\mid y_{1t})`$, which gives $`Y_2^{i-1}\perp (W_2,Y_{2i})\mid Y_1^{i-1}`$ and the required entropy equality.
- **(e)** Substitute the definition of $`U_i`$ in $`H(Y_{2i})-H(Y_{2i}\mid U_i)`$.
- **(f)** Appending $`W_2`$ to an observation cannot decrease its mutual information with $`W_1`$, because the added conditional mutual information is nonnegative.
- **(g)** The chain rule gives $`I(W_1;Y_1^n,W_2)=I(W_1;W_2)+I(W_1;Y_1^n\mid W_2)`$. Independence makes the first term zero, so this printed inequality is actually equality.
- **(h)** Apply the conditional mutual-information chain rule. The summation is over $`i=1,\ldots,n`$; the printed lower limit resembling $`i-1`$ is a typographical error.
- **(i)** Given $`U_i`$, the Markov chain $`W_1\to X_i\to Y_{1i}`$ holds by memorylessness. Conditional data processing gives the inequality. For the deterministic encoder in the setup it is in fact equality, since $`X_i`$ is a function of $`(W_1,W_2)`$ and $`H(Y_{1i}\mid W_1,U_i)=H(Y_{1i}\mid X_i)`$.

Thus, up to the Fano remainders,

```math
nR_1\le\sum_i I(X_i;Y_{1i}\mid U_i),\qquad
nR_2\le\sum_i I(U_i;Y_{2i}).
```

### Time-sharing and convex closure

Take $`Q`$ uniform on $`\{1,\ldots,n\}`$ and independent of the code experiment. With $`(X,Y_1,Y_2,U_0)=(X_Q,Y_{1Q},Y_{2Q},U_Q)`$, the two averages are exactly

```math
I(X;Y_1\mid U_0,Q),\qquad I(U_0;Y_2\mid Q).
```

Memorylessness gives the factorization $`p(q)p(u_0\mid q)p(x\mid u_0,q)p(y_1,y_2\mid x)`$. Set $`U=(U_0,Q)`$. Then

```math
I(X;Y_1\mid U)=I(X;Y_1\mid U_0,Q),
```

while

```math
I(U;Y_2)=I(U_0;Y_2\mid Q)+I(Q;Y_2)
\ge I(U_0;Y_2\mid Q).
```

Consequently every time-shared point is contained in an ordinary auxiliary-variable region. Conversely, a constant $`Q`$ includes every ordinary region. More explicitly, averaging the two rectangular rate bounds at each $`q`$ is precisely taking convex combinations of their points; any coordinate below an average upper bound can be allocated among these rectangles. Hence the union over auxiliary laws is already convex after permitting $`U`$ to include the time-sharing index. Taking closure gives exactly its convex closure, not a larger outer bound. Fano remainders vanish on reliable sequences, producing the asserted converse region.
