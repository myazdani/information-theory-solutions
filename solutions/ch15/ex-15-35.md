# Exercise 15.35: Capacity with per-codeword symbol costs

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 608, Exercise 15.35. Assume finite input and output alphabets, finite symbol costs $`c_j(x_j)`$, independent messages, and no feedback. The question's cost function $`r(x)`$ is denoted by $`c(x)`$ here to distinguish it from the budget. Rates refer to average-error capacity.

## Solution

### (a) A single cost-constrained channel

For a feasible budget $`r\ge\min_x c(x)`$,

```math
C(r)=\max_{p(x):\ \mathbb E c(X)\le r} I(X;Y).
```

The maximum exists by compactness and continuity. If the budget is below the minimum symbol cost there is no admissible codeword, so the constraint is infeasible, even for a singleton message.

For the converse, the usual Fano argument gives $`R-\epsilon_n\le n^{-1}\sum_i I(X_i;Y_i)`$. Concavity of mutual information in the input law bounds this by the mutual information under the averaged input distribution, whose cost is at most $`r`$. For achievability use an input law with cost strictly below budget and a standard random code. The probability that a generated codeword exceeds its budget tends to zero; replacing such words by a minimum-cost word and counting their indices as errors keeps the average error vanishing and satisfies every codeword constraint. Distributions on the budget boundary follow by mixing slightly with a minimum-cost symbol and taking limits. If $`r=\min c`$, restrict coding to the minimum-cost alphabet, where every word automatically obeys the constraint.

### (b) Multiple-access expression

The capacity region is the closure of the union of rate pairs satisfying

```math
R_1,R_2\ge0,\quad
R_1\le I(X_1;Y\mid X_2,Q),\quad
R_2\le I(X_2;Y\mid X_1,Q),\quad
R_1+R_2\le I(X_1,X_2;Y\mid Q),
```

under laws

```math
p(q)p(x_1\mid q)p(x_2\mid q)p(y\mid x_1,x_2),\qquad
\mathbb E c_1(X_1)\le r_1,\quad\mathbb E c_2(X_2)\le r_2.
```

The finite-valued $`Q`$ specifies a publicly known deterministic time-sharing schedule. Costs are constrained **on average across $`Q`$**. Requiring the budget separately in every time-sharing phase can incorrectly shrink the region: an encoder may use a high-cost phase and offset it with a lower-cost phase in the same admissible codeword.

For achievability, approximate $`p(q)`$ by deterministic phase proportions and use independent random codebooks according to each phase's product law. Joint decoding with the schedule known yields the three conditional mutual-information bounds by the usual MAC packing argument. With strict average cost slack, each user's fraction of over-budget words tends to zero by the law of large numbers. Replace them by minimum-cost words and charge their message indices to the error event. Every remaining codeword meets its budget, and the added average error vanishes. Boundary costs are obtained by minimum-cost mixing and closure, as in (a), separately for each sender. At a minimum possible budget, that sender's law must be supported on its minimum-cost alphabet in every positive-weight phase.

No unbounded time-sharing alphabet is needed: each product law contributes a vector of three mutual informations and two costs in $`\mathbb R^5`$. Carathéodory's theorem represents its averaged vector using at most six product laws. Thus $`|\mathcal Q|\le6`$ is a sufficient, though not necessarily minimal, bound. If either budget is below its minimum symbol cost, the region is infeasible.

### (c) Converse with the same constraints

Let a reliable block code have per-codeword costs at most $`r_j`$. Its independent message indices imply that $`X_{1i}`$ and $`X_{2i}`$ are independent at each fixed time. The standard MAC Fano and memorylessness argument gives

```math
n(R_1-\epsilon_n)\le\sum_i I(X_{1i};Y_i\mid X_{2i}),
```

```math
n(R_2-\epsilon_n)\le\sum_i I(X_{2i};Y_i\mid X_{1i}),\qquad
n(R_1+R_2-\epsilon_n)\le\sum_i I(X_{1i},X_{2i};Y_i).
```

For example, the first inequality follows by revealing $`W_2`$, writing $`I(W_1;Y^n\mid W_2)`$ as a difference of conditional entropies, upper-bounding the first by $`\sum_iH(Y_i\mid X_{2i})`$, and identifying the second as $`\sum_iH(Y_i\mid X_{1i},X_{2i})`$ using the deterministic encoders and the memoryless channel. The sum bound follows by applying Fano to the message pair.

Let $`Q`$ be uniform on the block coordinates, independent of the experiment, and set $`(X_1,X_2,Y)=(X_{1Q},X_{2Q},Y_Q)`$. Independence at each fixed coordinate gives exactly the conditional product factorization in (b), and the averages in the last displays become its three conditional mutual informations. Also

```math
\mathbb E c_j(X_j)
=\frac1n\sum_i\mathbb E c_j(X_{ji})
=\frac1{M_j}\sum_{w_j}\frac1n\sum_i c_j(x_{ji}(w_j))
\le r_j.
```

Compress $`Q`$ to at most six values while preserving the three bounds and two average costs. Compactness then gives a convergent subsequence of these laws; continuity and $`\epsilon_n\to0`$ prove that every achievable rate pair lies in the stated region. A time index cannot simply be discarded here: doing so may destroy independence of the two inputs and the correct cost-sharing structure.
