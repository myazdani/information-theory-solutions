# Exercise 15.10: Broadcast capacity and output marginals

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 599, Exercise 15.10. This is the ordinary memoryless broadcast channel without feedback.

## Solution

For any fixed block code, let $`E_j`$ be the event that receiver $`j`$ decodes its message incorrectly. The joint block error event is $`E_1\cup E_2`$. Event inclusion and the union bound give

```math
\max\{P(E_1),P(E_2)\}\le P(E_1\cup E_2)\le P(E_1)+P(E_2).
```

For a fixed encoder and message law, the distribution of $`(W_j,Y_j^n)`$ depends only on the marginal transition law $`p(y_j\mid x)`$, through its memoryless product. Therefore each individual error probability is identical for any two broadcast channels with these same conditional marginals.

If a sequence of codes has joint error tending to zero on one such channel, each individual error tends to zero. Their identical values on the second channel and the upper union bound imply that the joint error tends to zero there too. The same argument in reverse proves equality of the two achievable-rate sets and thus of their closures. The finite-block joint error can change because the intersection $`E_1\cap E_2`$ can change; this has no effect on whether both error probabilities vanish.
