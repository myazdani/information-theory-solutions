# Exercise 15.15: Shannon's two-way inner and outer bounds

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 602, Exercise 15.15. Sender 1's rate is decoded at terminal 2 and conversely. All alphabets are finite and the channel is memoryless.

## Solution

### (a) Independent-code inner bound

Fix a product input law $`p(x_1)p(x_2)`$. Independently generate i.i.d. codebooks for both terminals; these encoders ignore past outputs. Terminal 2 knows its own message and hence $`X_2^n`$. It selects the unique first-user codeword jointly typical with $`(X_2^n,Y_2^n)`$. The true triple is typical with probability tending to one. Each wrong first-user codeword is independent of that pair, giving the packing bound

```math
\mathbb E P_{e,2}\le o(1)+2^{nR_1}2^{-n(I(X_1;X_2,Y_2)-\delta)}.
```

Input independence makes $`I(X_1;X_2,Y_2)=I(X_1;Y_2\mid X_2)`$. Terminal 1's symmetric decoder gives

```math
\mathbb E P_{e,1}\le o(1)+2^{nR_2}2^{-n(I(X_2;Y_1\mid X_1)-\delta)}.
```

Both vanish under the two strict inequalities in the question. Their sum bounds the probability that either terminal fails; hence one deterministic pair of codebooks works for both. There is no sum-rate packing constraint because neither terminal must decode two unknown messages. Time-sharing gives further convex combinations if desired.

### (b) Joint-input outer bound, including adaptive encoders

A general deterministic two-way encoder may use $`X_{1i}=f_{1i}(W_1,Y_1^{i-1})`$ and $`X_{2i}=f_{2i}(W_2,Y_2^{i-1})`$. Fano and message independence give

```math
n(R_1-\epsilon_n)\le I(W_1;Y_2^n\mid W_2)
=\sum_i\left[H(Y_{2i}\mid W_2,Y_2^{i-1})
-H(Y_{2i}\mid W_1,W_2,Y_2^{i-1})\right].
```

In the first entropy retain only the known function $`X_{2i}`$. In the second entropy add $`Y_1^{i-1}`$ to the conditioning. Both changes increase the difference. The inputs are then determined, and memorylessness gives

```math
n(R_1-\epsilon_n)\le\sum_i I(X_{1i};Y_{2i}\mid X_{2i}).
```

The same derivation yields $`n(R_2-\epsilon_n)\le\sum_i I(X_{2i};Y_{1i}\mid X_{1i})`$ (enlarging a common vanishing remainder if necessary). Adaptive inputs need not be independent.

Let $`Q`$ be a uniform independent time index and let $`p(x_1,x_2)`$ be the averaged law of $`(X_{1Q},X_{2Q})`$. Because the conditional channel law does not depend on $`Q`$,

```math
I(X_{1Q};Y_{2Q}\mid X_{2Q},Q)
\le I(X_{1Q};Y_{2Q}\mid X_{2Q}),
```

as follows by removing $`Q`$ from the first conditional entropy and observing that the second conditional entropy is unchanged. The symmetric inequality holds for the other receiver, under the same averaged joint input law. Compactness of the finite input simplex supplies a convergent subsequence of such laws; continuity and $`\epsilon_n\to0`$ give

```math
R_1\le I(X_1;Y_2\mid X_2),\qquad
R_2\le I(X_2;Y_1\mid X_1)
```

for one joint law. Encoder randomization can be fixed in the usual average-error argument, or included as local independent seeds in the entropy argument. The difference between allowing general joint laws here and requiring product laws in (a) is why these bounds need not coincide.
