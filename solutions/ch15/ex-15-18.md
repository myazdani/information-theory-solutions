# Exercise 15.18: The square multiple-access channel

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 603, Exercise 15.18. Rates are in bits per channel use.

## Solution

### (a) Capacity region

Let $`B_j=X_j^2\in\{0,1\}`$. The output is $`B_1+B_2`$, so the signs of nonzero inputs carry no observable information. For independent inputs with $`P(B_1=1)=p`$, $`P(B_2=1)=q`$, the deterministic-channel bounds are

```math
R_1\le h_2(p),\qquad R_2\le h_2(q),\qquad R_1+R_2\le H(B_1+B_2).
```

We verify $`H(B_1+B_2)\le3/2`$. Write its output probabilities as $`a=(1-p)(1-q)`$, $`b=p+q-2pq`$, $`c=pq`$. For $`F(p,q)=H(a,b,c)`$,

```math
\partial_pF-\partial_qF=(p-q)\log_2(ac/b^2).
```

Since $`b^2\ge4ac`$, entropy increases when the two parameters move toward their average at fixed sum. On the diagonal it is $`2h_2(t)-2t(1-t)`$, a strictly concave function because its second derivative is $`4-2/((\ln2)t(1-t))<0`$. Its symmetry gives maximum $`3/2`$ at $`t=1/2`$. Continuity includes the boundary of the parameter square.

Consequently all product-law regions, and their convex hull, lie in

```math
\mathcal C=\{(R_1,R_2)\ge0:R_1\le1,\ R_2\le1,\ R_1+R_2\le3/2\}.
```

Independent fair $`B_1,B_2`$ give the three mutual informations $`1,1,3/2`$, so the MAC theorem attains this entire pentagon. This proves equality.

### (b) An attaining input law

For each user choose

```math
p_j^*(0)=\tfrac12,\qquad p_j^*(1)=p_j^*(-1)=\tfrac14,
```

independently across users. The induced squares are fair. The law therefore attains, for example, boundary points $`(1,1/2)`$, $`(1/2,1)`$, and $`(3/4,3/4)`$. Any division of the nonzero probability $`1/2`$ between the two signs works equally well. Coding distinguishes the zero/nonzero patterns; it cannot use the discarded signs for extra rate.
