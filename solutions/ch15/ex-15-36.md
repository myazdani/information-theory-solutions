# Exercise 15.36: Distributed descriptions of a shuffled three-card deck

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), pp. 608–609, Exercise 15.36, including the continuation specifying i.i.d. uniform permutations.

## Solution

Each source triple is a uniformly random permutation of three distinct cards, so $`H(X_1,X_2,X_3)=\log_2 6`$. Knowing two cards determines the third, giving

```math
H(X_i\mid X_j,X_k)=0.
```

Knowing one card leaves two equally likely assignments of the other two, so

```math
H(X_i,X_j\mid X_k)=1.
```

The three-source Slepian–Wolf theorem now gives the exact closed rate region, in bits per deal:

```math
R_1,R_2,R_3\ge0,
```

```math
R_1+R_2\ge1,\qquad R_1+R_3\ge1,\qquad R_2+R_3\ge1,
```

```math
R_1+R_2+R_3\ge\log_2 6.
```

There are no positive individual lower bounds, but all three pair inequalities are necessary. A sender may be silent: for example $`(R_1,R_2,R_3)=(0,1,\log_2 3)`$ meets every bound and is achievable in the closure. First describing the third card and then describing which of the remaining two cards the second sender holds also explains that corner operationally. Symmetry gives its permutations. If equal rates are required, the minimum common rate is $`\tfrac13\log_2 6`$; this automatically exceeds the pairwise requirement of one half bit per sender. The i.i.d. block formulation is what permits these noninteger per-deal rates.
