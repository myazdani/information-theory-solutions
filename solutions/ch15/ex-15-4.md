# Exercise 15.4: Typical-sequence achievability for a Gaussian MAC

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), pp. 597–598, Exercise 15.4. Assume independent messages, independent Gaussian noise of variance $`N>0`$, no feedback, and power budgets $`P_1,P_2`$ per use.

## Solution

First take positive budgets and a rate pair strictly inside the three stated bounds. Choose $`0<P_j'<P_j`$ sufficiently close to $`P_j`$ that the rate pair still satisfies the bounds with $`P_j'`$. Independently generate each user's $`M_j=\lfloor2^{nR_j}\rfloor`$ codewords with i.i.d. $`N(0,P_j')`$ coordinates.

For $`X_1\perp X_2`$ under these Gaussian laws, define the three information densities

```math
\imath_1=\log_2\frac{f(Y\mid X_1,X_2)}{f(Y\mid X_2)},\quad
\imath_2=\log_2\frac{f(Y\mid X_1,X_2)}{f(Y\mid X_1)},\quad
\imath_{12}=\log_2\frac{f(Y\mid X_1,X_2)}{f(Y)}.
```

Use the continuous typical set on which their block averages are at least $`I_1-\delta,I_2-\delta,I_{12}-\delta`$ and both empirical input powers are at most $`P_j`$. These conditions hold with probability tending to one by the law of large numbers; they are the likelihood-ratio form of weak joint typicality. The decoder chooses the unique codeword pair in this set with its observation.

A wrong first-user codeword is independent of the actual $`(X_2^n,Y^n)`$. Under the resulting product measure its chance of passing the first likelihood-ratio test is at most $`2^{-n(I_1-\delta)}`$: integrate over the acceptance set and replace the product density by at most that factor times the true joint density. The analogous bound holds for a wrong second-user codeword. When both codewords are wrong they are independent of each other and of the output, so the joint test has probability at most $`2^{-n(I_{12}-\delta)}`$. A union bound yields

```math
\mathbb E P_e^{(n)}\le o(1)
+2^{nR_1}2^{-n(I_1-\delta)}
+2^{nR_2}2^{-n(I_2-\delta)}
+2^{n(R_1+R_2)}2^{-n(I_{12}-\delta)}\longrightarrow0.
```

Gaussian entropy evaluation gives

```math
I_1=\tfrac12\log_2(1+P_1'/N),\quad
I_2=\tfrac12\log_2(1+P_2'/N),\quad
I_{12}=\tfrac12\log_2(1+(P_1'+P_2')/N).
```

To ensure a per-codeword, rather than merely ensemble-average, power constraint, replace every over-budget codeword by the zero vector and declare all its message indices errors in the analysis. The expected fraction of such indices tends to zero for each user. Use the original typicality decoder, which never accepts an over-budget word; for a pair of unchanged codewords the experiment is unchanged. Thus the modified code has the desired power constraint, and its expected average error is still bounded by a vanishing quantity. At least one deterministic pair of codebooks has error no larger than this expectation.

Choose $`\delta`$ below all three rate gaps. This proves the requested interior achievability. Taking closure and $`P_j'\uparrow P_j`$ includes boundary points. If a budget is zero, that user transmits zero and has rate zero; the remaining assertion is the ordinary Gaussian coding theorem, proved by the same single-user argument. The error criterion here is average error, as in the MAC capacity theorem.
