# Exercise 15.3: The three multiple-access cuts

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 597, Exercise 15.3; the three cuts in the printed diagram are used.

## Solution

Independence and the chain rule imply

```math
I(X_1;Y,X_2)=I(X_1;X_2)+I(X_1;Y\mid X_2)
=I(X_1;Y\mid X_2).
```

The same identity holds with the senders exchanged.

- Cut $`S_1`$ separates sender 1 from sender 2 and the receiver. The only independent message that must cross is $`W_1`$. The receiving side already has $`X_2`$, so the one-use information crossing the cut is $`I(X_1;Y,X_2)=I(X_1;Y\mid X_2)`$.
- Cut $`S_2`$ separates sender 2 from sender 1 and the receiver. It gives $`R_2\le I(X_2;Y\mid X_1)`$.
- Cut $`S_3`$ separates both senders from the receiver. Both independent messages cross, giving $`R_1+R_2\le I(X_1,X_2;Y)`$.

Strict inequalities describe directly achievable interior rates for the selected product input law; closure gives the weak inequalities. Time-sharing between product laws gives the full MAC capacity region. The independence assumption is essential for deleting $`I(X_1;X_2)`$ in the first identity.
