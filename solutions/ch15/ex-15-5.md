# Exercise 15.5: Gaussian multiple-access converse

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 598, Exercise 15.5. Noise variance $`N>0`$; no feedback; independent uniform messages; each codeword obeys its sender's average-power bound.

## Solution

Let $`\epsilon_n\to0`$ be the normalized Fano remainder for a reliable sequence. Independence of the messages implies independence of $`X_{1i}`$ and $`X_{2i}`$ at each time. Let $`v_{ji}=\mathrm{Var}(X_{ji})`$; the codeword constraints imply

```math
\frac1n\sum_i v_{ji}\le\frac1n\sum_i\mathbb E X_{ji}^2\le P_j.
```

Giving the other message to the decoder can only help. Memorylessness and conditioning reducing entropy give

```math
n(R_1-\epsilon_n)
\le I(W_1;Y^n\mid W_2)
\le\sum_i [h(Y_i\mid X_{2i})-h(Z_i)]
=\sum_i [h(X_{1i}+Z_i)-h(Z_i)].
```

A Gaussian maximizes differential entropy at a specified variance. Therefore, by concavity of the logarithm,

```math
R_1-\epsilon_n
\le\frac1{2n}\sum_i\log_2(1+v_{1i}/N)
\le\tfrac12\log_2(1+P_1/N).
```

The second user's bound follows by exchanging indices. For the sum,

```math
n(R_1+R_2-\epsilon_n)
\le I(W_1,W_2;Y^n)
\le\sum_i[h(Y_i)-h(Z_i)]
\le\frac12\sum_i\log_2\left(1+\frac{v_{1i}+v_{2i}}N\right)
\le\frac n2\log_2\left(1+\frac{P_1+P_2}N\right).
```

The sum of variances is valid because the two inputs are independent; nonzero input means do not improve these bounds. Gaussian convolution gives finite entropies under the second-moment constraints. Letting $`n\to\infty`$ proves all three inequalities, including cases where either power is zero. The same converse also holds under the weaker message-averaged power constraint.
