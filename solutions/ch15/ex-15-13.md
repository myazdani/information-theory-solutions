# Exercise 15.13: A binary symmetric channel followed by erasure

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 601, Exercise 15.13. Visual inspection of the diagram gives a BSC with crossover $`p`$ from $`X`$ to $`Y_1`$, followed by an independent erasure with probability $`\alpha`$ from $`Y_1`$ to $`Y_2`$. The central second-stage output is an erasure symbol.

## Solution

Put $`C=1-h_2(p)`$. For $`0\le\alpha<1`$, the capacity region is the triangle

```math
R_1,R_2\ge0,\qquad R_1+\frac{R_2}{1-\alpha}\le C.
```

To prove the outer bound, use the degraded broadcast theorem for $`U\to X\to Y_1\to Y_2`$. Independent erasure reveals nothing when it occurs, and reveals $`Y_1`$ otherwise, so

```math
I(U;Y_2)=(1-\alpha)I(U;Y_1).
```

Moreover $`H(Y_1\mid X,U)=h_2(p)`$, and therefore

```math
I(X;Y_1\mid U)+I(U;Y_1)
=H(Y_1)-h_2(p)\le1-h_2(p)=C.
```

Combining the two rate inequalities proves the claimed triangular outer bound. For achievability, send only to receiver 1 for fraction $`\lambda`$ of the block, achieving $`\lambda C`$. In the remainder send only to receiver 2, whose point-to-point capacity is $`(1-\alpha)C`$, achieved by a fair binary input. This gives the whole sloping boundary; rate reduction fills its interior.

If $`\alpha=1`$, receiver 2 is always erased and the region is $`0\le R_1\le C,R_2=0`$. If $`p=1/2`$, both rates are zero. Values $`p>1/2`$ cause no difficulty because complementing the BSC output changes its crossover to $`1-p`$ without changing $`C`$.
