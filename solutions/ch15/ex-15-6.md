# Exercise 15.6: When time-sharing strictly helps

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), p. 598, Exercise 15.6. All rates are in bits per use.

## Solution

### (a) The two endpoints

Hold $`X_2=0`$. The specified channel then has $`Y=X_1`$ deterministically, so sender 1 can send one bit per use with sender 2 silent. This gives $`(1,0)`$. Holding $`X_1=0`$ similarly gives $`(0,1)`$.

### (b) Every nondegenerate product law has a strict sum loss

Write $`p=P(X_1=1)`$ and $`q=P(X_2=1)`$. The only random output conditional on the inputs occurs for $`(1,1)`$, and its conditional entropy is one bit. Thus

```math
I(X_1,X_2;Y)=H(Y)-pq\le1-pq\lt 1
```

when $`0<p,q<1`$.

### (c) Strict enlargement by convexification

Time-share the two codes from (a) in fractions $`\lambda`$ and $`1-\lambda`$. Every point $`(\lambda,1-\lambda)`$, $`0<\lambda<1`$, is achievable. No single product input law can achieve such a point: if both input laws are nondegenerate, part (b) violates the required unit sum; if either input is deterministic, its own conditional mutual information is zero, contradicting the positivity of both rates.

In fact the complete capacity region is the triangle $`R_1,R_2\ge0`$, $`R_1+R_2\le1`$. The binary output gives the sum outer bound, and time-sharing plus rate reduction achieves the entire triangle. This also shows concretely why taking only a union of the product-law pentagons is insufficient.
