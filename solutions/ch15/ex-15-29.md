# Exercise 15.29: Three language-specific receivers

Source: Cover and Thomas, 2nd ed. (2006), pp. 606–607, Exercise 15.29. Assume $`M\ge1`$ distinct words per language, and measure rates in bits per spoken word.

## Solution

Every receiver recognizes the language of every transmitted word, but within a foreign language sees just its language label. Let $`L`$ denote the language.

### (a) Maximum rate to the Dutch receiver

The Dutch receiver's output alphabet has $`M+2`$ symbols: its $`M`$ Dutch words and one symbol each for Spanish and French. Its deterministic marginal channel is surjective, so

```math
R_D^{\max}=\log_2(M+2).
```

Attaining the maximum output entropy requires a uniform Dutch-view output. Accordingly each Dutch word has probability $`1/(M+2)`$, and

```math
P(L=D)=\frac{M}{M+2},\qquad P(L=S)=P(L=F)=\frac1{M+2}.
```

### (b) Maximum simultaneous Spanish rate

The joint Dutch/Spanish output reveals, beyond the Dutch view, only the Spanish word on Spanish positions. For any input distribution,

```math
H(Y_D,Y_S)\le H(Y_D)+P(L=S)\log_2M.
```

Applying Fano to the pair of decoded messages bounds $`R_D+R_S`$ by this joint output entropy, with a vanishing remainder (use the averaged input law and entropy concavity to single-letterize). If $`R_D\to\log_2(M+2)`$, the marginal-entropy bound forces the averaged Dutch-view output to tend to its unique uniform maximizer. Thus $`P(L=S)\to1/(M+2)`$, and

```math
R_S\le\frac{\log_2M}{M+2}.
```

The bound is achievable without sacrificing the Dutch rate. For a long block, use all Dutch-view sequences of an appropriate fixed composition approaching the uniform distribution on $`M+2`$ symbols as Dutch codewords. Their number has exponential rate $`\log_2(M+2)`$. Each contains $`n/(M+2)+o(n)`$ Spanish positions. Encode the Spanish message as a sequence of $`M`$-ary words in those positions. The Spanish receiver identifies its language positions directly and reads these words in order; the Dutch receiver still sees its prescribed Dutch-view sequence. Integer rounding of compositions has vanishing rate cost.

### (c) Additional French rate

The same construction simultaneously leaves $`n/(M+2)+o(n)`$ French positions. Fill them with an independent French-message word sequence. It changes neither of the other receivers' observations relevant to their messages. Consequently the full triple

```math
\left(R_D,R_S,R_F\right)
=\left(\log_2(M+2),\frac{\log_2M}{M+2},\frac{\log_2M}{M+2}\right)
```

is achievable in the closure. The pairwise Dutch/French version of the preceding converse proves this French rate is maximal even without imposing the Spanish constraint. It is positive for $`M>1`$. In the degenerate case $`M=1`$, all receivers see just the same language symbol, the Dutch maximum is $`\log_2 3`$, and no additional positive private rate remains.
