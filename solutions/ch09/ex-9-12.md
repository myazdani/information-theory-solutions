# Exercise 9.12 — A transmitter receding with time

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 294.

## Solution

Let $`P_i=\mathbb EX_i^2`$, so $`\sum_iP_i\le nP`$. By Gaussian maximum entropy, output subadditivity, and memorylessness,
```math
I(X^n;Y^n)\le\frac12\sum_{i=1}^n\log_2\left(1+\frac{P_i}{Ni^2}\right).
```
Choose $`k=\lceil\sqrt n\rceil`$. Each of the first $`k`$ summands is at most $`\frac12\log_2(1+nP/N)`$. For the tail use $`\ln(1+x)\le x`$ and $`i>k`$ to obtain
```math
I(X^n;Y^n)\le\frac{k}{2}\log_2(1+nP/N)
+\frac{nP}{2N k^2\ln2}=o(n).
```
For a uniform message among $`2^{nR}`$ alternatives, Fano gives $`nR\le I(X^n;Y^n)+1+nR P_e`$. If $`P_e\to0`$, division by $`n`$ forces $`R=0`$. Thus capacity per use is zero, even though finite early transmissions can carry a growing, sublinear amount of information. Assume $`N>0`$.
