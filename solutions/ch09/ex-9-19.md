# Exercise 9.19 — Parametrizing water filling by its level

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 296.

## Solution

Assume positive noise variances $`\lambda_i`$. At water level $`\lambda`$, the optimal powers are $`P_i=(\lambda-\lambda_i)_+`$. Summing gives
```math
P(\lambda)=\sum_{i:\lambda_i\le\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_i).
```
For an active channel, $`1+P_i/\lambda_i=\lambda/\lambda_i`$; for an inactive channel its capacity contribution is zero. Hence
```math
C(\lambda)=\frac12\sum_{i:\lambda_i\le\lambda}\log_2(\lambda/\lambda_i).
```
At the activation thresholds the new summand is zero, so both functions are continuous. Between successive ordered noise levels, the active set is fixed: $`P`$ is linear in $`\lambda`$ with slope equal to the number of active channels, while $`C`$ is an affine function of $`\log_2\lambda`$. Their derivatives on such an interval obey $`dC/dP=1/(2\lambda\ln2)`$. The notation in the source calling the noises $`X_i`$ is read as $`Z_i`$ consistently with the channel equation.
