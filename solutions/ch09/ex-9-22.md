# Exercise 9.22 — Recovering channel noise while carrying a message

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 299.

## Solution

Take any ordinary Gaussian-channel block code of rate $`R<C=\frac12\log_2(1+P/N)`$ with vanishing decoding error and codeword energy at most $`nP`$. Decode the codeword as $`\widehat X^n(Y^n)`$ and set
```math
\widehat Z^n=Y^n-\widehat X^n.
```
Whenever the codeword is correct, the real-valued noise vector is recovered exactly. Hence $`\Pr(\widehat Z^n\ne Z^n)\le\Pr(\widehat X^n\ne X^n)\to0`$. Expurgation, or a maximum-error coding theorem, makes this reliable uniformly over the arbitrarily selected codeword, rather than only on average.

Conversely, exact noise recovery lets the receiver form $`\widehat X^n=Y^n-\widehat Z^n`$. For distinct codewords, this identifies the transmitted message whenever the noise estimate is correct. Gaussian channel capacity and Fano's inequality therefore rule out any rate $`R>C`$ with vanishing error. The supremum of allowable rates is exactly $`C`$; every strictly smaller positive rate works when $`P>0,N>0`$. A claim of achievability exactly at the finite-block rate boundary is unnecessary. If $`P=0`$, only one input is available and the noise is directly observed.
