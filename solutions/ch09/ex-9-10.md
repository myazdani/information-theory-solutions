# Exercise 9.10 — Information per area of randomly placed film grains

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 293–294.

## Solution

A small cell has a grain with probability $`\alpha=\lambda\,dA+o(dA)`$. Let $`X=1`$ mean illumination and let $`Y=1`$ mean a visible developed grain. Then $`Y=XZ`$ with an independent Bernoulli-$`\alpha`$ occupancy variable. For $`q=\Pr(X=1)`$,
```math
I=h_2(\alpha q)-q h_2(\alpha),
\qquad C(\alpha)=\log_2(1+2^{-h_2(\alpha)/\alpha}).
```
The maximizing illumination probability is $`q^*=[\alpha(1+2^{h_2(\alpha)/\alpha})]^{-1}`$. Since
```math
2^{-h_2(\alpha)/\alpha}
=\alpha(1-\alpha)^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}
=\alpha/e+o(\alpha),
```
we get $`C(\alpha)=\alpha/(e\ln2)+o(\alpha)`$ and $`q^*\to1/e`$. Independent Poisson-cell occupancies permit summing their capacities. Divide by cell area and let the grid become fine:
```math
C_{\rm area}=\frac{\lambda}{e\ln2}\approx0.5307378454\lambda
\quad\text{bits per square inch}.
```
The thin-cell approximation can equally use exact occupancy probability $`1-e^{-\lambda dA}`$; it has the same first-order limit. Unknown grain locations reduce the coefficient from 1 to $`1/(e\ln2)`$.
