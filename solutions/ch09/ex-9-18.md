# Exercise 9.18 — Known and unknown Gaussian noise means

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 296.

## Solution

The printed exercise specifies the means but omits an explicit conditional noise variance. Denote that common variance by $`N>0`$, so the Gaussian model is $`Z_i=\mu_i+W_i`$, with independent $`W_i\sim N(0,N)`$. If unit variance was intended, set $`N=1`$ below.

### (a)
With all means zero, the usual Gaussian channel has capacity $`\frac12\log_2(1+P/N)`$.

### (b)
The receiver can subtract the known deterministic $`e^i`$ from each output. This invertible output translation costs the transmitter no power and restores the channel in part (a). Thus the capacity is unchanged, even though the uncentered noise means grow rapidly.

### (c)
If the unobserved means are independent $`N(0,N_1)`$ variables, also independent of $`W_i`$ and the input, then their sum with $`W_i`$ is iid $`N(0,N+N_1)`$. Consequently
```math
C=\frac12\log_2\left(1+\frac{P}{N+N_1}\right).
```
The independence and the common conditional variance are the natural Gaussian-noise interpretation; without specifying a variance, the source does not determine a numeric capacity.
