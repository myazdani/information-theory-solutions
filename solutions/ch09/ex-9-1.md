# Exercise 9.1 — Repeated conditionally independent observations

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 290.

## Solution

### (a)
Conditional independence and identical conditional laws imply
```math
I(X;Y_1,Y_2)=H(Y_1,Y_2)-2H(Y_1\mid X)
=2I(X;Y_1)-I(Y_1;Y_2).
```
For continuous variables with finite differential entropies, the same calculation uses $`h`$; more generally it follows from the mutual-information chain rule without subtracting undefined infinite entropies.

### (b)
For every admissible input distribution, $`I(X;Y_1,Y_2)\le2I(X;Y_1)`$. Thus the two-look capacity is at most twice the one-look capacity, under the same input constraints. Strictness needs qualification: zero-capacity channels give equality, and arbitrary noncompact channel families need not attain their suprema. For a finite channel with positive capacity, equality is impossible: independence of the outputs would force $`\operatorname{Var}(W(y\mid X))=0`$ for each $`y`$, making the used rows identical and one-look information zero. Compactness then gives a strict capacity inequality.
