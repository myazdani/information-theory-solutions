# Exercise 9.20 — Nearest-neighbor decoding under general noise

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 296–297.

## Solution

Assume the noise is independent of the transmitted signal and has second moment $`N>0`$. Rates below $`C_G=\frac12\log_2(1+P/N)`$ are meant by achievability of $`C_G`$ as a supremum.

### (a)
Take independent $`X\sim N(0,P)`$ and put $`a=P/(P+N)`$. The error $`X-aY`$ has second moment $`PN/(P+N)`$, even if the noise has a nonzero mean. Conditioning and the Gaussian maximum-entropy bound yield
```math
h_2(X\mid Y)=h_2(X-aY\mid Y)
\le\frac12\log_2\left(2\pi e\frac{PN}{P+N}\right).
```
Subtract from $`h_2(X)=\frac12\log_2(2\pi eP)`$ to obtain $`I(X;Y)\ge C_G`$. If singular conditional laws give infinite mutual information, the inequality is immediate; the entropy bound can otherwise be justified using conditional relative entropy to a Gaussian.

### (b) and (c): a common decoding argument
Draw each codeword independently and uniformly from the radius-$`\sqrt{nP}`$ sphere, and use minimum Euclidean distance. Let $`g_n(r)`$ be the ensemble-average error when the noise has fixed norm $`r`$. The following verifies each requested hint step.

#### Hint (a): rotational symmetry
For two noise vectors of the same norm, an orthogonal transformation maps one to the other. Applying the same transformation to the transmitted and competing codewords preserves their joint distribution and all decoding distances. Thus the average error depends on the noise only through its norm.

#### Hint (b): monotonicity
Fix the codebook, transmitted word $`x`$, and a unit noise direction $`u`$. A competitor $`x'`$ is at least as close at noise amplitude $`r`$ exactly when
```math
2r\,u\cdot(x'-x)\ge\|x'-x\|^2.
```
For $`r\ge0`$, this event is either impossible or an upper interval in $`r`$. The union over competitors is therefore increasing. Ties have probability zero for the continuous random-code ensemble. Averaging proves that $`g_n`$ is nondecreasing.

#### Hint (c): compare to a slightly noisier Gaussian channel
Fix $`R<C_G`$. Choose $`N<N_0<N'`$ such that $`R<\frac12\log_2(1+P/N')`$. For iid Gaussian noise $`G^n`$ of variance $`N'`$, write its ensemble error as $`e_n^G`$. Monotonicity gives
```math
g_n(\sqrt{nN_0})\le
\frac{e_n^G}{\Pr(\|G^n\|^2\ge nN_0)}.
```
For the actual noise,
```math
e_n\le g_n(\sqrt{nN_0})+\Pr(\|Z^n\|^2>nN_0).
```
#### Hint (d): conclude achievability
The stipulated Gaussian spherical-code theorem gives $`e_n^G\to0`$, and its denominator above tends to 1. For iid noise the final tail tends to zero by the strong law. For stationary ergodic noise the ergodic theorem gives the same fact, $`n^{-1}\sum_i Z_i^2\to N`$ almost surely. Thus in both cases $`e_n\to0`$. Some deterministic codebook has error at most the ensemble average, and all its codewords obey the power constraint exactly. This establishes (b) and the extension (c) with the same Euclidean decoder, without needing the actual noise density for decoding.
