# Exercise 9.21 — The Gaussian signal-noise saddle point

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 298.

## Solution

Assume $`P,N>0`$. For Gaussian noise $`Z^*`$ and any independent zero-mean input of variance $`P`$,
```math
I(X;X+Z^*)=h_2(X+Z^*)-h_2(Z^*)
\le\frac12\log_2(1+P/N).
```
This follows from the maximum-entropy bound at output variance $`P+N`$, with equality for Gaussian $`X^*`$.

For the other direction, take $`X^*\sim N(0,P)`$ and any independent zero-mean noise of variance $`N`$. The linear estimator $`aY`$, $`a=P/(P+N)`$, has error variance $`PN/(P+N)`$. Therefore
```math
I(X^*;Y)=h_2(X^*)-h_2(X^*\mid Y)
\ge\frac12\log_2\frac{P}{PN/(P+N)}
=\frac12\log_2(1+P/N).
```
The conditional-entropy inequality follows by replacing $`X^*`$ with $`X^*-aY`$, dropping the conditioning, and using Gaussian maximum entropy. This also handles the conclusion when the mutual information is infinite; a conditional Gaussian relative-entropy argument supplies the bound for singular noise laws.

Together these prove the saddle inequalities. The Gaussian signal guarantees at least the stated value against every noise law, and Gaussian noise caps every signal law at that value. Thus both orders of min/max equal $`\frac12\log_2(1+P/N)`$.

For densities, the exercise's suggested entropy-power proof is an alternative: EPI gives $`I(X^*;X^*+Z)\ge\frac12\log_2(1+2\pi eP/2^{2h_2(Z)})`$, and $`2^{2h_2(Z)}\le2\pi eN`$. The linear-estimation proof avoids needing a noise density. Operationally, Gaussian noise is the least favorable noise under a variance constraint, and Gaussian signaling is a robust maximizing choice. These are unilateral statements with the other player fixed at its Gaussian strategy, not a claim that every non-Gaussian pair is ordered in one direction.
