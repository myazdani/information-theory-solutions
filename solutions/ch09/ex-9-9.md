# Exercise 9.9 — A noiseless direction hidden in a covariance matrix

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 293.

## Solution

The covariance annihilates $`v=(1,1,-1)^T`$. Thus the Gaussian scalar $`v^TZ`$ has variance zero and is identically zero. Let $`u=v/\sqrt3`$ and transmit $`X=Au`$; then $`u^TY=A`$ exactly, while $`\mathbb E\|X\|^2=\mathbb EA^2`$.

For any $`P>0`$ and any finite integer $`M`$, choose $`M`$ distinct equally likely amplitudes in $`[-\sqrt P,\sqrt P]`$. Their labels are recovered without error, conveying $`\log_2 M`$ bits under the power constraint. Since $`M`$ is arbitrary, the capacity is infinite. At $`P=0`$ the only input is zero, and capacity is zero. A determinant formula assuming positive-definite noise would miss this noiseless subspace.
