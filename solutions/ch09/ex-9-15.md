# Exercise 9.15 — Binary signaling through a wide uniform disturbance

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 295.

## Solution

### (a)
The output ranges are $`[0,a]`$ for input 0 and $`[1,a+1]`$ for input 1. Outside their common interval $`[1,a]`$, the input is determined. In the overlap, both conditional densities equal $`1/a`$, so the posterior remains $`(1-p,p)`$. The overlap has probability $`(a-1)/a`$. Therefore
```math
H(X\mid Y)=\frac{a-1}{a}h_2(p),\qquad I(X;Y)=\frac1a h_2(p).
```
### (b)
The output density is $`(1-p)/a`$ on $`[0,1)`$, $`1/a`$ on $`[1,a]`$, and $`p/a`$ on $`(a,a+1]`$. Direct integration gives
```math
h_2(Y)=\log_2 a+\frac1a h_2(p),\qquad h_2(Y\mid X)=\log_2 a.
```
Their difference is again $`h_2(p)/a`$.

### (c)
Binary entropy is maximized by $`p=1/2`$, so $`C=1/a`$ bit per use. Endpoints of intervals have probability zero and do not affect the calculation.
