# Exercise 9.4 — Mean-limited input with exponential noise

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 291.

## Solution

The finite formula requires the intended nonnegative-input constraint $`X\ge0`$, in addition to $`\mathbb EX\le\lambda`$, with $`\lambda\ge0,\mu>0`$. The printed statement does not explicitly give that support restriction; its necessity is addressed below.

For a nonnegative random variable of mean at most $`m`$, comparison with the exponential density of mean $`m`$ gives $`h\le1+\ln m`$. Thus
```math
I(X;X+Z)=h(X+Z)-h(Z)\le\ln(\lambda+\mu)-\ln\mu
```
in nats. Equality is achieved when $`Y`$ is exponential with mean $`\lambda+\mu`$. Take the independent input mixture
```math
P_X=\frac{\mu}{\lambda+\mu}\delta_0
+\frac{\lambda}{\lambda+\mu}\mathrm{Exp}(\text{mean }\lambda+\mu).
```
Its mean is $`\lambda`$. Multiplying Laplace transforms verifies the output:
```math
\mathbb Ee^{-sX}=\frac{1+\mu s}{1+(\lambda+\mu)s},\qquad
\mathbb Ee^{-s(X+Z)}=\frac1{1+(\lambda+\mu)s}.
```
Therefore $`C=\log_2(1+\lambda/\mu)`$ bits.

If arbitrary real inputs are allowed with only the stated upper bound on their mean, capacity is instead infinite. For each finite $`M`$, choose $`M`$ equally spaced points centered at zero. Their mean remains zero regardless of spacing, while exponential noise causes a nearest/right-interval decoding error tending to zero as the spacing grows. Fano's inequality then gives information approaching $`\log_2 M`$; let $`M`$ grow. Thus the support condition is substantive, not merely technical.
