# Exercise 9.13 — Two-use colored Gaussian feedback optimization

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 294.

## Solution

Let $`r=|\rho|<1`$ and $`P\ge0`$. The requested expression $`\frac12\log_2(\det K_Y/\det K_Z)`$ is the total information over the two uses; divide it by 2 for information per use. Zero input power gives zero in either setting.

### Without feedback
Diagonalize the noise covariance, whose eigenvalues are $`1-r,1+r`$. Gaussian input and water filling are optimal. With total power $`2P`$, the resulting maximum is
```math
J_{\rm nf}=\begin{cases}
\frac12\log_2\bigl(1+2P/(1-r)\bigr),&P\le r,\\
\frac12\log_2\bigl((1+P)^2/(1-r^2)\bigr),&P\ge r.
\end{cases}
```
In the first case all power goes to the lower-noise eigenvector. In the second, the two eigenmode powers are $`P+r`$ and $`P-r`$.

### With causal feedback
An exact scalar-root answer follows. For $`r>0,P>0`$, let $`a_*`$ be the unique root in $`(P,2P)`$ of
```math
2P-2a+r\frac{2P+2(2P-1)a-3a^2}
{\sqrt{a(1+a)(2P-a)}}=0.
```
Define
```math
F(a)=(1+a)(1+2P-a)-r^2+2r\sqrt{a(1+a)(2P-a)}.
```
Then the maximum is
```math
J_{\rm fb}=\frac12\log_2\frac{F(a_*)}{1-r^2}.
```
At $`r=0`$, set $`a_*=P`$ and the same formula gives $`\log_2(1+P)`$.

Here is a derivation and an attaining strategy. Use $`X_1\sim N(0,a)`$, independent of the noises, and write
```math
X_2=uX_1+bZ_1+V,
```
where $`V`$ is independent Gaussian of variance $`s\ge0`$. The transmitter knows $`Z_1=Y_1-X_1`$ from feedback. This parameterization describes every jointly Gaussian causal strategy, with power $`a+au^2+b^2+s=2P`$. Writing $`Z_2=\rho Z_1+E`$ with independent innovation variance $`1-\rho^2`$ gives
```math
\det K_Y=(1+a)(1-r^2+s)+a(\rho+b-u)^2.
```
For fixed $`a,s`$, Cauchy–Schwarz maximizes the squared term by $`|\rho+b-u|=r+\sqrt{(1+a)(2P-a-s)/a}`$. Substitution gives $`F(a)`$ with the square root's final factor replaced by $`2P-a-s`$. For $`r>0`$ this is maximized at $`s=0`$. One attaining choice is
```math
b=\mathrm{sgn}(\rho)\sqrt{\frac{a(2P-a)}{1+a}},\qquad
u=-\mathrm{sgn}(\rho)\sqrt{\frac{2P-a}{a(1+a)}}.
```
The symbol $`u`$ in this display is the coefficient of $`X_1`$. Gaussian entropy maximization and the usual causal Gaussian covariance representation show that no other distribution improves this covariance optimum.

To verify the root uniquely determines the global maximum, put $`g(a)=a(1+a)(2P-a)`$. On $`0\le a\le P`$, both $`2P-2a`$ and $`g'(a)`$ are nonnegative, so $`F`$ is increasing. On $`[P,2P)`$, $`g''(a)=4P-2-6a<0`$, hence $`\sqrt g`$ is strictly concave and $`F'`$ strictly decreasing. It is positive at $`P`$ and tends to negative infinity at $`2P`$. This proves existence and uniqueness and supplies a stable bisection computation.

For $`|\rho|=1`$ and $`P>0`$, a noise-free eigenmode already makes the no-feedback capacity infinite; feedback cannot remove that fact. The displayed determinant ratio itself is undefined at a singular denominator and must be interpreted via the actual channel, not direct substitution.
