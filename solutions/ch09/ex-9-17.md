# Exercise 9.17 — Concentrating all block power into one time slot

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 295–296.

## Solution

### (a)
The zero second moments force $`X_i=0`$ almost surely for $`i\ge2`$, so those outputs carry no information about the input. Gaussian $`X_1`$ of variance $`nP`$ maximizes the remaining scalar-channel information. Therefore
```math
\max\frac1n I(X^n;Y^n)=\frac1{2n}\log_2(1+nP/N)\longrightarrow0.
```
### (b)
Let $`P_i=\mathbb EX_i^2`$. Output-entropy subadditivity and the scalar Gaussian bound imply
```math
\frac1n I(X^n;Y^n)\le\frac1{2n}\sum_i\log_2(1+P_i/N)
\le\frac12\log_2(1+P/N),
```
where the last inequality uses concavity and $`\sum_iP_i\le nP`$. Independent Gaussian inputs with variance $`P`$ attain equality. Thus spreading power optimally yields a positive constant rate, while an isolated impulse uses its increasing amplitude only logarithmically efficiently. Assume $`N>0`$.
