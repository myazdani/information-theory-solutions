# Exercise 9.2 — Correlated Gaussian observations of one input

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 290–291.

## Solution

Assume $`N>0`$. Define $`A=(Y_1+Y_2)/2`$ and $`D=(Y_1-Y_2)/2`$. Their noise variances are $`N(1+\rho)/2`$ and $`N(1-\rho)/2`$, and Gaussianity makes the noises independent. The difference $`D`$ contains no input and is independent of $`(X,A)`$, so only the average is useful.

### (a)
At $`\rho=1`$, the observations are identical; the capacity is $`\frac12\log_2(1+P/N)`$.

### (b)
At $`\rho=0`$, averaging halves the noise variance, giving $`\frac12\log_2(1+2P/N)`$.

### (c)
At $`\rho=-1`$, the noises cancel exactly, and $`A=X`$. If $`P>0`$, arbitrarily large finite constellations fit in a bounded interval satisfying the power constraint, so capacity is infinite. If $`P=0`$, the input is fixed and capacity is zero. Gaussian input attains the finite capacities in (a) and (b).
