# Exercise 11.18 — A geometric sample conditioned on a large mean

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 405.

## Solution

Assume $`0<p<1`$, with mean $`\mu=1/(1-p)`$.

### (a)
For $`\alpha>\mu`$, exponential tilting preserves the geometric family. The tilted law with mean $`\alpha`$ is
```math
P^*(k)=\frac1\alpha\left(1-\frac1\alpha\right)^{k-1},\qquad k\ge1.
```
Its natural-log divergence from $`Q(k)=(1-p)p^{k-1}`$ is
```math
J(\alpha)=\ln\frac1{\alpha(1-p)}
+(\alpha-1)\ln\frac{1-1/\alpha}{p}.
```
The geometric moment-generating function $`(1-p)e^t/(1-pe^t)`$ is finite for $`t< -\ln p`$. Chernoff optimization and the matching tilted-law lower bound give $`\Pr(\bar X_n\ge\alpha)=\exp[-n(J(\alpha)+o(1))]`$. For $`\alpha\le\mu`$ the upper-tail exponent is zero (and for $`\alpha\le1`$ the event is certain).

### (b)
The conditional limit for every fixed $`k`$ is $`P^*(k)`$ above when $`\alpha>\mu`$. It follows from the Gibbs conditioning principle, or from ratios of negative-binomial tail probabilities after fixing the first sample. For $`\alpha<\mu`$ the event has probability tending to one, giving the original law. At $`\alpha=\mu`$, the central-limit fluctuation event affects any fixed finite set of coordinates negligibly, again giving the original law. Thus the tilted mean is $`\max(\alpha,\mu)`$ for the nontrivial thresholds.

### (c)
For $`p=1/2,\alpha=4`$,
```math
J=\ln(27/16),\qquad
\Pr(\bar X_n\ge4)=2^{-n(\log_2(27/16)+o(1))},
```
and
```math
\Pr(X_1=k\mid\bar X_n\ge4)\longrightarrow\frac14(3/4)^{k-1}.
```
The first-order exponent is approximately $`0.7548875022`$ bits per sample.
