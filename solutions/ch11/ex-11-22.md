# Exercise 11.22 — A large deviation of the difference of two sample sums

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 406.

## Solution

Let $`W_i=X_i-Y_i`$. Independence of the two sequences makes these iid, with natural-log moment-generating function
```math
\Lambda(\theta)=\ln\mathbb E e^{\theta W}
=\ln\mathbb E_{Q_1}e^{\theta X}+\ln\mathbb E_{Q_2}e^{-\theta Y}.
```
For finite alphabets, or the usual Cramér regularity of exponential moments around zero, the upper-tail exponent is
```math
J(t)=\sup_{\theta\ge0}\{\theta t-\Lambda(\theta)\},\qquad
\Pr\left(\sum_iX_i-\sum_iY_i\ge nt\right)
=\exp[-n(J(t)+o(1))]
```
at attainable regular thresholds. It is zero for $`t\le\mathbb EX-\mathbb EY`$. At an interior upper-tail optimum, choose $`\theta_*\ge0`$ satisfying $`\Lambda'(\theta_*)=t`$; the most likely tilted marginals are
```math
P_1^*(x)=\frac{Q_1(x)e^{\theta_*x}}{\mathbb E_{Q_1}e^{\theta_*X}},\qquad
P_2^*(y)=\frac{Q_2(y)e^{-\theta_*y}}{\mathbb E_{Q_2}e^{-\theta_*Y}}.
```
Equivalently, for finite supports,
```math
J(t)=\inf_{P_1,P_2:\ \mathbb E_{P_1}X-\mathbb E_{P_2}Y\ge t}
[D(P_1\|Q_1)+D(P_2\|Q_2)].
```
Sanov's type bounds justify this expression directly. Allowing a joint tilted law does not help: its divergence from $`Q_1Q_2`$ is the displayed sum plus the nonnegative mutual information, and the constraint depends only on its marginals. An impossible threshold has infinite exponent. For unrestricted heavy-tailed laws without exponential-moment assumptions, a positive Cramér exponent need not exist; the moment formula always supplies a Chernoff bound where finite, but asserting a matching positive exponent would require additional hypotheses.
