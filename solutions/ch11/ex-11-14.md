# Exercise 11.14 — An unusually large Gaussian empirical second moment

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 404.

## Solution

Use a generic threshold $`t`$ to separate the source's $`\alpha^2`$ in part (a) from its $`\alpha`$ in part (b).

### (a)
For $`X\sim N(0,\sigma^2)`$, the moment-generating function of $`X^2`$ is $`M(s)=(1-2\sigma^2s)^{-1/2}`$ for $`s<1/(2\sigma^2)`$. Its upper-tail rate function is
```math
J(t)=\begin{cases}
0,&t\le\sigma^2,\\
\frac12\left[\frac t{\sigma^2}-1-\ln\frac t{\sigma^2}\right],&t>\sigma^2.
\end{cases}
```
Indeed, maximizing $`st-\ln M(s)`$ over $`s\ge0`$ yields $`s_*=(1-\sigma^2/t)/(2\sigma^2)`$ on the second branch. Chernoff's bound supplies the upper probability bound. Exponential tilting at this parameter changes the law to $`N(0,t)`$; its law of large numbers, or a slightly larger tilted variance followed by a limit, supplies the matching lower exponential bound. Consequently the requested probability is $`\exp[-n(J(\alpha^2)+o(1))]`$. Divide $`J`$ by $`\ln2`$ for a bit exponent.

### (b)
For a rare upper-second-moment event with threshold $`t>\sigma^2`$, the information projection is $`P^*=N(0,t)`$. To verify, any law with second moment $`v`$ satisfies
```math
D(P\|N(0,\sigma^2))
=-h(P)+\tfrac12\ln(2\pi\sigma^2)+\frac v{2\sigma^2}
\ge\tfrac12[v/\sigma^2-1-\ln(v/\sigma^2)].
```
Gaussian maximum entropy gives the inequality, with equality only for the zero-mean Gaussian of variance $`v`$. The last expression increases for $`v>\sigma^2`$, so the constrained minimum occurs at $`v=t`$. Conditional empirical distributions concentrate around this law. As part (b) is printed, take $`t=\alpha`$; if it refers to the event of part (a), take $`t=\alpha^2`$. If $`t\le\sigma^2`$, the minimizer is the original Gaussian and the exponent is zero.
