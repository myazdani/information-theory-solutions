# Exercise 11.21 — Entropy as the exponential rate of multinomial counts

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 406.

## Solution

The integral factorial bounds imply, in any fixed logarithm base,
```math
\log(n!)=n\log n-n\log e+O(\log(n+1)).
```
Let $`k=\lfloor np\rfloor`$ and $`q_n=k/n`$. Applying this to the three factorials gives
```math
\log_2\binom nk=n h_2(q_n)+O(\log(n+1)).
```
Since $`q_n\to p`$ and binary entropy is continuous on $`[0,1]`$, division by $`n`$ gives the requested limit $`h_2(p)`$. At the endpoints the binomial coefficient is one, so the result also follows directly.

For the multinomial count, take $`k_i=\lfloor np_i\rfloor`$ for $`i<m`$ and $`k_m=n-\sum_{i<m}k_i`$. These are nonnegative counts summing to $`n`$, and $`k_i/n\to p_i`$ for every $`i`$. The same expansion yields
```math
\log_2\frac{n!}{\prod_i k_i!}
=nH(k_1/n,\ldots,k_m/n)+O(m\log(n+1)).
```
Thus its normalized logarithm tends to $`H(p_1,\ldots,p_m)`$. Use $`0!=1`$ and $`0\log0=0`$ for zero counts. The last count is the residual after the first $`m-1`$ counts, resolving the harmless indexing mismatch in the printed summation.
