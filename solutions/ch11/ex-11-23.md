# Exercise 11.23 — A wrong model receiving a larger likelihood

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 406.

## Solution

First assume $`P,Q`$ are distinct and strictly positive on a finite common alphabet. Let $`L(x)=\ln(P(x)/Q(x))`$ and
```math
M(s)=\mathbb E_Qe^{sL(X)}=\sum_xP(x)^sQ(x)^{1-s}.
```
Its log derivative at zero is $`-D(Q\|P)<0`$ and at one is $`D(P\|Q)>0`$. Convexity therefore gives a minimizer $`s_*\in(0,1)`$, characterized by
```math
\sum_x\frac{P(x)^{s_*}Q(x)^{1-s_*}}{M(s_*)}\ln\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}=0.
```
The Chernoff/Cramér exponent at threshold zero is
```math
C(P,Q)=-\ln M(s_*)=-\ln\min_{0\le s\le1}\sum_xP(x)^sQ(x)^{1-s},
```
so the requested probability is $`\exp[-n(C(P,Q)+o(1))]`$. Strict rather than weak positivity of the empirical likelihood ratio leaves the exponent unchanged because positive likelihood-ratio symbols exist, and types with strictly positive means can approach the boundary optimum.

Equivalently, the minimizing type is $`T^*(x)\propto P(x)^{s_*}Q(x)^{1-s_*}`$, with $`D(T^*\|Q)=C(P,Q)`$. This follows by a Lagrange multiplier for the nonnegative mean-log-ratio constraint; type-class upper and lower bounds establish the exponent.

If $`P=Q`$, the strict event is empty and its exponent is infinite, rather than the zero Chernoff information for a non-strict threshold. If zeros occur, use the direct type formulation: restrict to $`S=\{x:P(x)Q(x)>0\}`$ and minimize $`D(T\|Q)`$ over $`T`$ supported on $`S`$ with positive mean log ratio, taking its closure only when that strict feasible set is nonempty. If no $`Q`$-possible symbol has $`P(x)>Q(x)`$, the event is impossible. This support-aware formulation avoids silently applying a finite full-support formula to an impossible strict event.
