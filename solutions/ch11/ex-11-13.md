# Exercise 11.13 — A direct binomial proof of a large-deviation exponent

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 403–404.

## Solution

Let $`1/2<q<p<1`$ and $`K=\sum_iX_i\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)`$. Define $`b_i=\binom ni q^i(1-q)^{n-i}`$.

The exact tail starts at $`\lceil np\rceil`$. Starting at $`\lfloor np\rfloor`$ as in the displayed upper bound only adds a nonnegative term. Moreover
```math
\frac{b_{i+1}}{b_i}=\frac{n-i}{i+1}\frac q{1-q}.
```
For all sufficiently large $`n`$, $`\lfloor np\rfloor`$ lies beyond the binomial mode because $`p>q`$. Hence this ratio is at most one throughout the indicated tail, and its first term is the largest. The assertion need not hold for every small $`n`$.

For any integer $`k`$, the binary type bounds give
```math
(n+1)^{-1}2^{-nD_2(k/n\|q)}\le b_k\le2^{-nD_2(k/n\|q)}.
```
Thus the first term, with either floor or ceiling, is $`2^{-n(D_2(p\|q)+o(1))}`$ by continuity. The whole probability is trapped between the ceiling term and at most $`n+1`$ times the floor term:
```math
b_{\lceil np\rceil}\le\Pr(K/n\ge p)
\le(n+1)b_{\lfloor np\rfloor}.
```
The polynomial factors have vanishing normalized logarithms, so
```math
-\frac1n\log_2\Pr(K/n\ge p)\longrightarrow
p\log_2\frac pq+(1-p)\log_2\frac{1-p}{1-q}.
```
At $`p=1`$ the event has probability exactly $`q^n`$ and the same endpoint formula holds.
