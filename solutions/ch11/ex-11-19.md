# Exercise 11.19 — Fisher information as negative expected curvature

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 405.

## Solution

Assume common support and sufficient smoothness/integrability to differentiate normalization twice. Pointwise,
```math
\partial_\theta^2\ln f_\theta
=\frac{\partial_\theta^2f_\theta}{f_\theta}
-\frac{(\partial_\theta f_\theta)^2}{f_\theta^2}.
```
Multiply by $`f_\theta`$ and integrate over the observation variable:
```math
\mathbb E_\theta\partial_\theta^2\ln f_\theta(X)
=\partial_\theta^2\int f_\theta(x)\,dx
-\int\frac{(\partial_\theta f_\theta(x))^2}{f_\theta(x)}\,dx
=-J(\theta).
```
This is the desired identity. The cancellation is a normalization/differentiation argument; no integration by parts in the observation variable is needed for a general parameterized family. If one derives it by differentiating the zero-mean score identity, the extra product-rule term is exactly $`J(\theta)`$. Parameter-dependent support can contribute boundary terms and invalidate the unqualified formula.
