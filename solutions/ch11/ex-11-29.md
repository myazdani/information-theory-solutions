# Exercise 11.29 — Three ways to generate simplex-valued vectors

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 408.

## Solution

For $`n\ge2`$, methods (b) and (c) are uniform; method (a) is not. For $`n=1`$ the simplex is a single point and all methods are trivially equivalent.

### (a)
Let $`S=\sum_iY_i`$ and set $`Y_i=S X_i`$, with $`X_n=1-\sum_{i<n}X_i`$. The Jacobian in coordinates $`(x_1,\ldots,x_{n-1},s)`$ is $`s^{n-1}`$. Uniform-cube input requires $`0<s<1/\max_i x_i`$. Integrating out $`s`$ gives the simplex-coordinate density
```math
f_X(x)=\int_0^{1/\max_i x_i}s^{n-1}\,ds
=\frac1{n(\max_i x_i)^n}.
```
This is nonconstant, with more density near balanced coordinates, so normalizing iid uniforms does not give uniform simplex measure.

### (b)
For iid exponentials of common rate $`\lambda>0`$, the same transformation gives joint density $`\lambda^n e^{-\lambda s}s^{n-1}`$, independent of $`x`$. Integrating over $`s>0`$ yields
```math
f_X(x)=\lambda^n\int_0^\infty e^{-\lambda s}s^{n-1}\,ds=(n-1)!.
```
This constant density is exactly the uniform simplex law, equivalently the Dirichlet law with all parameters 1. It does not depend on $`\lambda`$.

### (c)
Sort the $`n-1`$ uniform cut points as $`0<U_{(1)}<\cdots<U_{(n-1)}<1`$. Their ordered joint density is $`(n-1)!`$. Map them to successive spacings $`X_1=U_{(1)}`$, $`X_i=U_{(i)}-U_{(i-1)}`$, and $`X_n=1-U_{(n-1)}`$. The triangular transformation has unit Jacobian and maps the order region onto the simplex interior, preserving the constant density. Thus the stick lengths, in left-to-right order, are uniform on the simplex. Sequentially cutting a uniformly chosen fraction of the remaining stick is a different procedure and is not what is specified here.

The density calculations provide exact proofs. A seeded numerical comparison of the three methods is included as a reproducible check of their differing coordinate variances.
