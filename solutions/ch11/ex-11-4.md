# Exercise 11.4 — Sequential projection: a counterexample for inequalities

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 400–401.

## Solution

The asserted sequential-projection property is false for the inequality-defined convex sets as printed. Here is a three-symbol counterexample, with all masses strictly positive.

Take $`Q=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`$, let $`\mathcal P_1=\{p:p_1\ge2/5\}`$, and let $`\mathcal P_2=\{p:p_3\le1/10\}`$. The second constraint has the required form by taking $`g=-\mathbf1_{\{3\}}`$ and $`\beta=-1/10`$. The reference distribution belongs to neither set.

By symmetry and convexity, the projection onto the first set is
```math
P^*=(2/5,3/10,3/10).
```
The direct projection of uniform $`Q`$ onto the intersection maximizes entropy. Its optimum is
```math
R^*=(9/20,9/20,1/10),
```
which satisfies the first inequality strictly. In contrast, the projection of $`P^*`$ onto $`p_3\le1/10`$ keeps the first two masses in the ratio $`4:3`$, giving
```math
S^*=(18/35,27/70,1/10).
```
This also satisfies $`p_1\ge2/5`$, so it is the projection onto the intersection as well. Strict convexity gives $`D(S^*\|P^*)<D(R^*\|P^*)`$. Thus the requested conclusion fails.

The missing hypothesis can be seen from the identity
```math
D(R\|Q)=D(R\|P^*)+D(P^*\|Q)
+\sum_x(R(x)-P^*(x))\log\frac{P^*(x)}{Q(x)}.
```
For an affine family of equality constraints, the last term vanishes throughout that family, and sequential projection onto a nested affine subfamily does hold. For inequality families it is generally only nonnegative and varies with $`R`$. The two objectives then need not have the same minimizer. The explicit divergence comparison is reproduced numerically in the chapter artifact; this supplies a resolution of the printed claim rather than an invalid proof.
