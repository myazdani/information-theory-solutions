# Exercise 11.7 — The quadratic divergence metric and Fisher information

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 401.

## Solution

Assume the usual regularity: common support, differentiability twice in the parameter, justified differentiation under the integral, and finite Fisher information. Fix $`\theta`$ and differentiate $`F(t)=D_2(p_\theta\|p_t)`$, where the subscript denotes bits. Then $`F(\theta)=0`$ and
```math
F'(\theta)=-\frac1{\ln2}\mathbb E_\theta[\partial_\theta\ln p_\theta(X)]=0.
```
Normalization gives zero mean score. Differentiating normalization again gives $`-\mathbb E_\theta\partial_\theta^2\ln p_\theta(X)=J(\theta)`$, so $`F''(\theta)=J(\theta)/\ln2`$. Taylor's theorem therefore yields
```math
D_2(p_\theta\|p_{\theta+\delta})
=\frac{\delta^2}{2\ln2}J(\theta)+o(\delta^2),
```
and the requested limit is $`J(\theta)/\ln4`$.

The support/regularity qualification is essential. A family such as uniform distributions on $`[0,\theta]`$ changes support and can have a first-order or infinite directed divergence, so the displayed expansion is not a universal identity for arbitrary parameterized densities.
