# Exercise 11.12 — A directed testing exponent under the opposite error constraint

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 403.

## Solution

The bounded error is $`\Pr(\text{decide }H_1\mid H_2)`$. Thus the Chernoff–Stein lemma must be applied with $`p_2`$ as the null distribution: the best exponent for the other error is $`D(p_2\|p_1)`$, not the divergence with its arguments chosen by the numbering of the hypotheses.

The pointwise likelihood ratio $`p_2(x)/p_1(x)`$ is $`1/2,1,2`$ at $`x=-1,0,1`$. Therefore
```math
D_2(p_2\|p_1)=\frac14(-1)+\frac14(0)+\frac12(1)=\frac14\text{ bit}.
```
Writing $`\alpha_n^*`$ for the minimum constrained error,
```math
-\frac1n\log_2\alpha_n^*\longrightarrow\frac14,
\qquad \alpha_n^*=2^{-n(1/4+o(1))}.
```
The equivalent natural-log exponent is $`(\ln2)/4`$. An optimal likelihood-ratio test thresholds $`\sum_iX_i`$, with threshold and boundary randomization chosen to keep the error under $`H_2`$ at most one half. The constant one-half constraint is strictly between zero and one, so Stein's exponent applies.
