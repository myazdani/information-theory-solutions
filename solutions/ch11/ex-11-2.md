# Exercise 11.2 — The local chi-square expansion of divergence

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 400.

## Solution

Assume $`Q(x)>0`$ on a finite alphabet and write $`P(x)=Q(x)+\delta_x`$, $`\sum_x\delta_x=0`$, with $`|\delta_x/Q(x)|`$ small. In nats,
```math
D(P\|Q)=\sum_xQ(x)(1+u_x)\ln(1+u_x),\qquad u_x=\delta_x/Q(x).
```
Using $`(1+u)\ln(1+u)=u+u^2/2-u^3/6+O(u^4)`$ and cancellation of the linear term,
```math
D(P\|Q)=\frac12\sum_x\frac{\delta_x^2}{Q(x)}
-\frac16\sum_x\frac{\delta_x^3}{Q(x)^2}
+O\!\left(\sum_x\frac{\delta_x^4}{Q(x)^3}\right).
```
Thus the first nonzero term is half the Pearson chi-square statistic. In bits, every term must be divided by $`\ln2`$, so the leading term is $`\chi^2/(2\ln2)`$. The printed coefficient $`1/2`$ uses natural logarithms. A zero reference mass needs separate support treatment; adding positive mass there makes divergence infinite, not quadratic.
