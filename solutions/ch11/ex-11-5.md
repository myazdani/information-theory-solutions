# Exercise 11.5 — Counting sequences under an empirical mean constraint

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 401.

## Solution

Let $`\mathcal E=\{P:\sum_xP(x)g(x)\ge\alpha\}`$ and $`H^*=\max_{P\in\mathcal E}H(P)`$. If the set is empty, the sequence count is zero. Otherwise, the count is the sum of type-class sizes over feasible $`n`$-types. Since there are at most $`(n+1)^m`$ types,
```math
N_n\le(n+1)^m2^{nH^*}.
```
For the lower bound, choose a feasible entropy maximizer $`P^*`$ and approximate it by feasible types $`P_n`$ converging to it. Such approximations exist if $`\alpha<\max_xg(x)`$: first mix $`P^*`$ slightly with a point having strictly larger $`g`$, then round counts finely enough to preserve the inequality. If $`\alpha=\max_xg(x)`$, all feasible distributions are supported on the maximizing symbols, and rounding within that face works. The type-class bound gives
```math
N_n\ge|T_{P_n}|\ge(n+1)^{-m}2^{nH(P_n)}.
```
Continuity makes $`H(P_n)\to H^*`$. Taking normalized logarithms proves $`n^{-1}\log_2N_n\to H^*`$, the precise first-order exponential statement.
