# Exercise 11.1 — Four directed hypothesis divergences

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 399–400.

## Solution

The following divergences are in nats; divide finite values by $`\ln2`$ for bits.

### (a)
For positive Gaussian variances, averaging the log density ratio under $`f_1`$ gives
```math
D(f_1\|f_2)=\ln\frac{\sigma_2}{\sigma_1}
+\frac{\sigma_1^2}{2\sigma_2^2}-\frac12
=\frac12\left[\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}-1-\ln\frac{\sigma_1^2}{\sigma_2^2}\right].
```
### (b)
The log ratio is $`\ln(\lambda_1/\lambda_2)+(\lambda_2-\lambda_1)x`$. Since $`\mathbb E_1X=1/\lambda_1`$,
```math
D(f_1\|f_2)=\ln(\lambda_1/\lambda_2)+\lambda_2/\lambda_1-1.
```
### (c)
The interval $`[0,a)`$ has positive $`f_1`$ probability and zero $`f_2`$ probability. Thus $`D(f_1\|f_2)=+\infty`$.

### (d)
The fair coin assigns positive probability to tails, impossible for the two-headed coin. The divergence in the requested direction is again infinite. Reversing the distributions would give one bit, illustrating why the direction matters.
