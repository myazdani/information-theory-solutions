# Exercise 11.25 — Conditioning binomial blocks on a large average

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 407.

## Solution

Assume $`0<q<1`$ and $`m\ge1`$. The binomial moment-generating function is $`(1-q+qe^\theta)^m`$. Exponential tilting gives
```math
P_\theta(k)=\binom mk\lambda^k(1-\lambda)^{m-k},\qquad
\lambda=\frac{qe^\theta}{1-q+qe^\theta}.
```
For a rare upper-mean event $`mq<\alpha<m`$, the minimizing tilt has mean $`m\lambda=\alpha`$, hence $`\lambda=\alpha/m`$. Strict convexity of divergence identifies a unique minimizing type, and the finite-alphabet conditional limit theorem implies that the first observation converges to this tilted law.

If $`\alpha<mq`$, the conditioning event has probability tending to one, so the conditional law remains the original binomial and $`\lambda=q`$. At $`\alpha=mq`$, either the conditional limit theorem or the central limit theorem with any fixed first sample removed gives the same limit. Therefore, for attainable $`\alpha\le m`$,
```math
\lambda=\max\{q,\alpha/m\}.
```
At $`\alpha=m`$ the event forces every observation to be $`m`$, so the formula gives the point mass $`\lambda=1`$. For $`\alpha>m`$ the event is impossible and the conditional distribution is undefined. Degenerate $`q=0,1`$ are handled directly: whenever the event has positive probability, the deterministic original law remains unchanged.
