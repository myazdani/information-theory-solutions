# Exercise 11.24 — Estimating the weight of a known two-component mixture

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 407.

## Solution

Assume $`0<\theta<1`$ for the regular Fisher-information formulas.

### (a)
Marginalizing the unobserved label gives $`f_\theta(x)=\theta f_1(x)+(1-\theta)f_0(x)`$.

### (b)
The score is $`(f_1-f_0)/f_\theta`$, hence
```math
J(\theta)=\int\frac{[f_1(x)-f_0(x)]^2}{\theta f_1(x)+(1-\theta)f_0(x)}\,dx.
```
The integrand is taken as zero where both component densities vanish.

### (c)
For an unbiased estimator from one observation, the mean-square error is at least $`1/J(\theta)`$. For $`n`$ iid observations the bound is $`1/[nJ(\theta)]`$. If the component laws are identical, $`J=0`$ and the parameter is unidentifiable; there is no estimator unbiased for all $`\theta`$.

### (d)
If the laws are different, choose any measurable set $`A`$ with $`a_1=\int_Af_1\ne a_0=\int_Af_0`$. Then
```math
T(X)=\frac{\mathbf1_{\{X\in A\}}-a_0}{a_1-a_0}
```
is unbiased because $`\Pr_\theta(X\in A)=a_0+\theta(a_1-a_0)`$. It is bounded and has finite variance, although its values need not lie in $`[0,1]`$. Averaging this estimator over independent observations preserves unbiasedness and divides its variance by $`n`$. The construction works without requiring finite component means or disjoint supports.
