# Exercise 11.16 — Testing two geometric distributions

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 404.

## Solution

Let $`q=1-p`$ with $`0<p<1`$. The first law is geometric with mass $`2^{-x}`$, and the second has mass $`qp^{x-1}`$.

### (a)
Under the first law, $`\mathbb E_0X=2`$. Thus, in bits,
```math
D_2(p_0\|p_1)=\mathbb E_0[-X-\log_2q-(X-1)\log_2p]
=-2-\log_2(pq)=\log_2\frac1{4pq}.
```
It is zero at $`p=q=1/2`$ and infinite at the degenerate endpoints when the reference loses support.

### (b)
With equal priors, choose the hypothesis of larger likelihood. Writing $`S=\sum_iX_i`$, the log likelihood ratio favoring $`H_1`$ is
```math
\ln\frac{p_1(X^n)}{p_0(X^n)}
=n\ln(2q)+(S-n)\ln(2p).
```
Decide $`H_1`$ when this is positive, $`H_0`$ when negative, and break a tie arbitrarily. If $`p>1/2`$ it is an upper-threshold test on $`S`$; if $`p<1/2`$ it is a lower-threshold test. At $`p=1/2`$ the distributions coincide and the minimum Bayes error is one half. The likelihood comparison, rather than a threshold formula divided by $`\ln(2p)`$, handles that exceptional case cleanly.

The exact minimum Bayes error can also be written using the sufficient statistic $`S`$. There are $`\binom{s-1}{n-1}`$ positive-integer sample vectors with sum $`s`$, so
```math
P_{e,\min}=\frac12\sum_{s=n}^\infty\binom{s-1}{n-1}\min\{2^{-s},q^n p^{s-n}\}.
```
This is equivalently a sum of the two corresponding negative-binomial tails on opposite sides of the likelihood threshold. Either decision on an exact likelihood tie gives the same Bayes contribution.
