# Exercise 11.10 — An information projection under typicality equalities

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 402.

## Solution

Use natural logarithms for the exponential-family parameterization; changing log base only rescales its multipliers. Initially assume a finite common positive support and a nonempty constraint set.

### (a)
Minimize $`\sum_{x,y}P\ln(P/Q_0)`$ under normalization and the three affine moment constraints. Differentiating the Lagrangian at an interior optimum gives
```math
P^*(x,y)=Q_0(x,y)\exp[\lambda_0+\lambda_1\ln Q_X(x)
+\lambda_2\ln Q_Y(y)+\lambda_3\ln Q(x,y)],
```
where multipliers enforce the constraints. The feasible set is convex and compact, and divergence is strictly convex on the reference support, so the minimizing distribution is unique. Multipliers need not be unique if constraints are redundant. A boundary optimum may require a limiting exponential tilt rather than finite multipliers. If $`Q_0`$ has zeros, restrict to its support and require a feasible finite-divergence distribution; without that qualification a finite unique projection need not exist.

### (b)
For $`Q_0=Q_XQ_Y`$, take $`\lambda_0=0,\lambda_1=\lambda_2=-1,\lambda_3=1`$. This gives $`P^*=Q`$, and $`Q`$ satisfies all three constraints by the definitions of its entropies.

A direct verification makes optimality transparent. For any feasible $`P`$,
```math
D(P\|Q_XQ_Y)=D(P\|Q)+\sum_{x,y}P(x,y)\ln\frac{Q(x,y)}{Q_X(x)Q_Y(y)}
=D(P\|Q)+I_Q(X;Y).
```
The last sum is fixed by the three equalities. It is minimized uniquely at $`P=Q`$. If some $`Q(x,y)=0`$, the finite cross-entropy constraint forces the corresponding $`P`$ mass to zero, and the same argument applies on that support.
