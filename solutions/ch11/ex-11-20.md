# Exercise 11.20 — Integral bounds for a factorial

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 405–406.

## Solution

The printed upper integral comparison in (11.328) is not valid as written: summing the endpoint logarithms from 2 through $`n-1`$ cannot be bounded by the integral over only $`[2,n-1]`$. Also the displayed upper bound $`n!\le n(n/e)^n`$ fails at small $`n`$ (for example $`n=1`$). The intended weak asymptotic assertion is valid; here are precise bounds and a repair.

Because $`\ln x`$ is increasing, on every interval $`[i-1,i]`$ it is at most $`\ln i`$. Its improper integral at zero is finite, so
```math
\ln(n!)\ge\int_0^n\ln x\,dx=n\ln n-n.
```
This justifies (11.329) and proves the desired lower bound for every $`n\ge1`$.

For a correct all-$`n`$ upper comparison, use $`\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\ln i\le\int_1^n\ln x\,dx`$ and then add $`\ln n`$. It gives
```math
(n/e)^n\le n!\le en(n/e)^n,
```
already enough to conclude $`\ln n!=n\ln n-n+O(\ln n)`$.

One can also recover the printed sharper upper factor for sufficiently large $`n`$. Concavity of $`\ln x`$ makes its integral at least the trapezoidal sum, so
```math
\ln(n!)\le\int_1^n\ln x\,dx+\tfrac12\ln n
=n\ln n-n+1+\tfrac12\ln n.
```
Thus $`n!\le e\sqrt n(n/e)^n\le n(n/e)^n`$ for $`n\ge8`$. The $`n=7`$ case also holds by direct calculation. Consequently the printed two-sided bound is correct for all sufficiently large $`n`$, though its stated integral justification and unrestricted small-$`n`$ form need the qualifications above.
