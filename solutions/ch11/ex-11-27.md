# Exercise 11.27 — A logarithmic variational identity

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 408.

## Solution

Let $`M=\mathbb E_PX`$ with $`X>0`$, and initially suppose $`M<\infty`$. Define the size-biased tilted law $`P^*(x)=xP(x)/M`$. For every admissible $`Q\ll P`$,
```math
D(Q\|P^*)=D(Q\|P)-\mathbb E_Q\log X+\log M.
```
Thus
```math
\mathbb E_Q\log X-D(Q\|P)=\log M-D(Q\|P^*)\le\log M.
```
When the separate terms are finite, equality holds at $`Q=P^*`$. More generally restrict the tilt to $`\{1/k\le X\le k\}`$ and, if needed, finite subsets of the support. The associated normalizing constants approach $`M`$, so these laws approach the same supremum without subtracting undefined infinite terms.

If $`M=\infty`$, the truncated normalizing constants diverge and the variational supremum is infinite. A measure assigning mass outside the support of $`P`$ has infinite divergence and does not improve the objective. Logs and divergence must use the same base. The identity is the same as in Exercise 8.6.
