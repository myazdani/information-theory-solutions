# Exercise 17.5: Two observations of a common intermediate variable

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 687.

## Solution

Assume the mutual informations in the identity below are finite. If the right side of the desired inequality is infinite the inequality is automatic; otherwise data processing makes both terms on its left finite, so the finite-information proof applies.

The chain rule gives the identity

```math
I(X;Z)+I(X;W)
=I(X;Z,W)+I(Z;W)-I(Z;W\mid X).
```

The stipulated Markov chain yields $`I(X;Z,W)\le I(X;Y)`$, and conditional mutual information is nonnegative. Combining these facts proves

```math
I(X;Z)+I(X;W)\le I(X;Y)+I(Z;W).
```

For finite informations the gap can be identified explicitly as
$`I(X;Y\mid Z,W)+I(Z;W\mid X)`$. Both terms are nonnegative, and equality holds exactly when both vanish.
