# Exercise 17.2: A Schur-complement determinant bound

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 687.

## Solution

Partition the indices into $`A=\{1,\ldots,p\}`$ and $`B=\{p+1,\ldots,n\}`$. The Schur complement

```math
S=K_{AA}-K_{AB}K_{BB}^{-1}K_{BA}
```

is positive definite. Block determinant identities show

```math
\frac{\det K}{\det K_{BB}}=\det S,
\qquad
\frac{\det K_{\{i\}\cup B,\{i\}\cup B}}{\det K_{BB}}=S_{ii}.
```

Hadamard's inequality, $`\det S\le\prod_{i=1}^p S_{ii}`$, is therefore precisely the required inequality. One proof of Hadamard is to write $`S`$ as the Gram matrix of vectors: the squared volume of their parallelepiped is at most the product of their squared lengths. Equality holds exactly when $`S`$ is diagonal. For $`p=n`$, take the determinant of the empty block to be 1; the statement reduces to ordinary Hadamard.
