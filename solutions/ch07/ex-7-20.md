# Exercise 7.20 — Two conditionally independent observations

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 233.

## Solution

### (a)
Conditional independence and identical conditional laws give
```math
I(X;Y_1,Y_2)=H(Y_1,Y_2)-2H(Y_1\mid X)
=2H(Y_1)-I(Y_1;Y_2)-2H(Y_1\mid X)
=2I(X;Y_1)-I(Y_1;Y_2).
```
### (b)
Since $`I(Y_1;Y_2)\ge0`$, maximizing the identity gives $`C_{\rm two}\le2C_{\rm one}`$.

For a finite channel with $`C_{\rm one}>0`$, the inequality is strict. Indeed independence of the two outputs would require, for every output letter $`y`$,
```math
\mathbb E[W(y\mid X)^2]=(\mathbb EW(y\mid X))^2.
```
Thus each $`W(y\mid X)`$ has zero variance, making all used channel rows equal and $`I(X;Y_1)=0`$. Equality at $`2C_{\rm one}>0`$ is therefore impossible; a maximizing distribution exists by compactness. If the one-look capacity is zero, both capacities are zero, so the exercise's word “less” must be read non-strictly in that degenerate case.
