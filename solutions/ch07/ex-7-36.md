# Exercise 7.36 — Fresh versus fixed multiplicative signs

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 239–240.

## Solution

### (a)
With a fresh independent fair sign every time, $`Y_i`$ is fair and independent of $`X_i`$. The memoryless channel has capacity zero.

### (b)
A fixed unknown sign can be learned from one pilot. Send $`X_1=1`$, revealing $`Z=Y_1`$, then send $`n-1`$ arbitrary information signs; the receiver recovers them as $`X_i=ZY_i`$. The block rate is $`(n-1)/n`$ bits/use with zero error, tending to 1. The binary output gives the matching upper bound of 1 bit/use, so capacity is 1.

Equivalently, with uniform $`X^n`$, $`H(Y^n)=n`$ and $`H(Y^n\mid X^n)=1`$, hence block mutual information $`n-1`$. The first description supplies an explicit operational code, rather than relying only on normalized mutual information for a channel with memory.
