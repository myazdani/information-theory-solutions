# Exercise 7.30 — Largest and smallest capacity over noise triples

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 237.

## Solution

### (a)
The input entropy is at most 2 bits. Noise values $`0,4,8`$ allow recovery by $`X=Y\bmod4`$, so uniform input achieves the maximum 2.

### (b)
Noise values $`0,1,2`$ give at most six possible outputs, hence $`I(X;Y)\le\log_2 6-\log_2 3=1`$. Equal mass on inputs 0 and 3 makes those six outputs uniform and attains 1.

To prove minimality, translate any noise triple to $`0<u<v`$ together with 0. If a number $`d\in\{1,2,3\}`$ is absent from $`\{u,v,v-u\}`$, then the supports for inputs 0 and $`d`$ are disjoint and give a zero-error bit. Otherwise the three positive differences are exactly $`1,2,3`$, forcing noise $`\{0,1,3\}`$ or its reflection $`\{0,2,3\}`$. Uniform input then gives output masses $`(1,2,2,3,2,1,1)/12`$ up to reversal and information
```math
\frac32-\frac14\log_2 3>1.
```
Thus every noise triple has capacity at least 1, and the minimum is exactly 1 bit. This repeats Exercise 7.24.
