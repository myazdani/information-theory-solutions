# Exercise 7.21 — Bounds from two nonnegative averages

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 233.

## Solution

Heights and weights are nonnegative. Let $`F`$ denote a fraction of the room's population.

### (a)
If a fraction $`F`$ has height at least 15 feet, its contribution to the mean height is at least $`15F`$. Thus $`15F\le5`$, or $`F\le1/3`$. In particular no more than one third can have height exactly 15 feet.

### (b)
A fraction $`F`$ of people who are simultaneously at least 10 feet tall and at least 300 pounds must satisfy $`10F\le5`$ and $`300F\le100`$. Hence
```math
F\le\min(1/2,1/3)=1/3.
```
There is no justified multiplication of these bounds: no independence is assumed. The bound is sharp under nonnegativity, as shown by one third of the people having height 10 and weight 300, and the remainder height 2.5 and weight 0. With strictly positive weights the same upper bound can be approached arbitrarily closely.
