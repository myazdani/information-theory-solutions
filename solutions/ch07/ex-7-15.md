# Exercise 7.15 — Exact finite-block joint typicality

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 227–231.

## Solution

Use $`n=25`$, $`p=0.1`$, $`\epsilon=0.2`$, and $`M=512`$. The reference to “length 12” in part (g) conflicts with its repeated explicit value $`n=25`$; the latter is used throughout.

### (a)
$`H(X)=H(Y)=1`$, $`H(X,Y)=1+h_2(0.1)`$, and $`I(X;Y)=1-h_2(0.1)`$.

### (b)
Every binary sequence has marginal probability $`2^{-n}`$, so every input sequence and every output sequence is marginally typical.

### (c)
For $`z^n=y^n\oplus x^n`$, independence gives $`p(x^n,y^n)=2^{-n}p(z^n)`$. Subtracting $`H(X,Y)=1+H(Z)`$ from its normalized negative logarithm leaves exactly $`-n^{-1}\log_2p(z^n)-H(Z)`$. Therefore joint typicality is equivalent to typical noise (and the automatically satisfied input typicality).

### (d)
For a noise word of weight $`k`$, its per-symbol information is
```math
\ell_k=-\log_2(0.9)+(k/25)\log_2 9.
```
The condition $`|\ell_k-h_2(0.1)|<0.2`$ selects $`k=1,2,3,4`$. Thus
```math
T=|A_\epsilon^{(25)}(Z)|=\binom{25}1+\binom{25}2+\binom{25}3+\binom{25}4=15275.
```
### (e)
When the fixed codeword is transmitted, joint typicality has probability
```math
s=\sum_{k=1}^4\binom{25}k(0.1)^k(0.9)^{25-k}.
```
### (f)
For any fixed received word, exactly $`T`$ of the $`2^{25}`$ candidate codewords are jointly typical with it, so $`q=T/2^{25}`$.

### (g)
The other 511 independently drawn codewords miss the typicality set independently. The exact probability of at least one spurious typical codeword is
```math
r=1-(1-q)^{511}.
```
### (h)
Correct unique-typical decoding requires both typical true noise and no spurious match. Conditional on every channel output, the no-match probability is the same $`(1-q)^{511}`$, so
```math
P_e=1-s(1-q)^{511}.
```
The [executed exact finite sums](../../src/information_theory/ch07/second_batch.py) and [results](../../artifacts/ch07/second-batch/results.json) give approximately $`s=0.8302`$, $`q=0.00045523`$, $`r=0.2076`$, and $`P_e=0.3421`$. Including weight zero gives the alternative radius-4 decoder's error $`1-(s+0.9^{25})(1-(T+1)/2^{25})^{511}`$, also recorded. No simulation or rounded table probabilities are used.
