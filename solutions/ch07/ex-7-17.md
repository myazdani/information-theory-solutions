# Exercise 7.17 — Optimal small codes on flips and erasures

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 231–232.

## Solution

Messages are equiprobable and ties may be broken by any fixed rule or uniformly.

### (a)
Use the even-parity code $`\{000,011,101,110\}`$. A codeword received exactly is decoded to itself; each of the four remaining words is distance one from three codewords and is mapped to any nearest one. With $`p=0.1`$, the average success probability is
```math
\frac14\sum_{y\in\{0,1\}^3}\max_{x\in\mathcal C}p(y\mid x)
=\frac14[4(0.9)^3+4(0.1)(0.9)^2]=0.81.
```
Thus $`P_e=0.19`$. This is optimal among all four-word length-three codes: the four received words that are codewords contribute at most $`0.9^3`$ each, and the other four contribute at most $`0.1(0.9)^2`$ each. Our code attains every term of that upper bound.

### (b)
With all eight words, the decoder returns the received word, so $`P_e=1-0.9^3=0.271`$.

### (c)
For the two repetition words on a BEC, any nonerased bit identifies the message. All three bits are erased with probability $`0.1^3`$; a guess is then wrong with probability $`1/2`$. Thus $`P_e=0.0005`$.

### (d)
For the even-parity four-word code, zero or one erasure is correctable; with two erasures there are two compatible words, and with three there are four. Therefore
```math
P_{e,4}=\tfrac12\binom32(0.1)^2(0.9)+\tfrac34(0.1)^3=0.01425.
```
For the full eight-word code, given $`E`$ erased positions, success probability is $`2^{-E}`$. Consequently
```math
P_{e,8}=1-\mathbb E2^{-E}=1-(0.9+0.1/2)^3=0.142625.
```
All error values and the optimal four-word BSC bound are independently verified by exhaustive code/output enumeration in the chapter numerical artifact.
