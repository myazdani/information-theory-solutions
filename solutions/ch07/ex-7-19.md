# Exercise 7.19 — Pigeons as a discrete channel

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 233.

## Solution

Assume the loss/replacement events are independent across pigeons and independent of the transmitted letters. There are 12 channel uses per hour; the three-minute flight time affects latency but not the asymptotic transmission rate.

### (a)
The model is a noiseless 256-symbol channel, with 8 bits per use. Its capacity is 96 bits/hour.

### (b)
Known missing arrival slots are erasures. A 256-ary erasure channel has capacity $`8(1-\alpha)`$ bits per use, attained by uniform letters. Thus the rate is $`96(1-\alpha)`$ bits/hour.

### (c)
The transition law is
```math
W(y\mid x)=(1-\alpha)\mathbf1_{y=x}+\alpha/256.
```
The probability of an incorrect output is $`q=255\alpha/256`$. A channel row has entropy $`h_2(q)+q\log_2 255`$. Uniform input makes the output uniform, so symmetry and the output-entropy bound give
```math
C=12[8-h_2(q)-q\log_2 255]\quad\text{bits/hour}.
```
Replacement does not always change the letter, which explains the factor $`255/256`$. If only a long-run fraction of arbitrary adversarial attacks were specified, a different channel model would be needed; a fraction alone does not determine Shannon capacity.
