# Exercise 7.12 — An input symbol that is never needed

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 226.

## Solution

Let $`q`$ be the mass assigned to the middle row. The other two rows have entropy $`h_2(1/3)`$, while the middle row has entropy $`\log_2 3`$. Therefore
```math
I(X;Y)\le\log_2 3-(1-q)h_2(1/3)-q\log_2 3
=(1-q)\,[\log_2 3-h_2(1/3)]\le\frac23.
```
Use the first and third inputs with probability $`1/2`$ each and never the middle input. Their output mixture is uniform on three symbols, so the entropy bound is attained and $`C=2/3`$ bit.

The middle channel row is exactly the average of the other two. It can be simulated by privately randomizing between those inputs, and it creates more conditional output uncertainty without enlarging the achievable output distributions. The displayed strict loss when $`q>0`$ also shows that every capacity-achieving input must omit it.
