# Exercise 7.28 — Choosing between disjoint-output channels

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 236.

## Solution

Let $`J`$ be the selected channel, with probabilities $`\lambda,1-\lambda`$. The choice is included in the input and is determined by the output because the output alphabets are disjoint.

### (a)
The mutual information decomposes into
```math
I(X;Y)=H(J)+I(X;Y\mid J)
\le h_2(\lambda)+\lambda C_1+(1-\lambda)C_2.
```
Capacity-achieving conditional inputs attain this for every $`\lambda`$. Put $`Z=2^{C_1}+2^{C_2}`$ and $`\lambda^*=2^{C_1}/Z`$. The right side equals $`\log_2 Z-D(\operatorname{Bern}(\lambda)\|\operatorname{Bern}(\lambda^*))`$, proving $`C=\log_2 Z`$ and $`2^C=2^{C_1}+2^{C_2}`$.

### (b)
A noiseless alphabet of size $`M`$ has capacity $`\log_2 M`$, so $`2^C`$ acts as an effective alphabet size. Disjoint alternatives add their effective sizes, just as maximum-entropy choice between disjoint populations gives the analogous entropy identity. The selector itself contributes $`H(J)`$ bits, which is why a mere weighted average of capacities is insufficient.

### (c)
The diagram combines a BSC of capacity $`1-h_2(p)`$ with one noiseless isolated symbol of capacity zero. Therefore
```math
C=\log_2\bigl(1+2^{1-h_2(p)}\bigr).
```
Use the BSC with probability $`2^{1-h_2(p)}/(1+2^{1-h_2(p)})`$ and its two inputs equally; use the isolated symbol otherwise.
