# Exercise 6.8 — Parimutuel lottery growth

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 178.

## Solution

Assume every population fraction $`f_i`$ is positive and that the bettor is negligible relative to the pool.

## (a)
A dollar on ticket $`i`$ pays $`1/f_i`$ if that number is selected. Since the winning number is uniform, proportional betting prescribes $`b_i^*=1/8`$ for all eight tickets. The crowd fractions affect payouts, but not the optimal fractions when all wealth is allocated.

## (b)
The optimal doubling rate is
```math
W^*=\frac18\sum_{i=1}^8\log_2\frac1{8f_i}
=D(U_8\|f)\ge0.
```
Because $`\sum_i f_i=1`$, these are fair bookmaking odds, so allowing cash does not improve the optimum.

## (c)
The fractions in the exercise consist of two $`1/8`$ entries, two $`1/4`$ entries, and four $`1/16`$ entries. Thus $`W^*=(0-2+4)/8=1/4`$ bit per day. Solving the typical-growth approximation $`2^{n/4}=10^6`$ gives $`n=4\log_2 10^6\approx79.73`$: about 80 days. This is not an exact expected hitting time or a guarantee. The random log wealth has increments $`-1,0,1`$ with probabilities $`1/4,1/4,1/2`$. For a literal first-passage expectation to $`10^6`$, the integer log wealth must reach 20; the biased lazy walk has mean hitting time $`20/(1/4)=80`$ days. One can prove this by removing zero steps, using the biased nearest-neighbor hitting-time mean $`20/(2/3-1/3)=60`$ nonzero steps, then multiplying by $`4/3`$. The deterministic calculations are reproduced in the chapter script.
