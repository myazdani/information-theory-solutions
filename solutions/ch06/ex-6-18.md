# Exercise 6.18 — Relative growth in the super St. Petersburg game

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 182.

## Solution

Let $`X=2^{2^K}`$ with $`\Pr(K=k)=2^{-k}`$, and let $`b`$ be the invested fraction. For $`0<b<1`$, the ratio to the half-invested reference is
```math
R_b(X)=\frac{2[c(1-b)+bX]}{c+X}.
```
It lies between $`2\min(b,1-b)`$ and $`2\max(b,1-b)`$, so $`F(b)=\mathbb E\ln R_b(X)`$ is finite despite the infinite absolute log-growth rates. At $`b=1`$ it is finite as well, since $`X\ge4`$; at $`b=0`$ it equals negative infinity.

On $`0<b\le1`$, differentiation is legitimate on compact subintervals away from zero, and
```math
F'(b)=\mathbb E\frac{X-c}{c(1-b)+bX},\qquad
F''(b)=-\mathbb E\frac{(X-c)^2}{[c(1-b)+bX]^2}\lt 0.
```
The right derivative at zero is positive infinite. Define
```math
c_0=\left(\mathbb E\frac1X\right)^{-1}
=\left(\sum_{k\ge1}2^{-k-2^k}\right)^{-1}.
```
At one, $`F'(1)=1-c/c_0`$. Therefore the unique maximizer is $`b^*=1`$ when $`c\le c_0`$; when $`c>c_0`$, it is the unique interior root
```math
\mathbb E\frac{X-c}{c(1-b^*)+b^*X}=0.
```
Strict concavity proves uniqueness, including the threshold. Existence follows from the endpoint derivative signs (and continuity away from zero).

This is the uniquely best portfolio in relative long-run log growth. For any other fixed positive fraction $`a`$, the log wealth ratio has bounded iid increments, so its per-round value converges almost surely to $`F(b^*)-F(a)>0`$. Thus the optimal portfolio exponentially outgrows every different fixed portfolio, although each positive investment already has superexponential absolute growth. The zero-investment portfolio stays constant and is also dominated.
