# Exercise 6.16 — Short selling in a negative horse race

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 181.

## Solution

Take $`m\ge2`$. Put $`t_i=1-b_i`$. Positive wealth on positive-probability outcomes requires $`t_i>0`$ there, and the budget constraint becomes $`\sum_i t_i=m-1`$. Require nonnegative wealth in all outcomes; negative wealth would make the logarithm undefined.

### (a)
Let $`q_i=t_i/(m-1)`$. Then
```math
\sum_i p_i\ln(1-b_i)
=\ln(m-1)+\sum_i p_i\ln q_i
=\ln(m-1)-H_{\mathrm{nat}}(p)-D_{\mathrm{nat}}(p\|q).
```
The optimum is $`q=p`$, namely
```math
b_i^*=1-(m-1)p_i.
```
These allocations sum to 1 and can be negative, as allowed. If all $`p_i>0`$ they produce strictly positive wealth. At a zero-probability outcome the formal optimal wealth is zero; if strict positivity is demanded even there, the same value is a supremum approached by a small perturbation.

### (b)
The optimal natural-log growth rate is
```math
W^*=\ln(m-1)+\sum_i p_i\ln p_i=\ln(m-1)-H_{\mathrm{nat}}(p).
```
The permission to use negative bets does not mean that negative terminal wealth is admissible.
