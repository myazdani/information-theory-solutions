# Exercise 6.11 — A randomized envelope rule

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 179.

## Solution

Assume $`b>0`$, and regard envelope 1 as the initially observed envelope, with envelope 2 the other one. Their larger-amount index $`J`$ is uniform on $`\{1,2\}`$.

### (a)
Both marginal amounts take $`b`$ and $`2b`$ with probability $`1/2`$, so $`\mathbb EX=\mathbb EY=3b/2`$.

### (b)
The ratio is 2 or $`1/2`$, equiprobably, hence $`\mathbb E(Y/X)=5/4`$. In contrast $`\mathbb E[X(Y/X)]=\mathbb EY=3b/2\ne(3b/2)(5/4)`$. The factorization fails because $`X`$ and $`Y/X`$ are dependent.

### (c)
For the selected index $`J'`$,
```math
\Pr(J'=1\mid J=1)=1-p(2b),\qquad
\Pr(J'=1\mid J=2)=1-p(b).
```
The logistic rule $`p(x)=1/(1+e^{2x})`$ is strictly decreasing, so these conditional probabilities differ. Thus $`J`$ and $`J'`$ are not independent and $`I(J;J')>0`$. Equivalently, their joint law has positive relative entropy from the product of its marginals.

### (d)
Conditioning on the initially seen amount gives
```math
\mathbb EZ=\tfrac12[b+bp(b)]+\tfrac12[2b-bp(2b)]
=\tfrac32b+\tfrac b2[p(b)-p(2b)]>\mathbb EX.
```
Strict improvement requires $`p(b)>p(2b)`$. A merely nonincreasing rule guarantees only weak improvement; a constant switching probability is an equality case. This qualifies the exercise's informal use of “monotonic decreasing.”
