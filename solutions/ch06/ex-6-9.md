# Exercise 6.9 — Comparing two odds schedules

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 178.

## Solution

For strictly positive odds and all wealth allocated, the optimal allocation is $`b=p`$ for either schedule. Thus
```math
W^*(o)=\sum_i p_i\log_2 o_i-H(p),
\qquad
W^*(o)-W^*(o')=\sum_i p_i\log_2\frac{o_i}{o_i'}.
```
The first schedule is better exactly when this last quantity is positive, or equivalently when $`\prod_i(o_i/o_i')^{p_i}>1`$. Equality means identical optimal doubling rates. Only odds on positive-probability outcomes matter. If the bettor may hold cash under subfair odds, this simple criterion does not generally apply; the question's fully invested horse-race convention is essential.
