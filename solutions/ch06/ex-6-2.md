# Exercise 6.2 — Cash and subfair horse-race odds

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 176–177.

## Solution

Assume positive finite odds and $`p_i>0`$; zero-probability horses may be removed (or handled by the same optimality conditions). Write $`r_i=1/o_i`$ and $`R=\sum_i r_i`$.

## (a)
If $`R<1`$, replace any cash amount $`b_0>0`$ by bets $`b_0r_i/R`$. In every outcome this component returns $`b_0/R>b_0`$, so cash cannot be optimal. With no cash,
```math
W(b)=\sum_i p_i\log_2(p_i o_i)-D(p\|b).
```
Thus $`b_0^*=0`$ and $`b_i^*=p_i`$.

## (b)
Let $`s_i=b_0+o_i b_i`$. Concavity makes the Karush–Kuhn–Tucker conditions sufficient. Homogeneity gives a Lagrange multiplier equal to 1 when natural logarithms are used: the gradient dotted with the allocation equals $`\sum_i p_i=1`$. For positive bets,
```math
p_i o_i/s_i=1,
\qquad b_i=p_i-b_0r_i.
```
For a zero bet, $`p_i o_i/b_0\le1`$. Hence, when $`b_0>0`$,
```math
b_i^*=(p_i-b_0^*r_i)_+,
\qquad b_0^*+\sum_i(p_i-b_0^*r_i)_+=1.
```
To choose the nonzero solution, set $`A=\{i:p_i o_i>b_0^*\}`$. Then
```math
b_0^*=\frac{1-\sum_{i\in A}p_i}{1-\sum_{i\in A}r_i},
```
and verify its active-set inequalities. Equivalently, sort $`p_i o_i`$ and test the finitely many candidate sets. With $`R>1`$ and all $`p_i>0`$, the all-invested vector fails the cash derivative test, so a solution with positive cash exists. If every $`p_i o_i\le1`$, all cash is optimal. Boundary equalities may cause nonunique allocations with identical outcome wealth. This is the water-filling characterization.
