# Exercise 6.4 — Optimal sequential card gambling

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 177.

## Solution

Let $`P(x^{52})`$ be the uniform distribution on the $`\binom{52}{26}`$ feasible color sequences, and let $`b(x^{52})`$ be the probability distribution induced by the sequential bets. Then
```math
\mathbb E\log_2(S_{52}/S_0)
=52+\sum_{x^{52}}P(x^{52})\log_2 b(x^{52})
=52-H(P)-D(P\|b).
```
The maximum is therefore
```math
52-\log_2\binom{52}{26}\approx3.1829035303\text{ bits}.
```
It is attained by betting at each deal in proportion to the remaining counts of red and black. This strategy induces $`b=P`$. In fact its terminal factor is identically $`2^{52}/\binom{52}{26}`$ on every feasible deck; no asymptotic argument is needed. Initial wealth merely adds $`\log_2 S_0`$ to the answer.
