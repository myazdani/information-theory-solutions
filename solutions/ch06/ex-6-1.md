# Exercise 6.1 — Proportional betting and ruin

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 176.

## Solution

All logarithms are base 2. Assume independent repetitions of the stated race.

## (a)
For any bet vector with positive mass on the support of $`p`$,
```math
W(b)=\log_2 3-H(p)-D(p\|b).
```
Here $`H(p)=3/2`$. Nonnegativity of relative entropy gives the unique maximizer $`b^*=(1/2,1/4,1/4)`$ and
```math
W^*=\log_2 3-\frac32\approx0.0849625007\quad\text{bits per race}.
```
The strong law applied to the bounded log returns gives $`n^{-1}\log_2(S_n/S_0)\to W^*`$ almost surely. Thus the precise growth statement is $`S_n=S_0 2^{n(W^*+o(1))}`$ almost surely, rather than convergence of the ratio to $`S_0 2^{nW^*}`$.

## (b)
The first loss of horse 1 makes wealth zero forever. The probability of avoiding this through race $`n`$ is $`(1/2)^n\to0`$. Consequently ruin occurs in finite time almost surely.
