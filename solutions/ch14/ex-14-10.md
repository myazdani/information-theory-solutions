# Exercise 14.10: Encoding a symmetric silhouette and one damaged cell

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 505.

## Solution

### (a)
Under the silhouette interpretation in the figure, each row has at most one centered horizontal foreground segment. Given $`m`$, one endpoint determines the other by mirror symmetry. There are at most $`m+1`$ row choices, including an empty row; parity-aware encoding reduces this to at most $`\lceil m/2\rceil+1`$. Thus an upper bound is

```math
K(x\mid m)\le m\log_2(\lceil m/2\rceil+1)+O(1)
\le m\log_2(m+1)+O(1).
```

Without $`m`$ as side information, add $`K(m)+O(1)`$. If multiple disjoint segments per row are allowed, the single-endpoint description is no longer valid; mirror symmetry alone instead gives the general bound $`m\lceil m/2\rceil+O(1)`$ conditional bits.

### (b)
Describe the undamaged image, then the row and column of the flipped cell. This gives

```math
K(y\mid m)\le K(x\mid m)+2\lceil\log_2m\rceil+O(1).
```

The same operation reverses the change, so
$`|K(y\mid m)-K(x\mid m)|\le2\log_2m+O(1)`$.
In particular, the silhouette upper bound grows by at most $`2\log_2m+O(1)`$. One cannot assert that every defect increases complexity by that exact amount, since its location may itself be simple.
