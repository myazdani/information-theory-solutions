# Exercise 14.8: Complexity under Boolean set operations

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 505.

## Solution

All complexities are conditional on $`n`$ and all additive constants are independent of the particular images.

### (a)
A fixed program complements every pixel, and complementation is its own inverse. Therefore

```math
K(A^c\mid n)=K(A\mid n)+O(1).
```

### (b)
Run shortest self-delimiting descriptions of $`A,B`$ and take their pixelwise OR. This yields

```math
0\le K(A\cup B\mid n)
\le\min\{K(A\mid n)+K(B\mid n)+O(1),\ n^2+O(1)\}.
```

No lower bound growing with either input complexity is generally possible. Taking $`B=A^c`$ makes both inputs potentially incompressible while their union is the full grid, which has complexity $`O(1)`$ given $`n`$. Taking $`B`$ to be the full grid shows that even one complex and one simple input can have a simple union.

### (c)
The same program construction with pixelwise AND gives the identical upper bound for $`K(A\cap B\mid n)`$. There is likewise no generally growing lower bound: complements have empty intersection, and intersecting any complex image with the empty set gives an empty result. These examples explain why subtraction-style bounds such as $`|K(A)-K(B)|`$ are invalid for union or intersection.
