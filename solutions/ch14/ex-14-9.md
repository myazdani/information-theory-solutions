# Exercise 14.9: Predicting the next digit of a computable sequence

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 505.

## Solution

Under the universal random-program model, after a sufficiently long matching prefix one expects the next bit to agree with the computable expansion with probability close to 1. This is a model-based prediction, not a numeric probability determined solely by the stated prefix length.

For a precise asymptotic argument, let $`s`$ be the binary expansion of $`1/\sqrt2`$ and let $`M(s_1^n)`$ be the probability that the random program prints that prefix. A fixed algorithm prints all of $`s`$, so $`M(s_1^n)\ge c>0`$ for every $`n`$. Define

```math
q_n=\frac{M(s_1^{n+1})}{M(s_1^n)}.
```

Each $`q_n\le1`$ is the conditional probability of printing the correct next bit, treating stopping before that bit as failure. The products telescope:

```math
\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}q_j=\frac{M(s_1^n)}{M(\varnothing)}\ge c.
```

Therefore $`\sum_j-\ln q_j<\infty`$ and, since $`1-q_j\le-\ln q_j`$, $`q_n\to1`$. Conditioning additionally on a next bit being printed can only increase the correct-bit probability. The convergence need not have a computable rate, and constants depend on the universal machine; a universal finite-$`n`$ formula such as $`1-1/n`$ is not justified.
