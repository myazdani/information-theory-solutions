# Exercise 14.7: Incompressibility forces balanced ternary frequencies

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 504.

## Solution

Let $`n_j`$ count symbol $`j`$ and let $`q_j=n_j/n`$. Given $`n`$, specify two counts using $`O(\log n)`$ ternary symbols; the third is determined. Then specify the string's index among the
$`n!/(n_0!n_1!n_2!)`$ strings with those counts. Thus

```math
K(x^n\mid n)\le
\log_3\frac{n!}{n_0!n_1!n_2!}+O(\log n)
\le nH_3(q)+O(\log n),
```

where $`H_3(q)=-\sum_jq_j\log_3q_j`$. Stirling's formula supplies the first-order equality $`\log_3(n!/\prod n_j!)=nH_3(q)+O(\log n)`$, including zero-count conventions.

The function $`H_3`$ has its unique maximum 1 at $`q=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`$. If any frequency stays at least a fixed $`\epsilon`$ away from $`1/3`$, continuity on the compact simplex gives $`H_3(q)\le1-c_\epsilon`$ for some $`c_\epsilon>0`$. The resulting description saves a linear number of symbols, contradicting $`K(x^n\mid n)=n-o(n)`$. Consequently all three empirical frequencies tend to $`1/3`$. The quantitative identity $`1-H_3(q)=D_2(q\Vert\mathrm{unif})/\log_2 3`$ (where $`D_2`$ is relative entropy in bits) also converts a given complexity deficiency into a frequency-deviation bound.
