# Exercise 14.2: Integer descriptions and a simple sum of complex terms

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 503.

## Solution

### (a)
For $`n\ge2`$, let $`L=\lfloor\log_2n\rfloor+1`$. Describe $`L`$ self-delimited by doubling each bit of its binary expansion and adding a fixed delimiter, then append the $`L`$ bits of $`n`$. A fixed interpreter recovers $`n`$. The description length is

```math
L+2\lfloor\log_2L\rfloor+O(1)
\le\log_2n+2\log_2\log_2n+O(1).
```

Finitely many small $`n`$ can be absorbed into the constant.

### (b)
Concatenate shortest self-delimiting programs for the two integers, run both, and add their outputs. Addition requires a fixed interpreter independent of the values, giving
$`K(n_1+n_2)\le K(n_1)+K(n_2)+O(1)`$.

### (c)
Choose an $`m`$-bit integer $`n_1`$ with $`K(n_1\mid m)\ge m-O(1)`$, and put $`n_2=2^m-n_1`$. Given $`m`$, either summand computes the other, so both have conditional complexity $`m-O(1)`$ and unconditional complexity at least that much up to a constant. Their sum is $`2^m`$, described by a program for $`m`$ followed by a fixed exponentiation routine; its complexity is at most $`K(m)+O(1)=O(\log m)`$. Thus two individually complex, strongly dependent integers can have a very simple sum.
