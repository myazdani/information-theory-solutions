# Exercise 14.1: Concatenating two self-delimiting descriptions

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 503.

## Solution

Use the chapter's self-delimiting universal programming convention. Let $`p_x,p_y`$ be shortest programs for $`x,y`$. A fixed interpreter reads and runs $`p_x`$, then reads and runs $`p_y`$, and prints the ordered pair using a fixed pair representation. Because the programs are self-delimiting, their boundary is recognized without transmitting their lengths. The combined description consists of a fixed interpreter prefix followed by $`p_xp_y`$. Hence

```math
K(x,y)\le |p_x|+|p_y|+c=K(x)+K(y)+c,
```

where $`c`$ depends on the universal machine and pair convention, not on $`x,y`$. For plain, non-self-delimiting complexity one generally needs additional logarithmic boundary overhead; the constant-overhead statement uses the self-delimiting convention.
