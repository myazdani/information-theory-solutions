# Exercise 14.3: First-order description lengths for simple images

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 503.

## Solution

Condition on the grid size $`n`$ and take the shapes to be axis-aligned as in the source figures. Statements such as $`\log_2n`$ below are maximal or typical first-order complexities; a specially located image may be much simpler.

### (a)
A full horizontal line is specified by its row number, so
$`K(x\mid n)\le\lceil\log_2n\rceil+O(1)`$.
There are $`n`$ choices, and a counting argument gives examples with complexity $`\log_2n-O(1)`$.

### (b)
An axis-aligned square is described by two coordinates and its side length, giving $`K(x\mid n)\le3\log_2n+O(1)`$. There are
$`\sum_{s=1}^n(n-s+1)^2=\Theta(n^3)`$ possible squares. Thus the maximal complexity is $`3\log_2n+O(1)`$. The same first-order count holds for either filled squares or square outlines.

### (c)
Each of the two full lines needs an orientation bit and one coordinate. Thus $`K(x\mid n)\le2\log_2n+O(1)`$. The family has $`\Theta(n^2)`$ distinct unions, including the $`n^2`$ horizontal/vertical crosses, so its maximal complexity is $`2\log_2n+O(1)`$.

These lower bounds mean that for a finite family of $`N`$ distinct images, fewer than $`2^k`$ can have complexity below $`k`$; most images cannot beat $`\log_2N`$ by more than a fixed allowance.
