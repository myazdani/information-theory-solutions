# Exercise 14.11: Counting unions of grid-aligned rectangles

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 505.

## Solution

Condition on $`n`$. For each finite computably enumerable family of $`N_n`$ images, an index gives maximum complexity at most $`\log_2N_n+O(1)`$, while program counting gives a member of complexity at least $`\log_2N_n-O(1)`$. Thus counting distinct admissible images settles the maximal first-order complexity.

### (a)
Two rectangles need four coordinates each, giving an $`8\log_2n+O(1)`$ upper bound. Restricting the rectangles to two separated fixed-size fractions of the grid gives $`\Theta(n^8)`$ distinct identifiable unions. Hence the maximum is $`8\log_2n+O(1)`$.

### (b)
For rectangles meeting at a single common corner, specify the shared corner (two coordinates), their four side lengths, and their relative orientation (constant information). The bound is $`6\log_2n+O(1)`$. Choosing all six parameters in separated intervals of length proportional to $`n`$ gives $`\Theta(n^6)`$ distinct unions, so the bound is sharp to first order.

### (c)
For congruent rectangles, give the shared width and height and the two locations. There are $`\Theta(n^6)`$ distinguishable separated configurations, so the maximum is again $`6\log_2n+O(1)`$. Allowing rotations changes only a constant number of choices.

### (d)
Equal **integer grid areas** need a little care: a naive seven-coordinate description is an upper bound but is not first-order sharp. Write widths and heights as $`w_1h_1=w_2h_2`$. Reducing the width ratio gives the unique parameterization

```math
w_1=ga,\quad w_2=gb,\quad h_1=hb,\quad h_2=ha,
\qquad\gcd(a,b)=1.
```

For $`r=\max(a,b)`$ there are at most $`2r`$ pairs $`(a,b)`$, and at most $`(n/r)^2`$ choices for $`g,h`$. Thus the number of size quadruples is at most
$`2n^2\sum_{r=1}^n1/r=O(n^2\log n)`$. Including both positions gives $`O(n^6\log n)`$ unions. Congruent separated rectangles already give $`\Omega(n^6)`$ distinct unions. Consequently

```math
\max K(A\cup B\mid n)=6\log_2n+O(\log\log n)
```

to the requested first order. This arithmetic restriction is specific to an exact discrete grid. The experiment checks the reduced-ratio count against direct equal-product enumeration for small grids.

### (e)
The minimum is $`O(1)`$: choose a fixed simple configuration, such as the full grid represented by two identical full-grid rectangles. If distinct rectangles are required, two predetermined halves of the grid have the same constant-description property.

### (f)
There are $`2^{n^2}`$ unrestricted binary images. Literal printing gives $`n^2+O(1)`$ bits, and counting gives an incompressible image with complexity at least $`n^2-O(1)`$. Thus the maximal complexity is $`n^2+O(1)`$.
