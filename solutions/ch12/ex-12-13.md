# Exercise 12.13: The golden-mean entropy rate

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 423.

## Solution

Let $`\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2`$. The maximum entropy rate is $`\log_2\varphi`$. A maximizing stationary Markov chain has transitions

```math
P=\begin{pmatrix}
0&1\\
\varphi^{-2}&\varphi^{-1}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\pi=\left(\frac1{\varphi^2+1},\frac{\varphi^2}{\varphi^2+1}\right).
```

Here rows and columns are ordered $`0,1`$, so the forbidden transition has probability zero. To prove the upper bound without assuming the maximizing process is Markov, let $`A_n`$ be the number of binary length-$`n`$ strings avoiding 00. Splitting by the final symbol gives $`A_n=A_{n-1}+A_{n-2}`$, with $`A_0=1,A_1=2`$. Thus $`A_n=F_{n+2}`$ and
$`H(X_1^n)\le\log_2 A_n=n\log_2\varphi+O(1)`$.

For the chain above, only visits to state 1 contribute conditional entropy:

```math
\overline H=\pi_1h_2(\varphi^{-2})
=\frac{\varphi^2}{\varphi^2+1}
(2\varphi^{-2}+\varphi^{-1})\log_2\varphi
=\log_2\varphi.
```

This attains the universal upper bound.
