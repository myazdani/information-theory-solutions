# Exercise 12.22: The least mean at prescribed positive-support entropy

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 425.

## Solution

Take the prescribed entropy $`h`$ in bits.

### (a)
If $`X>0`$ has finite mean $`m`$, compare its density with the exponential density $`g_m(x)=m^{-1}e^{-x/m}`$. In nats,

```math
0\le D(f\Vert g_m)=-h(f)\ln2+\ln m+1.
```

Hence $`m\ge 2^h/e`$. A density with infinite mean cannot improve the minimum. Equality is attained by the exponential density with mean $`2^h/e`$, whose entropy is $`\log_2(em)=h`$. Therefore the minimum is exactly $`2^h/e`$, and the minimizing density is unique almost everywhere.

### (b)
Put $`U=X-a>0`$. Translation preserves differential entropy, and $`EX=a+EU`$. The answer is

```math
\min EX=a+\frac{2^h}{e},\qquad
f_*(x)=\frac{e}{2^h}\exp\left[-\frac{e(x-a)}{2^h}\right]1_{\{x>a\}}.
```

If $`h`$ is measured in nats instead, replace $`2^h`$ by $`e^h`$ in both parts.
