# Exercise 12.21: A lag-two Gaussian autoregression

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 425.

## Solution

### (a)
For $`|\alpha|<1`$, a maximizing process is

```math
X_i=\alpha X_{i-2}+Z_i,\qquad
Z_i\stackrel{\rm iid}{\sim}N(0,1-\alpha^2).
```

Use independent stationary Gaussian initial states for the even and odd subsequences. Each subsequence is an AR(1) chain with variance 1 and coefficient $`\alpha`$; the two subsequences are independent. Thus the specified lag-two moment is exactly $`\alpha`$. The two separate parity chains are important; using an ordinary AR(1) with coefficient $`\alpha`$ would give lag-two moment $`\alpha^2`$ instead.

### (b)
Every admissible process satisfies
$`E(X_i-\alpha X_{i-2})^2=1-\alpha^2`$. Thus, for $`i\ge3`$,

```math
h(X_i\mid X_1^{i-1})
\le h(X_i-\alpha X_{i-2})
\le\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e(1-\alpha^2)).
```

The constructed process has independent Gaussian innovations and attains the bound. Its maximum entropy rate is therefore the final expression above.

### (c)
For this stationary maximum-entropy construction, adjacent samples belong to independent, centered parity chains, so $`EX_iX_{i+1}=0`$. Its full covariance is $`R_{2k}=\alpha^{|k|}`$ and $`R_{2k+1}=0`$.

Cauchy–Schwarz excludes $`|\alpha|>1`$. At $`\alpha=\pm1`$, $`E(X_i-\alpha X_{i-2})^2=0`$, so the recursion is deterministic and blocks of length at least three have no full-dimensional density. The differential-entropy-rate formula tends to $`-\infty`$; there is no nonsingular density optimizer at these endpoints. Nonstationary initial effects can leave transient adjacent correlations without changing an asymptotic rate, so the value in (c) specifies the stationary maximizing process rather than claiming uniqueness among all possible nonstationary initializations.
