# Exercise 12.14: Even-moment entropy constraints and nonattainment

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 423.

## Solution

Use nats. A density with the two moments requires $`a>0`$ and $`b>a^2`$; equality in $`E(X^8)^2\ge(EX^8)^2`$ would force $`|X|`$ to be constant.

### (a)
When $`a^2<b<9a^2`$, the unique maximum-entropy density has the parametric form

```math
f_*(x)=Z(\lambda,\mu)^{-1}e^{\lambda x^8+\mu x^{16}},\qquad
Z(\lambda,\mu)=\int_{\mathbb R}e^{\lambda x^8+\mu x^{16}}dx,
\qquad \mu<0,
```

with $`\partial_\lambda\ln Z=a`$ and $`\partial_\mu\ln Z=b`$. Normalization also permits the boundary $`\mu=0,\lambda<0`$. For a candidate meeting the moments, $`h(f_*)-h(f)=D(f\Vert f_*)\ge0`$ proves optimality.

The boundary $`\mu=0`$ gives $`f_a(x)=Z_a^{-1}e^{-x^8/(8a)}`$. Under this law $`X^8`$ is gamma with shape $`1/8`$ and scale $`8a`$, so $`EX^8=a`$ and $`EX^{16}=9a^2`$. To check the full range below this boundary, set $`\mu=-c<0`$ and adjust $`\lambda`$ to preserve $`EX^8=a`$. Writing $`Y=X^8`$,

```math
\frac{d}{dc}E(Y^2)
=-\operatorname{Var}(Y^2)+
\frac{\operatorname{Cov}(Y,Y^2)^2}{\operatorname{Var}(Y)}<0.
```

The second moment tends to $`9a^2`$ as $`c\downarrow0`$ and to $`a^2`$ as $`c\to\infty`$ (the density concentrates at $`Y=a`$). Continuity supplies all intermediate values.

For $`b>9a^2`$, no maximizing density exists. The entropy supremum is $`h(f_a)`$, because the eighth-moment constraint alone has that unique maximizer, which has the wrong sixteenth moment. The supremum is approached by mixing a rescaled $`f_a`$ with a vanishing amount of mass in narrow intervals near $`\pm t`$, as $`t\to\infty`$. More explicitly, let this outlier density have moments $`u_t\to\infty`$, $`v_t\sim u_t^2`$, and fixed entropy. Choose $`\epsilon_t`$ so that the core has eighth moment $`a_t=(a-\epsilon_tu_t)/(1-\epsilon_t)`$ and
$`9(1-\epsilon_t)a_t^2+\epsilon_tv_t=b`$. Continuity gives a root with $`\epsilon_t=O(u_t^{-2})`$, hence $`a_t\to a`$. Concavity of entropy gives convergence up to the upper bound $`h(f_a)`$.

### (b)
With only the sum constrained, the unique optimizer is

```math
\widetilde f(x)=\widetilde Z(t)^{-1}e^{-t(x^8+x^{16})},\qquad t>0,
\qquad -\frac{d}{dt}\ln\widetilde Z(t)=a+b.
```

For every $`a+b>0`$ there is a unique $`t`$: the moment decreases continuously from infinity to zero. The same divergence argument proves optimality.

### (c)
Every density feasible in (a) is feasible in (b), so the entropy in (b) is at least the supremum in (a). Equality occurs exactly when the optimizer in (b) has the separately required moments; in the attained exponential family this is the condition $`\lambda=\mu=-t`$. Otherwise the inequality is strict. For $`b>9a^2`$, the (b) optimizer has eighth moment greater than $`a`$: the ratio $`EX^{16}/(EX^8)^2<9`$ and its fixed sum exceeds $`a+9a^2`$. Scaling $`f_a`$ upward to this sum gives entropy strictly above $`h(f_a)`$, so strictness also holds in that nonattainment regime.
