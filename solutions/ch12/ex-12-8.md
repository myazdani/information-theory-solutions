# Exercise 12.8: Characteristic-function constraints and exponential tilts

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 422.

## Solution

For finite $`a>0`$ and feasible interior constraint values, define the normalizer

```math
Z(\lambda,\mu)=\int_0^a\exp\{\lambda\cos(u_0x)+\mu\sin(u_0x)\}\,dx.
```

### (a)
The maximizing density is $`f_*(x)=e^{\lambda\cos(u_0x)}/Z(\lambda,0)`$, with $`\partial_\lambda\ln Z(\lambda,0)=\alpha`$.

### (b)
It is $`f_*(x)=e^{\mu\sin(u_0x)}/Z(0,\mu)`$, where $`\partial_\mu\ln Z(0,\mu)=\beta`$.

### (c)
Writing the required characteristic function as $`\alpha+i\beta`$, the answer is

```math
f_*(x)=Z(\lambda,\mu)^{-1}
 e^{\lambda\cos(u_0x)+\mu\sin(u_0x)},
\quad \nabla\ln Z=(\alpha,\beta).
```

In all three cases $`\ln f_*`$ is a linear combination of the constrained functions, so the divergence identity $`h(f_*)-h(f)=D(f\Vert f_*)\ge0`$ proves optimality. Constraint values must belong to the convex hull of the curve of sufficient statistics. Boundary values may force singular laws rather than a density. If $`u_0=0`$, the only feasible characteristic value is 1, and the unconstrained uniform density on $`[0,a]`$ maximizes entropy.

### (d)
On the half-line, every finite exponential tilt above is bounded above and bounded away from zero, so its normalizer diverges. This reflects a genuine escape of entropy. If $`u_0\ne0`$, take a feasible density for the phase modulo $`T=2\pi/|u_0|`$ on $`[0,T)`$ and place equal copies in $`N`$ consecutive periods. The sine and cosine expectations remain unchanged, while entropy increases by $`\ln N`$. Hence the finite-support formula has no normalizable half-line counterpart, and entropy is unbounded for feasible absolutely continuous phase constraints. For $`u_0=0`$, uniform densities on expanding intervals give the same unboundedness.
