# Exercise 12.20: The maximum entropy of a sum

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 424.

## Solution

Use base-two differential entropy and assume at least one power is positive.

### (a)
Independence gives

```math
\operatorname{Var}(Y)=\operatorname{Var}(X_1)+\operatorname{Var}(X_2)
\le P_1+P_2.
```

The Gaussian maximum-entropy inequality therefore bounds the entropy by
$`\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e(P_1+P_2))`$. Independent centered Gaussian summands with variances $`P_1,P_2`$ attain this value.

### (b)
For dependent summands, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

```math
\operatorname{Var}(Y)\le EY^2
=P_1+P_2+2EX_1X_2\le(\sqrt{P_1}+\sqrt{P_2})^2.
```

The answer is consequently
$`\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e(\sqrt{P_1}+\sqrt{P_2})^2)`$, attained by $`X_i=\sqrt{P_i}Z`$ with a shared standard Gaussian $`Z`$. The joint law may be singular although $`Y`$ has a density. If a full-dimensional joint density is required, take correlations tending to 1 to attain the supremum. If both powers are zero, the sum is deterministic and has no differential entropy as a Lebesgue density.
