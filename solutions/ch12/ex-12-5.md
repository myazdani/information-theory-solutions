# Exercise 12.5: The Markov extension of adjacent marginals

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 422.

## Solution

Assume the specified adjacent densities are consistent on shared one-variable marginals. Let $`f_i`$ be these marginals, and define conditional kernels on their positive-density support by
$`q_i(x_{i+1}\mid x_i)=f_{i,i+1}(x_i,x_{i+1})/f_i(x_i)`$.
Define kernels arbitrarily on null sets. Then

```math
f_*(x_1,\ldots,x_n)=f_1(x_1)\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}q_i(x_{i+1}\mid x_i)
=\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}f_{i,i+1}(x_i,x_{i+1})}
{\prod_{i=2}^{n-1}f_i(x_i)}.
```

Successive integration shows that this is normalized and has every prescribed adjacent marginal. For any joint density with those marginals, the chain rule gives

```math
h(X_1^n)=h(X_1)+\sum_{i=2}^n h(X_i\mid X_1^{i-1})
\le h(X_1)+\sum_{i=2}^n h(X_i\mid X_{i-1}).
```

The right side is fixed by the constraints, and the Markov density attains it. Equality requires $`I(X_i;X_1^{i-2}\mid X_{i-1})=0`$ for every $`i`$, precisely the first-order Markov property. For differential entropies the assertion presupposes well-defined finite terms; equivalently, whenever cross-entropies are defined, the entropy gap is $`D(f\Vert f_*)`$.
