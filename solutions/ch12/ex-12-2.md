# Exercise 12.2: An information projection onto linear constraints

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 421.

## Solution

Use nats; changing the logarithm base rescales the multipliers. Restrict to the support of $`Q`$, since putting positive mass elsewhere gives infinite divergence.

### (a)
For finitely many constraints, differentiating

```math
\sum_x P(x)\ln\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}
-\sum_i\lambda_i\left(\sum_xP(x)g_i(x)-\alpha_i\right)
-\nu\left(\sum_xP(x)-1\right)
```

with respect to $`P(x)`$ yields
$`\ln(P(x)/Q(x))+1-\sum_i\lambda_i g_i(x)-\nu=0`$.
Consequently

```math
P_*(x)=Q(x)\exp\left(\lambda_0+\sum_i\lambda_i g_i(x)\right),
\qquad
\lambda_0=-\ln\sum_xQ(x)e^{\sum_i\lambda_i g_i(x)}.
```

The parameters must make the normalizer finite and meet all constraints. For countably many constraints the same is the formal candidate; the series and expectations must converge so that exchanging summation and expectation is justified, for example by absolute integrability. Existence is not automatic from the formal multiplier calculation.

### (b)
For any feasible $`P`$ and a candidate satisfying those convergence conditions,

```math
D(P\Vert Q)-D(P\Vert P_*)
=\sum_xP(x)\ln\frac{P_*(x)}{Q(x)}
=\lambda_0+\sum_i\lambda_i\alpha_i
=D(P_*\Vert Q).
```

Thus $`D(P\Vert Q)=D(P\Vert P_*)+D(P_*\Vert Q)`$. Nonnegativity of divergence proves global optimality, and equality forces $`P=P_*`$. Boundary optima can be limits of this exponential family; the claim here concerns an actual normalizable candidate meeting the constraints.
