# Exercise 12.18: Kinetic and gravitational energy at maximum entropy

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 424.

## Solution

Take the physical state space to be $`z\ge0`$ and $`v\in\mathbb R^3`$, with $`m,g,E_0>0`$. A lower bound on height is necessary: on the whole real line the asserted equilibrium is not normalizable. The entropy maximizer under mean energy has Gibbs density

```math
f_*(z,v)=\frac1{Z(\beta)}
\exp\{-\beta(\tfrac12m\|v\|^2+mgz)\},\qquad\beta>0,
\quad Z(\beta)=\frac1{\beta mg}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\beta m}\right)^{3/2}.
```

It factors into an exponential height with rate $`\beta mg`$ and three independent centered Gaussian velocities with variance $`1/(\beta m)`$. Consequently

```math
E[mgZ]=1/\beta,\qquad
E[\tfrac12m\|V\|^2]=3/(2\beta),\qquad
E_0=5/(2\beta).
```

Thus $`\beta=5/(2E_0)`$, the mean kinetic energy is $`3E_0/5`$, and the mean potential energy is $`2E_0/5`$. The relative shares do not depend on $`g`$; instead the mean height changes inversely with $`g`$. Since $`\ln f_*`$ is affine in energy, every feasible density satisfies $`h(f_*)-h(f)=D(f\Vert f_*)\ge0`$, proving that this candidate is indeed the unique maximizer. At $`g=0`$ the height is unconstrained and entropy is unbounded, so the positive-field assumption matters.
