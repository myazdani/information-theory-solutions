# Exercise 12.10: Gaussian sums with independent or dependent summands

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 423.

## Solution

Assume $`P_1,P_2\ge0`$ and use bits for differential entropy.

### (a)
For independent summands the optimizer for $`Y`$ is
$`N(0,P_1+P_2)`$, achieved by independent centered Gaussian $`X_i\sim N(0,P_i)`$. Its entropy is $`\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e(P_1+P_2))`$.

### (b)
Without independence the optimizer for $`Y`$ is
$`N(0,(\sqrt{P_1}+\sqrt{P_2})^2)`$, attained by $`X_i=\sqrt{P_i}Z`$ for one $`Z\sim N(0,1)`$. Indeed Cauchy–Schwarz yields

```math
\operatorname{Var}(Y)\le EY^2
=P_1+P_2+2E(X_1X_2)
\le(\sqrt{P_1}+\sqrt{P_2})^2,
```

and the Gaussian entropy bound finishes the proof. The maximizing joint law is singular when both powers are positive; the question only asks that the sum have a density. If a nonsingular joint density is additionally imposed, this value is a supremum approached as correlation tends to 1.

### (c)
For independent variables, the relevant bound is on variance, not on the raw second moment of the sum:

```math
\operatorname{Var}(X_1+X_2)=\operatorname{Var}(X_1)+\operatorname{Var}(X_2)
=P_1+P_2-(EX_1)^2-(EX_2)^2\le P_1+P_2.
```

Consequently $`h(Y)\le\tfrac12\log_2(2\pi e(P_1+P_2))`$, with the Gaussian construction in (a) attaining equality. If both powers vanish, $`Y=0`$ and there is no Lebesgue density; the formal entropy limit is $`-\infty`$.
