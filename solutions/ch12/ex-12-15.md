# Exercise 12.15: Why one Laplace-transform value does not bound entropy

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 424.

## Solution

As printed, no finite support is specified. On either $`\mathbb R`$ or the usual Laplace-transform domain $`[0,\infty)`$, there is no finite maximum-entropy density under this sole constraint. The formal multiplier expression

```math
f(x)=Z(\lambda)^{-1}\exp\{\lambda e^{-x}\}
```

cannot be normalized for any finite real $`\lambda`$, because its integrand tends to 1 as $`x\to+\infty`$.

Here is a direct unboundedness proof. Choose a uniform density $`g`$ on a fixed bounded interval with $`A=E_g e^{-X}>\alpha`$. For $`T`$ large, choose a disjoint interval $`[L,L+T]`$ to the right and let $`u_T`$ be its uniform density. Its transform value is
$`B_T=e^{-L}(1-e^{-T})/T<\alpha`$. Put

```math
w_T=\frac{\alpha-B_T}{A-B_T},\qquad
f_T=w_Tg+(1-w_T)u_T.
```

This density meets the transform constraint exactly. Its components have disjoint support, so in nats

```math
h(f_T)=h_2^{(e)}(w_T)+w_Th(g)+(1-w_T)\ln T\longrightarrow+\infty,
```

since $`w_T\to\alpha/A<1`$. On $`\mathbb R`$ this works for every $`\alpha>0`$. On $`[0,\infty)`$ the feasible density values are $`0<\alpha<1`$, and $`g`$ may be chosen sufficiently close to zero. The endpoints cannot be attained by a density.

If a bounded support was intended but omitted, the displayed exponential family is normalizable for all real multipliers, with $`\partial_\lambda\ln Z=\alpha`$. That is a different, well-posed version of the problem.
