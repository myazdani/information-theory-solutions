# Exercise 12.6: A distribution as its own entropy constraint

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 422.

## Solution

Suppose $`h(f_0)`$ is finite and put
$`\alpha_0=\int f_0\ln f_0=-h(f_0)`$. Interpret $`\ln f_0=-\infty`$ off the support of $`f_0`$; a feasible density with finite constraint therefore gives that set zero mass. For any feasible $`f`$,

```math
D(f\Vert f_0)=\int f\ln f-\int f\ln f_0
=-h(f)-\alpha_0=h(f_0)-h(f)\ge0.
```

Thus the maximum is attained uniquely, up to null sets, at $`f_0`$. This also identifies the appropriate constraint value. A finiteness qualification is essential: if the indicated integrals are undefined or infinite, one cannot subtract them and the statement need not be a meaningful finite-entropy maximization problem.
