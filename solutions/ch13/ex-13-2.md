# Exercise 13.2: A cyclic three-source information radius

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 458.

## Solution

### (a)
The uniform mixture of the three rows is $`u=(1/3,1/3,1/3)`$. Every row has entropy $`h=H(0.7,0.2,0.1)`$. The divergence decomposition gives, for every $`P`$,

```math
\frac13\sum_{\theta\in\{a,b,c\}}D(P_\theta\Vert P)
=\log_2 3-h+D(u\Vert P).
```

Hence the maximum row divergence is at least $`\log_2 3-h`$. At $`P=u`$ all three divergences attain that value. The optimal law and ideal lengths are therefore

```math
P_*=(1/3,1/3,1/3),\qquad l_i=\log_2 3,
\qquad D_*=\log_2 3-H(0.7,0.2,0.1).
```

The executed calculation gives $`H=1.1567796494470395`$, $`D_*=0.4281828512741166`$, and $`l_i=1.584962500721156`$ bits. These are ideal real lengths, not rounded prefix-code lengths.

### (b)
Every channel row has entropy $`h`$, so $`I(U;V)=H(V)-h\le\log_2 3-h`$. A uniform input produces a uniform output and meets the bound. Thus the channel capacity equals $`D_*`$.
