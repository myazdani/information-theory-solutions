# Exercise 13.8: When an LZ77 match is worth its pointer

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 459.

## Solution

### (a)
A pointer to one of $`W`$ window positions needs $`p=\lceil\log_2 W\rceil`$ bits. A match length in $`\{1,\ldots,M\}`$ needs $`l=\lceil\log_2 M\rceil`$ bits (encode length minus one). A one-bit flag distinguishes match and literal records.

### (b)
A match record costs $`1+p+l`$ bits, whereas $`k`$ separate literal records cost $`9k`$ bits, including all $`k`$ flags. Thus the shortest strictly cheaper match is

```math
k_{\min}=\left\lfloor\frac{1+\lceil\log_2W\rceil+\lceil\log_2M\rceil}{9}\right\rfloor+1.
```

If equal-cost matches are allowed, replace this by the ceiling of the fraction. If the threshold exceeds $`M`$, no available match gives a saving. A format grouping literals under one flag has a different threshold; the expression above uses the individual $`(F,C)`$ records specified in the question.

### (c)
At $`W=4096,M=256`$, the pointer and length fields are 12 and 8 bits. A match costs 21 bits, compared with 18 for two literals and 27 for three. Thus the shortest advantageous match has length **3**.
