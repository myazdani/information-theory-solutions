# Exercise 13.11: Why Tunstall coding is optimal

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 460.

## Solution

Assume the finite phrase dictionary is complete, so every source sequence can be parsed. Zero-probability letters may be removed; initially suppose all remaining letter probabilities are positive.

### (a)
Let $`L`$ be phrase length and $`V=X_1^L`$ the phrase. Since its probability is the product of its letter probabilities, a stopped-sum calculation gives

```math
H(V)=E\sum_{j=1}^L[-\log_2p(X_j)]
=H(X)\sum_{j\ge1}P(L\ge j)=H(X)EL.
```

The event $`L\ge j`$ depends only on preceding symbols and is independent of $`X_j`$. As there are $`D`$ possible phrases, $`H(V)\le\log_2D`$, so $`R\ge H(X)`$.

### (b)
For a full $`m`$-ary tree with $`D=1+k(m-1)`$ leaves, there are exactly $`k`$ internal nodes, counting the root. Expected phrase length is the sum of the probabilities of all internal nodes: each node contributes the chance that a phrase traverses it. Expanding a leaf of probability $`q`$ therefore increases $`EL`$ by exactly $`q`$ and increases the leaf count by $`m-1`$.

Node probabilities never increase down a branch. At any stage, every as-yet-unavailable descendant has probability at most that of its available leaf ancestor. Thus a largest-probability unselected node can always be chosen among the current leaves. Selecting the largest leaf at each step chooses the $`k`$ largest node weights, with ties resolved in ancestor-first order; the selected internal-node set is prefix-closed. No other $`k`$-node set can have larger total weight. This proves that the stated greedy algorithm maximizes $`EL`$ and minimizes $`R`$ for fixed $`D`$.

### (c)
Let $`p_{\min}>0`$ be the smallest letter probability, and $`q`$ the largest final leaf probability of a Tunstall tree. Each leaf was born from a node whose probability was at least $`q`$, because the probabilities expanded by the algorithm are nonincreasing. Therefore every leaf probability is at least $`p_{\min}q`$, so $`q\le1/(Dp_{\min})`$. It follows that

```math
H(V)\ge-\log_2q\ge\log_2D-\log_2(1/p_{\min}),
\qquad
0\le R-H(X)\le\frac{\log_2(1/p_{\min})}{EL}.
```

For a nondegenerate source, $`H(V)=H(X)EL`$ and the first inequality show $`EL\to\infty`$ as admissible $`D\to\infty`$. Hence $`R\to H(X)`$, and some finite $`D`$ has $`R<H(X)+1`$. For a deterministic source, repeatedly expanding its probability-one branch gives $`EL=k`$ while $`\log_2D=O(\log k)`$, so the same conclusion follows directly.
