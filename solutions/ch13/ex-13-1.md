# Exercise 13.1: Erasure-source minimax redundancy

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 457.

## Solution

Let $`q=(p_1+p_2)/2`$, with entries ordered $`(1,e,0)`$. Then
$`q=((1-\alpha)/2,\alpha,(1-\alpha)/2)`$.

### (a)
For every candidate coding distribution $`p`$, expanding the logarithms gives

```math
\frac12D(p_1\Vert p)+\frac12D(p_2\Vert p)
=\frac12D(p_1\Vert q)+\frac12D(p_2\Vert q)+D(q\Vert p)
=1-\alpha+D(q\Vert p).
```

The maximum of the two divergences is at least this average, hence at least $`1-\alpha`$. At $`p=q`$, both divergences equal $`1-\alpha`$, proving $`D_*=1-\alpha`$ bits.

### (b)
The minimizing distribution is $`p_*=q`$. The preceding identity also proves uniqueness on the relevant support: any minimizer must have $`D(q\Vert p)=0`$. At $`\alpha=1`$ the result specializes to a point mass on the erasure symbol.

### (c)
For the binary erasure channel, the erasure indicator is independent of the input, and the nonerased output reveals the input. Thus $`I(U;V)=(1-\alpha)H(U)`$, maximized by a fair input. The capacity is $`1-\alpha`$, exactly the minimax redundancy. In this example the optimal coding law is the output distribution under a capacity-achieving input.
