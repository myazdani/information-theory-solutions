# Exercise 13.9: Fast and slow LZ78 phrase growth

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed p. 459.

## Solution

### (a)
Continuing the dictionary initialized by the three given phrases produces

```text
0 | 00 | 001 | 000 | 0001 | 1 | 01 | 011 | 1 (terminal remainder)
```

The last bit is a reference to the already stored phrase 1. Exact parsing and decoding are verified by the companion experiment.

### (b)
Concatenate all nonempty binary strings in order of increasing length, using any fixed order within each length:
$`0,1,00,01,10,11,000,\ldots`$. Every string's proper prefix has appeared earlier but the string itself has not, so these are exactly the LZ78 phrases. After completing length $`k`$ there are $`c=2^{k+1}-2`$ phrases occupying

```math
n=\sum_{j=1}^k j2^j=(k-1)2^{k+1}+2.
```

Thus $`c\sim n/\log_2n`$. This is the fastest first-order growth: $`c`$ distinct binary phrases must have total length at least $`c\log_2c-O(c)`$, because there are only $`2^j`$ strings of length $`j`$.

### (c)
A constant sequence gives complete phrases $`0,00,000,\ldots`$, so $`c\sim\sqrt{2n}`$. This is the slowest first-order growth. The $`j`$th new phrase extends one of the previous phrases by one symbol and hence has length at most $`j`$. Therefore $`c`$ complete phrases occupy at most $`c(c+1)/2`$ symbols; a possible terminal remainder changes the bound by only $`O(c)`$. No parse can cover length $`n`$ with asymptotically fewer than $`\sqrt{2n}`$ phrases.
