# Exercise 5.33: When Shannon and Huffman means coincide

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 154. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Binary Huffman merges 0.1 and 0.3, giving lengths $`(1,2,2)`$ and mean 1.4 bits. The binary Shannon lengths are
```math
\left(\lceil-\log_2(0.6)\rceil,\lceil-\log_2(0.3)\rceil,\lceil-\log_2(0.1)\rceil\right)=(1,2,4),
```
with mean 1.6 bits.

## (b)
For every $`D\ge3`$, a one-digit code for all three symbols is optimal: each positive-probability symbol must have a nonempty word, so the minimum mean is 1. Shannon's mean equals 1 exactly when all three of its integer lengths are 1. This requires $`D\ge1/0.1=10`$. For $`3\le D\le9`$ the least probable symbol has Shannon length at least 2; $`D=2`$ was ruled out in (a). Thus the smallest integer alphabet size is $`D=10`$.
