# Exercise 5.44: Binary Huffman lengths for one hundred equal masses

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 157. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Since $`2^6=64<100<128=2^7`$, an optimal uniform-source tree has only depths 6 and 7. If $`a`$ leaves have depth 6 and $`b`$ have depth 7, then
```math
a+b=100,\qquad\frac a{64}+\frac b{128}=1.
```
Solving gives $`a=28`$ and $`b=72`$. Thus any assignment of 28 length-6 words and 72 length-7 words obtained from a full tree is optimal; the common mean is $`(28\cdot6+72\cdot7)/100=6.72`$ bits. Construct it by splitting 36 leaves of a depth-6 complete tree. The uniform-probability exchange argument rules out a better tree with depths differing by more than one.
