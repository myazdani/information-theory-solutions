# Exercise 5.8: State-dependent compression of a Markov source

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 144. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Use codes indexed by the known previous state. One choice is:

| Previous state | Next S1 | Next S2 | Next S3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | 0 | 10 | 11 |
| S2 | 10 | 0 | 11 |
| S3 | impossible | 0 | 1 |

For S3 the impossible transition needs no transmitted codeword; the decoder only considers transitions in the process's support. Each row is instantaneous on that support.

## (a)
The encoder and decoder maintain the present state; the initial state can be supplied separately at a finite startup cost.

## (b)
They select the corresponding row of the table.

## (c)
The encoder sends the entry for the next state. Prefix decoding identifies that next state, and both sides update their state.

## (d)
Conditional mean lengths are $`L_1=L_2=3/2`$ and $`L_3=1`$. Each equals the entropy of the corresponding transition row, because its nonzero probabilities are dyadic. Solving $`\pi P=\pi`$ gives $`\pi=(2/9,4/9,1/3)`$. Thus the stationary mean is
```math
L=\frac29\frac32+\frac49\frac32+\frac13=\frac43\ \text{bits per source symbol}.
```
This equals the Markov entropy rate $`\sum_i\pi_iH(P_{i,\cdot})`$, attaining maximal compression up to startup cost. Irreducibility makes the same long-run mean independent of the initial state.
