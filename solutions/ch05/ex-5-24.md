# Exercise 5.24: The optimal code for a uniform alphabet

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 148. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Let $`k=\lfloor\log_2m\rfloor`$, initially with $`m\ge2`$.

## (a)
An optimal full binary tree has leaf depths differing by at most one. To see this, if a shallow leaf has depth $`a`$ and a deepest sibling pair has depth $`b\ge a+2`$, replace the shallow leaf by two children and the deepest pair by its parent. The number of leaves stays fixed, while the sum of depths changes by $`2(a+1)+(b-1)-a-2b=a-b+1<0`$, contradicting optimality for equal weights.

Thus depths are $`k`$ and $`k+1`$. If their counts are $`a,b`$, solve $`a+b=m`$ and $`a2^{-k}+b2^{-(k+1)}=1`$ to obtain
```math
a=2^{k+1}-m,\qquad b=2m-2^{k+1},
\qquad L_m=k+2-\frac{2^{k+1}}m.
```
Construct it by starting with $`2^k`$ leaves at depth $`k`$ and splitting $`m-2^k`$ of them. For $`m=1`$, the empty codeword has mean zero.

## (b)
Equality with entropy occurs precisely when all codeword probabilities $`1/m`$ are reciprocal powers of 2, that is, $`m`$ is a power of 2 (including 1).

## (c)
Write $`x=m/2^k\in[1,2]`$. The redundancy is
```math
r(x)=2-\frac2x-\log_2x,\qquad r'(x)=\frac2{x^2}-\frac1{x\ln2}.
```
The unique continuous maximizer is $`x_*=2\ln2`$. For integer $`m`$ in the specified interval, evaluate the two admissible integers nearest $`2^{k+1}\ln2`$ and take whichever gives larger redundancy (both if tied). This precisely characterizes the discrete maximizers. As $`k\to\infty`$, the grid in $`x`$ becomes dense, and the worst redundancy tends to
```math
2-\frac1{\ln2}-\log_2(2\ln2)\approx0.0860713321\ \text{bits}.
```
