# Exercise 5.30: The excess length of a mismatched dyadic code

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 151. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

The probability vectors are $`p=(1/2,1/4,1/8,1/16,1/16)`$ and $`q=(1/2,1/8,1/8,1/8,1/8)`$.

## (a)
Direct substitution gives
```math
H(p)=\frac{15}{8},\qquad H(q)=2,
\qquad D(p\Vert q)=D(q\Vert p)=\frac18\ \text{bit}.
```
For example in $`D(p\Vert q)`$, the second coordinate contributes $`1/4`$ and the last two each contribute $`-1/16`$.

## (b)
The given code $`C_1`$ has lengths $`(1,2,3,4,4)=-\log_2p`$ and $`C_2`$ has lengths $`(1,3,3,3,3)=-\log_2q`$. Both are prefix-free. Their matched mean lengths equal $`H(p)=15/8`$ and $`H(q)=2`$, respectively, so the entropy bound proves optimality.

## (c)
Under $`p`$, code $`C_2`$ has mean $`(1/2)\cdot1+(1/2)\cdot3=2`$. Its excess is $`2-15/8=1/8=D(p\Vert q)`$.

## (d)
Under $`q`$, code $`C_1`$ has mean $`1/2+(2+3+4+4)/8=17/8`$. Its excess above $`H(q)`$ is $`1/8=D(q\Vert p)`$. The equality of the two losses is particular to these laws; relative entropy is not generally symmetric.
