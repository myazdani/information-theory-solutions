# Exercise 5.28: Constructing a Shannon prefix code

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 150. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Interpret taking the first $`\ell_i`$ binary digits as truncation, not rounding to the nearest dyadic number, and use the terminating expansion for dyadic endpoints. Let $`\ell_i=\lceil-\log_2p_i\rceil`$ and let the codeword be the first $`\ell_i`$ digits of $`F_i`$.

## (a)
Since $`p_i`$ are nonincreasing, $`\ell_i`$ are nondecreasing. A binary word of length $`\ell_i`$ specifies a half-open dyadic interval of width $`2^{-\ell_i}\le p_i`$ containing $`F_i`$. For $`j>i`$,
```math
F_j-F_i=\sum_{k=i}^{j-1}p_k\ge p_i\ge2^{-\ell_i}.
```
Thus $`F_j`$ is outside the dyadic interval for word $`i`$. Since $`\ell_j\ge\ell_i`$, the word for $`i`$ cannot prefix the word for $`j`$. This proves prefix-freeness. Also
```math
-\log_2p_i\le\ell_i\lt  -\log_2p_i+1,
\qquad H(X)\le\sum_ip_i\ell_i\lt H(X)+1.
```
Positive probabilities are assumed; null symbols may be omitted. A singleton source may use the empty word.

## (b)
The cumulative starting points are $`0,1/2,3/4,7/8`$ and the lengths are $`1,2,3,3`$. The code is $`(0,10,110,111)`$, with mean $`7/4`$ bits, exactly equal to the dyadic source entropy.
