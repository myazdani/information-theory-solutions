# Exercise 5.4: Binary and ternary Huffman constructions

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 143. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Number the symbols in the order given. Ties may be resolved in different ways; the following are concrete optimal codes.

## (a)
A binary code is

| Symbol | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Code | 0 | 11 | 100 | 10110 | 10111 | 10101 | 10100 |

Using probabilities scaled by 100, the successive merges are $`2+3=5`$, $`4+4=8`$, $`5+8=13`$, $`12+13=25`$, $`25+26=51`$, and $`49+51=100`$. Each combines the two smallest current masses, certifying Huffman optimality.

## (b)
The length vector is $`(1,2,3,5,5,5,5)`$, giving
```math
L_2=0.49+2(0.26)+3(0.12)+5(0.04+0.04+0.03+0.02)=2.02.
```

## (c)
A ternary code is

| Symbol | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Code | 2 | 1 | 02 | 012 | 00 | 011 | 010 |

The three-way merges on scaled masses are $`2+3+4=9`$, $`4+9+12=25`$, and $`25+26+49=100`$. There is no dummy symbol because seven leaves already fit a full ternary tree. The mean is 1.34 ternary digits.
