# Exercise 5.37: Classifying four codebooks

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 155. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a) Unique decodability
- $`C_1`$: no. The output 00 is either its word 00 or two copies of its word 0.
- $`C_2`$: yes, because it is prefix-free.
- $`C_3`$: yes. Its words are $`1^k0`$ for $`k\ge0`$; each zero ends one codeword unambiguously.
- $`C_4`$: no. Again 00 is either one word or two copies of 0.

## (b) Instantaneous decoding
Exactly $`C_2`$ and $`C_3`$ are instantaneous. In $`C_2`$, the words starting 0 have length 2, and those starting 1 are 100,101,11; none prefixes another. In $`C_3`$, two different words differ where the shorter one first ends with 0 and the longer still has 1. In $`C_1`$ and $`C_4`$, 0 is a proper prefix of another codeword.
