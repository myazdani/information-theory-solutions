# Exercise 5.12: Two different optimal length assignments

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 145. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Scale the masses by 12 to $`(4,4,3,1)`$. Merging 1 and 3 gives three equal masses 4, so ties permit different Huffman trees. One code is $`(0,10,110,111)`$, with lengths $`(1,2,3,3)`$ and mean 2.

## (b)
After the first merge, merge the two original weight-4 symbols instead; this gives a balanced tree. For example $`(10,11,01,00)`$ has lengths $`(2,2,2,2)`$ and mean 2. Both constructions consist of valid Huffman merges, so both are optimal. Directly,
```math
\frac13\cdot1+\frac13\cdot2+\frac14\cdot3+\frac1{12}\cdot3=2.
```

## (c)
The Shannon lengths are $`(2,2,2,4)`$. The first optimal code assigns length 3 to the symbol of probability $`1/4`$, exceeding its Shannon length 2. Huffman minimizes a weighted total, not each symbol's length separately.
