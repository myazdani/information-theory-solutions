# Exercise 3.5: Probability thresholds and the AEP

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 66. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Every word in $`C_n(t)`$ has probability at least $`2^{-nt}`$. Thus $`1\ge |C_n(t)|2^{-nt}`$, proving $`|C_n(t)|\le2^{nt}`$. If $`t<0`$, the set is empty.

## (b)
For finite entropy the AEP gives
```math
\Pr(X^n\in C_n(t))=\Pr\left(-\frac1n\log_2p(X^n)\le t\right)
\longrightarrow\begin{cases}1,&t>H(X),\\0,&t\lt H(X).\end{cases}
```
At $`t=H(X)`$ the AEP alone gives no conclusion. A uniform distribution on its support makes the probability exactly 1 for every $`n`$. For a nonuniform finite-alphabet source, the surprisal has strictly positive finite variance, so the central limit theorem makes this boundary probability tend to $`1/2`$. For a general countable distribution the boundary needs additional information. Thus $`t>H(X)`$ is the general sufficient range, with the boundary explicitly exceptional.


If $`H(X)=\infty`$, the normalized nonnegative surprisal tends to infinity almost surely by truncation and the strong law. Therefore for every finite $`t`$, $`\Pr(X^n\in C_n(t))\to0`$.
