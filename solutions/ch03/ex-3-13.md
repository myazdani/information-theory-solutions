# Exercise 3.13: Computing typical and smallest probable sets

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 68–69. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

For a word with $`k`$ ones, its probability is $`q_k=0.6^k0.4^{25-k}`$ and its multiplicity is $`\binom{25}{k}`$. Some entries in the printed probability column on p. 69 do not agree with the stated binomial-mass formula (several instead match cumulative probabilities). All values here are independently calculated from the formula. The [recomputed table](../../artifacts/ch03/ex-3-13-binomial.csv) records all 26 weights using these formulas.

## (a)
$`H(X)=h_2(0.6)\approx0.9709505945`$ bits.

## (b)
The per-symbol surprisal is
```math
s_k=-\frac1{25}\log_2q_k=-\log_2(0.4)-\frac{k}{25}\log_2(1.5).
```
Solving $`|s_k-H(X)|\le0.1`$ with integer $`k`$ gives exactly $`11\le k\le19`$. Thus
```math
|A|=\sum_{k=11}^{19}\binom{25}{k}=26\,366\,510,
\qquad\Pr(A)=\sum_{k=11}^{19}\binom{25}{k}q_k\approx0.9362462771.
```

## (c)
Interpret probability 0.9 as at least 0.9: exact equality is impossible because each sequence probability has denominator dividing $`5^{25}`$, whereas $`0.9=9/10`$ has an even denominator in lowest terms. A smallest high-probability set takes the most likely individual words first. Since $`q_{k+1}/q_k=1.5`$, take all words with $`k\ge13`$, then just enough with $`k=12`$. The required number from that boundary class is
```math
m=\left\lceil\frac{0.9-\sum_{k=13}^{25}\binom{25}{k}q_k}{q_{12}}\right\rceil
=3\,680\,673.
```
Therefore the minimum cardinality is $`20\,457\,889`$, with probability $`0.9000000137276422`$. Removing the last word drops below 0.9. A swapping argument proves optimality: replacing a chosen less-likely word by an omitted more-likely word never decreases probability at fixed cardinality.

## (d)
Every choice of the required $`k=12`$ words lies in the typical set. The intersection comprises those boundary words and all words of weights 13 through 19. Hence
```math
|A\cap B|=m+\sum_{k=13}^{19}\binom{25}{k}=20\,389\,483,
\qquad\Pr(A\cap B)=mq_{12}+\sum_{k=13}^{19}\binom{25}{k}q_k
\approx0.8706378089353882.
```
This result is independent of how ties within weight 12 are broken.
