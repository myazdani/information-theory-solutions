# Exercise 3.1: From Markov to the weak law

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 64–65. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
For $`X\ge0`$ and $`t>0`$, pointwise $`X\ge t\mathbf1_{\{X\ge t\}}`$. Taking expectations gives $`\Pr(X\ge t)\le\mathbb EX/t`$. Equality holds for any law supported on $`\{0,t\}`$: if $`\Pr(X=t)=a`$, both sides equal $`a`$.

## (b)
Apply (a) to the nonnegative variable $`(Y-\mu)^2`$ at threshold $`\epsilon^2`$:
```math
\Pr(|Y-\mu|>\epsilon)\le\Pr((Y-\mu)^2\ge\epsilon^2)
\le\frac{\mathbb E(Y-\mu)^2}{\epsilon^2}=\frac{\sigma^2}{\epsilon^2}.
```

## (c)
Independence gives $`\mathbb E\bar Z_n=\mu`$ and $`\operatorname{Var}(\bar Z_n)=\sigma^2/n`$. Apply (b):
```math
\Pr(|\bar Z_n-\mu|>\epsilon)\le\frac{\sigma^2}{n\epsilon^2}\longrightarrow0.
```
The finite-variance assumption is used for this quantitative proof.
