# Exercise 3.11: Lower bounding every probable set

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 67. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
De Morgan's law and the union bound give
```math
\Pr(A\cap B)=1-\Pr(A^c\cup B^c)
\ge1-\Pr(A^c)-\Pr(B^c)>1-\epsilon_1-\epsilon_2.
```
For sufficiently large $`n`$, the AEP supplies $`\Pr(A_\epsilon^{(n)})>1-\epsilon`$. Apply this with the stated probable set to obtain the first inequality (a non-strict version suffices).

## (b)
The next equality is the definition of probability as a sum over atoms. Typicality bounds every atom in the intersection by $`2^{-n(H-\epsilon)}`$. Summing this constant gives the intersection's cardinality times that bound, and containment in $`B_\delta^{(n)}`$ bounds its cardinality by $`|B_\delta^{(n)}|`$. Hence
```math
|B_\delta^{(n)}|\ge(1-\epsilon-\delta)2^{n(H-\epsilon)}.
```

## (c)
Fix the theorem's $`\delta<1/2`$ and any $`\delta'>0`$. Choose $`0<\epsilon<\min(1/2,\delta'/2)`$. Taking logarithms gives
```math
\frac1n\log_2|B_\delta^{(n)}|
\ge H-\epsilon+\frac1n\log_2(1-\epsilon-\delta)>H-\delta'
```
for all sufficiently large $`n`$. This proves the asserted lower bound. Typical sets also supply probable sets with cardinality at most $`2^{n(H+\epsilon)}`$, establishing first-order exponential size $`2^{nH}`$ for a smallest probable set.
