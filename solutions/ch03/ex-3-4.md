# Exercise 3.4: Intersecting two high-probability sets

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 65–66. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Yes. The finite-alphabet AEP implies $`\Pr(X^n\in A_n)\to1`$ for each fixed $`\epsilon>0`$.

## (b)
The weak law also gives $`\Pr(X^n\in B_n)\to1`$. By the union bound,
```math
\Pr(X^n\notin A_n\cap B_n)\le\Pr(X^n\notin A_n)+\Pr(X^n\notin B_n)\to0.
```

## (c)
Each word in $`A_n`$ has probability at least $`2^{-n(H+\epsilon)}`$. Therefore
```math
1\ge\sum_{x^n\in A_n\cap B_n}p(x^n)
\ge |A_n\cap B_n|2^{-n(H+\epsilon)}.
```

## (d)
For sufficiently large $`n`$, (b) makes the intersection probability at least $`1/2`$. Each word in $`A_n`$ has probability at most $`2^{-n(H-\epsilon)}`$, so
```math
\frac12\le |A_n\cap B_n|2^{-n(H-\epsilon)},
\qquad |A_n\cap B_n|\ge\frac12\,2^{n(H-\epsilon)}.
```
