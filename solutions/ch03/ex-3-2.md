# Exercise 3.2: An independence likelihood ratio

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 65. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

The normalized log ratio is the sample average
```math
\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\log_2\frac{p_X(X_i)p_Y(Y_i)}{p_{XY}(X_i,Y_i)}.
```
By the strong law, when the information density is integrable (in particular for finite alphabets), it converges almost surely, and therefore in probability, to
```math
\sum_{x,y}p(x,y)\log_2\frac{p_X(x)p_Y(y)}{p(x,y)}=-I(X;Y).
```
Thus under the true joint law the independence-to-joint likelihood ratio is $`2^{-n(I(X;Y)+o(1))}`$. It has zero exponential rate exactly under independence.


For a countable alphabet with $`I(X;Y)=\infty`$, the same conclusion holds in the extended sense: the limit is $`-\infty`$. Indeed for information density $`J=\log_2(p_{XY}/(p_Xp_Y))`$, its negative part has finite expectation because, writing $`q=p_Xp_Y`$ and $`a=p/q<1`$,
```math
\mathbb E J^- =\sum_{p<q}q\,a\log_2(1/a)\le\frac1{e\ln2}\sum_{p<q}q\le\frac1{e\ln2}.
```
The strong law for $`J^-`$ and for each bounded truncation of $`J^+`$ then forces the sample average of $`J`$ to tend to $`+\infty`$ almost surely.
