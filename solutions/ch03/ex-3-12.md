# Exercise 3.12: Expected divergence of empirical laws decreases

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 68. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Split the $`2n`$ samples into two blocks and denote their empirical laws by $`\widehat p_n`$ and $`\widehat p'_n`$. Then $`\widehat p_{2n}=(\widehat p_n+\widehat p'_n)/2`$. Convexity in the first argument yields
```math
D(\widehat p_{2n}\Vert p)\le\tfrac12D(\widehat p_n\Vert p)+\tfrac12D(\widehat p'_n\Vert p).
```
The two empirical laws have the same distribution, so taking expectations proves the desired inequality. The argument does not require a binary alphabet.

## (b)
Let $`\widehat p_{n-1}^{(-i)}`$ be the empirical law after deleting sample $`i`$. Each sample is counted in exactly $`n-1`$ of these laws, so
```math
\widehat p_n=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\widehat p_{n-1}^{(-i)}.
```
Convexity followed by expectation now gives
```math
\mathbb ED(\widehat p_n\Vert p)
\le\frac1n\sum_i\mathbb ED(\widehat p_{n-1}^{(-i)}\Vert p)
=\mathbb ED(\widehat p_{n-1}\Vert p).
```
The leave-one-out laws need not be independent; identical marginal laws suffice. Nonnegativity makes the expectation argument valid also with infinite values.
