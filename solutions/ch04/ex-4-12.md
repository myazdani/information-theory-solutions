# Exercise 4.12: A persistent random walk

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 93. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Let $`D_i=X_i-X_{i-1}\in\{-1,1\}`$ be direction and let $`B_i=\mathbf1_{\{D_i\ne D_{i-1}\}}`$ for $`i\ge2`$. The initial direction is fair and the reversal indicators are independent Bernoulli($`0.1`$), independent of it.

## (a)
Given $`X_0=0`$, the path and $`(D_1,B_2,\ldots,B_n)`$ determine each other. Therefore
```math
H(X_1,\ldots,X_n)=1+(n-1)h_2(0.1).
```

## (b)
Dividing by $`n`$ gives entropy rate $`h_2(0.1)\approx0.4689955936`$ bits per step, despite nonstationarity of the absolute position.

## (c)
The initial straight-run length $`L`$ is geometric on $`1,2,\ldots`$ with parameter 0.1: $`\Pr(L=\ell)=0.9^{\ell-1}0.1`$. Its mean is 10 steps before the first reversed step. If one counts the step on which the reversed direction first appears, that step's index is $`L+1`$ and has mean 11. Stating the convention resolves the possible off-by-one ambiguity.
