# Exercise 4.27: A noisy binary second-order recursion

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 98. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Under the Bernoulli-process interpretation, $`Z_i`$ are iid Bernoulli($`p`$) innovations independent of the preceding state history. Given $`X_{k-1},X_k`$, the next state is the innovation possibly complemented. Thus for $`k\ge2`$,
```math
H(X_{k+1}\mid X_1^k)=h_2(p).
```
The chain rule gives $`H(X_1^n)=H(X_1,X_2)+(n-2)h_2(p)`$ for $`n\ge2`$, and hence the rate is $`h_2(p)`$. The initial-pair entropy is at most 2 and disappears after normalization.

If “Bernoulli($`p`$)” were intended to specify only each noise marginal, with no independence from the past or across time, this conclusion would not follow; it uses the standard independent-innovation meaning.
