# Exercise 4.7: Binary Markov entropy and Fibonacci growth

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 92. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Write $`h_2`$ for binary entropy.

## (a)
When $`p_{01}+p_{10}>0`$, the stationary law is
```math
\pi_0=\frac{p_{10}}{p_{01}+p_{10}},\qquad
\pi_1=\frac{p_{01}}{p_{01}+p_{10}}.
```
Thus the entropy rate is
```math
h=\frac{p_{10}h_2(p_{01})+p_{01}h_2(p_{10})}{p_{01}+p_{10}}.
```
If both transition probabilities are zero, the chain remains at its initial state and its rate is zero. Absorbing boundary cases of the formula also give zero.

## (b)
A binary process has rate at most 1. Equality is attained, and only attained here, when both rows are uniform: $`p_{01}=p_{10}=1/2`$. Indeed all positive-stationary-mass row entropies must equal 1; a single-state stationary class cannot yield positive entropy.

## (c)
Set $`p_{01}=p`$ and $`p_{10}=1`$. The rate becomes $`h(p)=h_2(p)/(1+p)`$.

## (d)
Differentiating on $`0<p<1`$ gives a derivative with the sign of
```math
(1+p)h_2'(p)-h_2(p)=\log_2\frac{(1-p)^2}{p}.
```
The unique maximum occurs at $`(1-p)^2=p`$, namely $`p_*=(3-\sqrt5)/2\approx0.3819660113`$. With $`\phi=(1+\sqrt5)/2`$, $`p_*=\phi^{-2}`$ and $`1-p_*=\phi^{-1}`$. Substitution gives maximum $`\log_2\phi\approx0.6942419136`$ bits per step. The endpoints have rate zero.

## (e)
For $`0<p<1`$, allowed binary words are exactly those with no consecutive ones. Classify a word by whether it begins with 0 or with 10: for $`t\ge2`$,
```math
N(t)=N(t-1)+N(t-2),\qquad N(0)=1,\quad N(1)=2.
```
Thus $`N(t)=F_{t+2}`$ for Fibonacci numbers $`F_0=0,F_1=1`$, and $`H_0=\lim_t t^{-1}\log_2N(t)=\log_2\phi`$. Since every distribution on the allowed words has entropy at most $`\log_2N(t)`$, this is an upper bound on any source rate using those words. Part (d) attains it. At $`p=0`$ or 1, positive-probability paths form a smaller support; the combinatorial count refers to the full constraint graph.
