# Exercise 4.28: A fixed mixture versus a refreshed mixture

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 98–99. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Assume the two Bernoulli input streams and the mixing choices are mutually independent. Write $`h_j=h_2(p_j)`$ and $`\bar p=(p_1+p_2)/2`$.

## (a)
Yes. Conditional on the fixed selector $`\theta`$, the law is stationary iid, and a fixed mixture of stationary laws remains stationary.

## (b)
It is iid exactly when $`p_1=p_2`$. Otherwise
```math
\operatorname{Cov}(Y_i,Y_j)=\operatorname{Var}(p_\theta)=\frac{(p_1-p_2)^2}{4}>0\quad(i\ne j).
```

## (c)
Since $`H(Y_1^n\mid\theta)=n(h_1+h_2)/2`$ and the selector contributes at most one bit,
```math
n\frac{h_1+h_2}{2}\le H(Y_1^n)\le1+n\frac{h_1+h_2}{2}.
```
The entropy rate is $`h=(h_1+h_2)/2`$.

## (d)
In general, no. Conditional on $`\theta=j`$, the normalized log-probability of the mixture converges almost surely to $`h_j`$. Indeed the mixture likelihood is $`\tfrac12p_j(Y^n)(1+p_{3-j}(Y^n)/p_j(Y^n))`$; if the component laws differ, the likelihood ratio of the wrong component decays exponentially almost surely by the iid relative-entropy law (or becomes zero under support mismatch). If they coincide the conclusion is immediate. Thus the almost-sure limit is the random variable $`h_\theta`$. It equals the mean rate $`h`$ exactly when $`h_1=h_2`$, including equal or complementary Bernoulli parameters. Nonergodicity alone does not rule out a constant information-rate limit when the component entropies agree.

## (e)
Yes. For each $`n`$, choose Shannon prefix lengths $`\ell(y^n)=\lceil-\log_2p(y^n)\rceil`$ on supported words. Kraft's inequality guarantees a code, and
```math
H(Y^n)\le\mathbb E L_n\lt H(Y^n)+1.
```
Thus $`\mathbb EL_n/n\to h`$. This is an expected-length statement and does not require a constant almost-sure information-rate limit.

## (a-prime)
The refreshed process $`Z_i`$ is stationary.

## (b-prime)
It is iid Bernoulli($`\bar p`$), because each output uses independent same-time inputs and a fresh independent selector.

## (c-prime)
Its rate is $`h_2(\bar p)`$.

## (d-prime)
Yes. The ordinary iid AEP gives normalized surprisal tending almost surely to $`h_2(\bar p)`$.

## (e-prime)
Yes. Block Shannon or Huffman codes have expected per-symbol length tending to $`h_2(\bar p)`$.
