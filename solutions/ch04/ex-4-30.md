# Exercise 4.30: Independent increments of a three-state chain

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 99–100. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

The transition law depends only on the modulo-3 increment: increment 0 has probability $`1/2`$, and increments 1 and 2 each have probability $`1/4`$.

## (a)
Yes. The transition matrix is doubly stochastic and the initial distribution is uniform, so every state marginal is uniform and the time-homogeneous chain is stationary.

## (b)
Each transition row has entropy $`H(1/2,1/4,1/4)=3/2`$. Therefore
```math
H(X_1^n)=\log_23+(n-1)\frac32,\qquad h(X)=\frac32.
```

## (c)
The transformation from states to $`Z_1=X_1`$ and the successive increments is bijective: $`X_i=Z_1+\cdots+Z_i\pmod3`$. Hence
```math
H(Z_1^n)=\log_23+(n-1)\frac32.
```
More specifically, its joint law factors as a uniform first coordinate times independent increments with law $`(1/2,1/4,1/4)`$.

## (d)
For every $`n\ge2`$, $`H(Z_n)=3/2`$ and $`H(X_n)=\log_23`$.

## (e)
The factorization gives $`H(Z_n\mid Z_{n-1})=3/2`$ for all $`n\ge2`$, including the pair with the uniform $`Z_1`$.

## (f)
Yes. The entire collection $`Z_1,Z_2,\ldots`$ is independent, although $`Z_1`$ has a different marginal distribution from the later increments.
