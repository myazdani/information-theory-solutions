# Exercise 4.21: Extremal connected four-edge graphs

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 97. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Take a simple connected undirected graph and uniform choice of a neighbor. The rate is
```math
h=\frac18\sum_vd_v\log_2d_v,
```
because there are four edges. A simple connected graph with four edges has either four or five vertices. With five vertices it is a tree; the three possible degree sequences are $`(4,1,1,1,1)`$, $`(3,2,1,1,1)`$, and $`(2,2,2,1,1)`$. With four vertices it is unicyclic: either a four-cycle, with degrees $`(2,2,2,2)`$, or a triangle with one pendant vertex, with degrees $`(3,2,2,1)`$. These exhaust the cases.

## (a)
The maximum is the triangle with a pendant vertex:
```math
h_{\max}=\frac{3\log_23+4}{8}\approx1.0943609378.
```

## (b)
The minimum is the five-vertex path, with
```math
h_{\min}=\frac{3(2\log_22)}8=\frac34.
```
For comparison, the star and four-cycle each have rate 1, and the remaining tree has rate $`(3\log_23+2)/8`$. Allowing loops or parallel edges would change the model and requires stating how such moves are counted.
