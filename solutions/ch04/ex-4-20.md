# Exercise 4.20: Chess-piece random walks

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 97. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Use legal moves to a different square, chosen uniformly among all legal destinations; rooks, bishops, and queens may move any permitted distance. Their move graphs are undirected, so on a connected component the stationary mass is proportional to degree and the entropy rate is $`\sum_vd_v\log_2d_v/\sum_vd_v`$.

- King: four corners have degree 3, four edge middles degree 5, and the center degree 8. The total degree is 40 and
```math
h_K=\frac{12\log_23+20\log_25+8\log_28}{40}.
```
- Rook: every square has degree 4, giving the uniform stationary law and $`h_R=2`$ bits per move.
- Bishop on the five-square color class: the four corners have degree 2 and the center degree 4. The rate is $`(8\log_22+4\log_24)/12=4/3`$.
- Bishop on the other color class: its four edge-middle squares form a degree-2 cycle, giving rate 1.
- Queen: the eight noncentral squares have degree 6 and the center degree 8. Thus
```math
h_Q=\frac{48\log_26+8\log_28}{56}=\frac67\log_26+\frac37.
```
The two bishop classes are closed and disconnected. If an initial stationary mixture chooses between them with probability $`w`$ for the five-square class, its entropy rate is $`w(4/3)+(1-w)`$; the one-time uncertainty about the class does not contribute to the rate.
