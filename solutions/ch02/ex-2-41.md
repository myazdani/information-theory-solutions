# Exercise 2.41: Information from random questions

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 52. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Use independence of $`X,Q`$ and determinism of the answer:
```math
I(X;Q,A)=I(X;Q)+I(X;A\mid Q)
=H(A\mid Q)-H(A\mid X,Q)=H(A\mid Q).
```
Thus a question's average value is the uncertainty of its answer after the question is known.

## (b)
The pair $`(Q_1,Q_2)`$ is independent of $`X`$, and both answers are deterministic once the object and questions are given. Hence
```math
I(X;Q_1,A_1,Q_2,A_2)
=H(A_1,A_2\mid Q_1,Q_2)
\le H(A_1\mid Q_1,Q_2)+H(A_2\mid Q_1,Q_2)
=H(A_1\mid Q_1)+H(A_2\mid Q_2)
=2I(X;Q_1,A_1).
```
The penultimate equality follows because the unused question is independent of $`(X,Q_i)`$. The final equality uses identical question distributions. Redundancy between the two answers accounts for possible strict inequality.
