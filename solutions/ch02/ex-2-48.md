# Exercise 2.48: What a stopped word says about its length

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 54. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
In the first experiment $`\Pr(N=n)=2^{-n}`$ and the word is necessarily $`0^{n-1}1`$. The word and length determine each other, so $`I(N;X^N)=H(N)=2`$ bits.

## (b)
Given $`N`$, there is only one possible stopped word, so $`H(X^N\mid N)=0`$.

## (c)
Consequently $`H(X^N)=H(N)=2`$.

## (d)
In the independent stopping experiment, a finite word still determines its own length. Thus
```math
I(N;X^N)=H(N)=h_2(1/3)=\log_2 3-\frac23\approx0.918296.
```

## (e)
Given length $`n`$, the word is uniform over $`2^n`$ strings. Therefore
```math
H(X^N\mid N)=\mathbb EN=\frac13\,6+\frac23\,12=10\ \text{bits}.
```

## (f)
The chain rule and recoverability of the length give
```math
H(X^N)=H(N)+H(X^N\mid N)=10+\log_2 3-\frac23\approx10.918296.
```
Independence of the stopping choice from the underlying process does not make it independent of the observed variable-length word, since length is visible in that word.
