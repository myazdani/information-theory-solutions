# Exercise 2.32: A three-way estimation problem

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 50–51. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Each column has total mass $`1/3`$. Conditional on that column, the diagonal entry has probability $`1/2`$ and the other two entries have probability $`1/4`$ each.

## (a)
The maximum-a-posteriori estimator is $`\widehat X(a)=1`$, $`\widehat X(b)=2`$, $`\widehat X(c)=3`$. It succeeds with probability $`3(1/6)=1/2`$, so its minimum error probability is $`P_e=1/2`$. No randomized estimator can improve the columnwise maximum posterior probability.

## (b)
Every conditional probability vector is a permutation of $`(1/2,1/4,1/4)`$, so $`H(X\mid Y)=3/2`$ bits. The textbook's version of Fano gives
```math
H(X\mid Y)\le h_2(P_e)+P_e\log_2 3
=1+\tfrac12\log_2 3\approx1.792481.
```
There is slack of about 0.292481 bit. The sharper version with $`\log_2(|\mathcal X|-1)`$ gives $`1+1/2=3/2`$, equality here because, conditioned on an error and the observed column, the two incorrect possibilities are equiprobable.
