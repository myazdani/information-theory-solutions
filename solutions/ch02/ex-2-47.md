# Exercise 2.47: Entropy after moving one card

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 54. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Assume the removed card and its replacement position are independent and uniform among the $`n`$ possibilities. There are $`n^2`$ equally likely operations.

- The unchanged deck has $`n`$ preimages, hence probability $`1/n`$.
- Each of the $`n-1`$ adjacent transpositions has two preimages, since either of its two cards can be the moved card. Each has probability $`2/n^2`$.
- Every other attainable deck has one preimage. There are $`n^2-n-2(n-1)=(n-1)(n-2)`$ such decks.

To justify the final uniqueness assertion, any nontrivial move rotates the interval between its original and final positions by one step. An interval of length at least three reveals both its endpoints and its rotation direction, hence the moved card uniquely; for an interval of length two the two directions agree, producing the stated duplicate.

It follows that
```math
H=\frac1n\log_2n+\frac{2(n-1)}{n^2}\log_2\frac{n^2}{2}
+\frac{(n-1)(n-2)}{n^2}\log_2 n^2
=\left(2-\frac1n\right)\log_2n-\frac{2(n-1)}{n^2}.
```
For $`n=1`$ the result is zero, with empty categories omitted. An exhaustive small-$`n`$ enumeration accompanies the analytic preimage count.
