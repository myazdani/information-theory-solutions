# Exercise 2.26: Relative entropy from a logarithmic bound

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 49. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
The function $`x-1-\ln x`$ has derivative $`1-1/x`$, unique minimum at 1, and minimum value zero. Thus $`\ln x\le x-1`$, with equality only at 1.

## (b)
If $`q(x)=0<p(x)`$ anywhere, then $`D(p\Vert q)=+\infty`$ and the claim is immediate. Otherwise sum only over $`S=\{x:p(x)>0\}`$. The definition in natural-log units and part (a) give
```math
-D(p\Vert q)=\sum_{x\in S}p(x)\ln\frac{q(x)}{p(x)}
\le\sum_{x\in S}(q(x)-p(x))=q(S)-1\le0.
```
Changing the logarithm base multiplies everything by a positive constant.

## (c)
Equality requires $`q(x)/p(x)=1`$ at every positive $`p(x)`$, and requires $`q(S)=1`$. These conditions together are equivalent to $`p=q`$ everywhere.
