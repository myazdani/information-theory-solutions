# Exercise 2.9: An entropy distance

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 45. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Assume the relevant entropies are finite; otherwise this is an extended distance and differences of infinities must be avoided.

## (a)
Nonnegativity and symmetry follow directly from the definition. For the triangle inequality,
```math
H(X\mid Z)\le H(X,Y\mid Z)
=H(Y\mid Z)+H(X\mid Y,Z)
\le H(Y\mid Z)+H(X\mid Y).
```
Interchanging $`X`$ and $`Z`$ and adding proves $`\rho(X,Z)\le\rho(X,Y)+\rho(Y,Z)`$. Finally, distance zero means that each variable is an almost-sure deterministic function of the other. These two functions are inverses on their positive-probability supports. Thus the distance is a metric on equivalence classes under invertible relabeling (a pseudometric on the raw variables).

## (b)
Use $`H(X\mid Y)=H(X)-I(X;Y)`$ and its symmetric version to get
```math
\rho=H(X)+H(Y)-2I(X;Y).
```
Substituting $`I(X;Y)=H(X)+H(Y)-H(X,Y)`$ once or twice gives
```math
\rho=H(X,Y)-I(X;Y)=2H(X,Y)-H(X)-H(Y).
```
