# Exercise 2.28: Balancing two coordinates

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 50. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Write $`f(t)=-t\log_2t`$, continuously extended at zero. It is strictly concave. Replacing masses $`a,b`$ by their average changes only their contribution, and
```math
f(a)+f(b)\le2f((a+b)/2),
```
with strict inequality unless $`a=b`$.

More generally suppose $`a>b`$ and transfer mass $`t`$ from $`a`$ to $`b`$, with $`0\le t\le(a-b)/2`$. The derivative of the new contribution is
```math
\frac d{dt}[f(a-t)+f(b+t)]=\log_2\frac{a-t}{b+t}\ge0.
```
It is positive before equality is reached (interpreted as a right derivative if $`b=0`$). Every nontrivial equalizing transfer strictly increases entropy. If a larger transfer still reduces the absolute difference, the same conclusion follows by symmetry of the two coordinates. Any sequence of such equalizing transfers therefore increases entropy; equivalently, entropy is Schur-concave.
