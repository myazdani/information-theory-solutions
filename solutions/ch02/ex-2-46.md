# Exercise 2.46: Uniqueness from continuity and grouping

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 53–54. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Write $`h(t)=H_2(t,1-t)`$ and define $`f(n)=H_n(1/n,\ldots,1/n)`$, with $`f(1)=0`$. Terms having zero total mass are interpreted by continuity.

### 1. Boundary values and repeated grouping
Symmetry gives $`h(t)=h(1-t)`$. Evaluate $`H_3(1,0,0)`$ by first grouping the first two entries and then by first grouping the last two. The results are $`2h(1)`$ and $`h(1)`$, so $`h(0)=h(1)=0`$. Zero coordinates can consequently be inserted or deleted without changing the value.

Repeated use of grouping gives the general block rule: the entropy of a fine distribution equals the entropy of its block masses plus the weighted sum of the normalized within-block entropies. In particular, grouping $`mn`$ equal masses into $`m`$ blocks of size $`n`$ yields
```math
f(mn)=f(m)+f(n),\qquad f(2)=1,
\qquad f(2^r)=r.
```
Continuity of $`h`$ on $`[0,1]`$ gives a bound $`|h(t)|\le M`$. Apply the block rule along a balanced binary tree with $`n`$ leaves and depth at most $`\lceil\log_2n\rceil`$. At each depth the node masses sum to at most 1, so for every $`n`$-point distribution,
```math
|H_n(p)|\le M\lceil\log_2n\rceil.
```
This conclusion does not presume nonnegativity of the unknown functional.

### 2. The uniform values are logarithms
Fix $`n\ge2`$. There are integers $`k_j\to\infty`$ and $`r_j=\lfloor k_j\log_2n\rfloor`$ such that
```math
\theta_j=\frac{2^{r_j}}{n^{k_j}}\longrightarrow1.
```
Here is an elementary justification. If $`\log_2n`$ is rational, take denominator multiples. Otherwise the pigeonhole principle gives multiples whose distance to an integer is arbitrarily small. If their fractional part is already near zero, use them. If it is $`1-\delta`$, multiply that multiple by $`\lfloor1/\delta\rfloor`$; the new fractional part is between zero and $`\delta`$. Irrationality ensures these increasingly good multiples are unbounded.

Set $`N_j=n^{k_j}`$ and $`B_j=2^{r_j}`$. If $`N_j=B_j`$, multiplicativity directly gives $`k_jf(n)=r_j`$. Otherwise group the uniform $`N_j`$-point distribution into blocks of sizes $`B_j`$ and $`N_j-B_j`$:
```math
k_jf(n)=h(\theta_j)+\theta_jr_j+(1-\theta_j)f(N_j-B_j).
```
Divide by $`k_j`$. The first term vanishes since $`h`$ is bounded. The second tends to $`\log_2n`$. The absolute value of the last term is at most
```math
(1-\theta_j)M\frac{\log_2N_j+1}{k_j}
=(1-\theta_j)M\left(\log_2n+\frac1{k_j}\right)\longrightarrow0.
```
Therefore $`f(n)=\log_2n`$.

### 3. Rational and then arbitrary probabilities
For rational $`p_i=a_i/N>0`$, partition a uniform $`N`$-point set into blocks of integer sizes $`a_i`$. The block rule and the formula for $`f`$ give
```math
\log_2N=H_m(p)+\sum_i p_i\log_2a_i,
\qquad H_m(p)=-\sum_i p_i\log_2p_i.
```
Zero rational masses may be omitted. Finally the grouping rule implies continuity of every $`H_m`$ inductively from continuity of $`h`$: whenever a merged mass tends to zero, its multiplied binary term tends to zero because $`h`$ is bounded. Approximate any real probability vector by rational vectors and pass to the limit. This proves the asserted Shannon form everywhere, with normalization fixing the logarithm base to 2.
