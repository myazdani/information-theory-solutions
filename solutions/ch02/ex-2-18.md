# Exercise 2.18: A stopped sporting series

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 47–48. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Each admissible outcome word of length $`j`$ has probability $`2^{-j}`$. The number of such words is $`n_j=2\binom{j-1}{3}`$ for $`j=4,5,6,7`$: choose the winning team and its three earlier wins. Therefore
```math
\Pr(Y=4,5,6,7)=(1/8,1/4,5/16,5/16),
\qquad H(X)=\sum_j n_j2^{-j}j=\frac{93}{16}.
```
The length is a function of the outcome, so $`H(Y\mid X)=0`$. Also
```math
H(Y)=\frac{27}{8}-\frac58\log_2 5\approx1.923795,
\qquad H(X\mid Y)=\frac{39}{16}+\frac58\log_2 5\approx3.888705.
```
The conditional distribution given length $`j`$ is uniform on its $`n_j`$ valid words, so equivalently $`H(X\mid Y)=\sum_j\Pr(Y=j)\log_2n_j`$. This also verifies the chain-rule total $`93/16=5.8125`$ bits.
