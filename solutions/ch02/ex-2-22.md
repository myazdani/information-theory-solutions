# Exercise 2.22: Logical implications among information identities

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 48. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
The relative-entropy chain rule is the most general identity in this list. Applying it with a joint law in the first argument and the product of its marginals in the second gives the mutual-information chain rule:
```math
I(X_1,\ldots,X_n;Y)=\sum_i I(X_i;Y\mid X_1,\ldots,X_{i-1}).
```
Setting $`Y=(X_1,\ldots,X_n)`$ in this formula gives $`I(X^n;X^n)=H(X^n)`$ on the left and $`H(X_i\mid X^{i-1})`$ in each summand, hence the entropy chain rule. Thus an implication ordering is relative-entropy chain rule → mutual-information chain rule → entropy chain rule. These are generality implications; their usual elementary proofs can also start directly from factorization of probabilities.

## (b)
Jensen's inequality, applied to the convex function $`-\log`$ and the ratio $`q(X)/p(X)`$, gives $`D(p\Vert q)\ge0`$ (mass outside the support of $`p`$ only strengthens the bound). Taking $`p=p_{XY}`$ and $`q=p_Xp_Y`$ then gives $`I(X;Y)\ge0`$. Hence an appropriate ordering is Jensen → nonnegativity of relative entropy → nonnegativity of mutual information.
