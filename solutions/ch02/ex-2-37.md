# Exercise 2.37: Divergence from the product of marginals

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 51. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Expanding the logarithm and summing out unused coordinates gives
```math
D(p_{XYZ}\Vert p_Xp_Yp_Z)=H(X)+H(Y)+H(Z)-H(X,Y,Z).
```
This formula is valid when the displayed entropy difference is defined, in particular for finite alphabets. An always-nonnegative chain-rule expression is
```math
D(p_{XYZ}\Vert p_Xp_Yp_Z)=I(X;Y)+I((X,Y);Z).
```
The divergence is zero precisely when the joint distribution equals the product of all three marginals, that is, mutual independence. Pairwise independence alone is insufficient, as the even-parity example shows.
