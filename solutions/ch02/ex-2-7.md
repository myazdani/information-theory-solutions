# Exercise 2.7: Identifying a counterfeit coin

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 44–45. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
There are $`2n+1`$ hypotheses: all genuine, or one specified coin heavy or light. A depth-$`k`$ balance decision tree has at most $`3^k`$ leaves, giving $`2n+1\le3^k`$.

A sharper bound accounts for the first weighing. A useful first weighing puts $`a`$ coins against $`a`$ coins. Each imbalance leaves $`2a`$ possibilities and the balanced outcome leaves $`2(n-2a)+1`$. Each branch has at most $`3^{k-1}`$ leaves. Since $`2a`$ is even,
```math
2a\le3^{k-1}-1,\qquad
2(n-2a)+1\le3^{k-1},\qquad
n\le\frac{3^k-3}{2}.
```
This is the usual balance model, with no preexisting genuine reference coin and comparisons of equal-size groups. Weighing unequal groups supplies no robust distinction for an arbitrarily small counterfeit weight difference.

## (b)
Label the coins 1 through 12. A left-heavy result below means the left pan is heavier.

1. Weigh 1,2,3,4 against 5,6,7,8.
2. If balanced, weigh 9,10,11 against 1,2,3. If balanced again, weigh 12 against 1: the three outcomes identify 12 heavy, 12 light, or all genuine. If the second weighing tilts, the counterfeit is among 9,10,11 and its heavy/light direction is known; compare 9 against 10 to identify it.
3. If the first weighing is left-heavy, weigh 1,2,5 against 3,6,9. Coin 9 is now known genuine.
   - Left-heavy leaves 1 heavy, 2 heavy, or 6 light. Compare 1 against 2; left-heavy, right-heavy, balanced select these cases respectively.
   - Right-heavy leaves 3 heavy or 5 light. Compare 3 against 9; left-heavy selects 3 heavy, balanced selects 5 light.
   - Balanced leaves 4 heavy, 7 light, or 8 light. Compare 7 against 8; balanced, right-heavy, left-heavy select these cases respectively.
4. If the first weighing is right-heavy, use the preceding branch after exchanging labels 1↔5, 2↔6, 3↔7, 4↔8. This reduces it to the same left-heavy case.
