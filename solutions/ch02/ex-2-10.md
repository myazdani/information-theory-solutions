# Exercise 2.10: A mixture with disjoint supports

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 45. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

## (a)
Let $`B`$ indicate the selected component. Its entropy is $`h_2(\alpha)`$. Because the supports are disjoint, $`B`$ is determined by $`X`$. The chain rule therefore yields
```math
H(X)=H(B)+H(X\mid B)
=h_2(\alpha)+\alpha H(X_1)+(1-\alpha)H(X_2).
```

## (b)
Put $`a=2^{H(X_1)}`$, $`b=2^{H(X_2)}`$, and $`\alpha_*=a/(a+b)`$. Algebra rewrites the preceding expression as
```math
H(X)=\log_2(a+b)-D\big((\alpha,1-\alpha)\Vert(\alpha_*,1-\alpha_*)\big).
```
Its unique maximum is $`\log_2(a+b)`$, attained at $`\alpha_*`$. Exponentiating gives the requested bound. The mixture's effective alphabet size cannot exceed the sum of its components' effective alphabet sizes, and optimal mixing weights are proportional to those sizes.
