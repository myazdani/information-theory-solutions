# Exercise 2.8: Sampling with and without replacement

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 45. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

Let $`N=r+w+b`$ and observe the ordered sequence of colors $`C_1,\ldots,C_k`$, where without-replacement sampling requires $`k\le N`$. Set $`p=(r/N,w/N,b/N)`$, omitting zero coordinates. Each draw has marginal distribution $`p`$ in either experiment, by symmetry. With replacement the draws are independent, so the joint entropy is $`kH(p)`$. Without replacement,
```math
H(C_1,\ldots,C_k)=\sum_{i=1}^kH(C_i\mid C_1,\ldots,C_{i-1})
\le\sum_{i=1}^kH(C_i)=kH(p).
```
Thus replacement gives at least as much entropy. If at least two colors occur and $`k\ge2`$, the first two colors are dependent (for a color of count $`0<c<N`$, the conditional chance of repeating it is $`(c-1)/(N-1)\ne c/N`$), so the inequality is strict. With one color both entropies are zero. If individual labeled balls rather than colors are observed, the same proof applies and the two entropies are $`k\log_2N`$ and $`\log_2(N!/(N-k)!)`$. The observation must be specified: the claim concerns ordered draws, not an arbitrary further summary.
