# Exercise 2.30: Maximum entropy with a fixed mean

Source: Cover and Thomas, *Elements of Information Theory*, 2nd ed. (2006), printed pp. 50. Logarithms are base 2 unless explicitly written as ln.

## Solution

The maximizing law is geometric on the nonnegative integers:
```math
q_n=\frac1{A+1}\left(\frac A{A+1}\right)^n,\qquad n\ge0.
```
It sums to 1 and has mean $`A`$. For any feasible law $`p`$, its cross-entropy against this law depends only on the mean:
```math
-\sum_np_n\log_2q_n
=\log_2(A+1)+A\log_2\frac{A+1}{A}
=(A+1)\log_2(A+1)-A\log_2A.
```
The cross-entropy is finite; Gibbs' inequality therefore bounds $`H(p)`$ by this value, with equality exactly at $`p=q`$. One rigorous way to avoid initially assuming finite entropy is to apply Gibbs to finite partitions consisting of the first $`N`$ atoms and one tail atom, then let $`N\to\infty`$. Their entropies increase to $`H(p)`$ and their cross-entropies are bounded by the full cross-entropy. Thus the displayed expression is the finite maximum.
